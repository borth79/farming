# Building a buyer network for non-commodity crops and products

## Summary
Commodity grain sells itself at a posted bid. Everything else on this farm's list,
whether specialty flour, popped sorghum, cold-pressed oil, birdseed, produce, popcorn,
heritage wheat, food-grade sorghum or prairie seed, sells only to a buyer the farm has
found, convinced and kept. The network is therefore the asset, built once and used by
every vertical. This file defines the buyer segments, how to reach and keep each one, the
Kansas programs that shorten the path, the records to keep, and a twelve-month plan to
go from zero to a working list. The market-analyst agent owns it; buyers live in
`data/processed/buyers.csv`.

## Principle
Grow nothing non-commodity without a named buyer or a tested channel. The sequence is:
identify buyers, sample, secure a letter of intent or trial order, then plant.

## Kansas programs and shortcuts
- **From the Land of Kansas** (Kansas Department of Agriculture trademark program):
  membership opens each January; staff connect members to restaurants, retailers and
  distributors; retailers seeking Kansas products are routed to members; the program is
  the central registration for Kansas farmers markets. Join first.
- **Meet the Buyers** (KDA with Kansas Farm Bureau, Missouri Department of Agriculture and
  K-State Research and Extension): one-on-one producer–buyer meetings with restaurants,
  retailers, grocers and distributors; the 2026 event was 11 March at K-State Olathe.
  Attend every year with samples and a price sheet.
- **K-State Research and Extension** local foods and horticulture staff; Kansas Rural
  Center; Kansas farm-to-school program at KDA; food hub and online farmers market
  platforms operating in Kansas (verify which serve Wichita).
- **Wichita**: Kansas Grown Farmers Market, Old Town Farm & Art Market, chef community,
  independent grocers identified in the competitor analyses.

## What every buyer needs from the farm (prepare once)
- One-page product spec per product: what it is, variety or hybrid, growing practices,
  lab data (protein, falling number, oil profile, moisture, gluten test), pack sizes,
  shelf life, storage, price list, minimums, delivery days, contact.
- Samples in retail-ready packaging with a label that meets state rules.
- Food-safety documentation: license numbers, FSMA status, GAP audit if any, product
  liability certificate, recall plan.
- Story sheet: Sedgwick County, family trust farm, soil and water practices, photos.
- Ordering path: phone, text, email and an online order form; a fixed weekly delivery day.
- Consistency plan: what happens in a short crop year (allocation rules, substitute
  supply, honest notice).

## Twelve-month network plan (year 0 to year 1)
| Month | Action | Output |
|---|---|---|
| Nov–Dec | enroll in From the Land of Kansas; list every target buyer in `buyers.csv` from the competitor analyses and directories; prepare spec sheets | 60–100 named targets |
| Jan–Feb | sample runs (toll-milled flour, toll-pressed oil, popped test lots); lab tests; 20 chef and bakery visits in Wichita | 20 conversations logged |
| Mar | Meet the Buyers event; grocer and distributor meetings; price sheet finalized | 5–10 wholesale leads |
| Apr–May | farmers markets open; launch online store; convert interest to letters of intent before planting decisions | LOIs by product |
| Jun–Aug | deliver first produce and birdseed; weekly delivery route; collect feedback | repeat orders |
| Sep–Oct | harvest specialty grain; fulfill contracts; pumpkin season; sign up CSA for next year | contracts fulfilled, CSA list |
| Nov | review `buyers.csv`: repeat rate, margin per account, time per account; drop the bottom, double the top | year-2 target list |

## Metrics
- Named buyers by segment and status (target, contacted, sampled, trial, repeat, lost).
- Letters of intent and contracted volume before planting, by product.
- Repeat order rate; revenue and gross margin per account; hours per account.
- Share of production pre-sold versus speculative.
- Concentration: no single account above 25% of a product's sales by year 3.

## Decision rules
1. No specialty acre is planted without a letter of intent, contract or tested channel
   covering at least 60% of expected output at a floor price.
2. Every buyer contact is logged the same day in `buyers.csv`.
3. Prefer many small reliable accounts over one large one until volume justifies the
   concentration risk.
4. One fixed delivery day per week; reliability beats price in keeping chefs and bakers.
5. Review the network each November; the bottom quartile of accounts by margin per hour
   is dropped or repriced.
6. Any channel that requires an audit, certification or license is costed into the
   vertical's assumptions before pursuing it.
