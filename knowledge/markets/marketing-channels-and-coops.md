# Grain marketing channels, buyer types and cooperatives

## Summary
A south-central Kansas grain farm sells into a layered market: farmer-owned cooperatives
and independent country elevators (the default outlet, 10-30 miles away), rail shuttle
terminals in and around Wichita that set the regional basis, and end users (soybean
crush, ethanol, flour mills, feed yards) that sometimes pay the best net price but have
narrow specifications and hours. Every bid is futures price plus basis, less discounts;
the right comparison is net price at the farm gate after freight, shrink, drying,
discounts and storage. Contract choice is a separate decision from buyer choice: each
contract type fixes some combination of futures, basis and delivery date and leaves the
rest open. In Kansas, grain delivered under deferred-payment or delayed-price contracts
is an unsecured loan to the elevator and is NOT covered by the elevator's warehouse bond
(K.S.A. 34-2,111), so counterparty risk is a real cost that must be priced into channel
choice. Companion file: `knowledge/markets/local-elevators-and-basis.md` lists the actual
buyers within about 75 miles of Wichita.

## Key facts and numbers

### Buyer types

| Buyer type | What it is | Typical role for this farm | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Farmer-owned cooperative | Member-owned elevator network with agronomy, fuel, feed; markets grain itself or through a marketing LLC (e.g., CoMark Equity Alliance; MKC/CHS "Producer Ag" venture, being wound down since 2025-10) | Default harvest outlet; storage; input account | Patronage refunds, local, bundled services, multiple dump points, flexible contracts | Country basis weaker than terminal; harvest lines; equity tied up for years |
| Independent country elevator | Privately owned local elevator (e.g., Beachner Grain, regional independents) | Alternative dump point; sometimes aggressive on one crop | Can bid aggressively when it needs bushels; fewer rules | No patronage; check license/bond; may lack drying |
| Terminal elevator | Large (multi-million bu) concrete house in Wichita/Hutchinson/Salina (Bartlett, Cargill, Bunge/Viterra ex-Gavilon, ADM, Scoular) | Benchmark for regional basis; direct haul if trucks and distance allow | Highest local cash bid in most weeks; takes big volumes; protein premiums on HRW | Long lines at harvest; strict grades; no agronomy relationship |
| River/rail shuttle loader | Elevator that loads 100-120 car unit trains (BNSF or UP); in KS there are no barge rivers, so "shuttle" is the export-connected tier (Wichita terminals; MKC/CHS Canton, Milan, Sterling; Wellington) | Sets the "shuttle basis" that country elevators price off | Best basis when export program is active (Gulf/Mexico/PNW) | Basis swings with rail freight and export demand |
| Processor: soybean crusher | Cargill Wichita; Bartlett Cherryvale; Bunge Emporia | Soybean outlet, often best basis | Pays for beans at the plant, year-round demand | Hours, moisture/FM limits, long lines at harvest |
| Processor: ethanol plant | Kansas Ethanol (Lyons); Cardinal Colwich (ex-Element); others west | Corn (and sometimes sorghum) outlet | Often bid over country elevators; price-later programs | Milo acceptance varies; moisture caps (Kansas Ethanol: 17% corn, dated page, verify); weekday hours |
| Processor: flour mill | Ardent Mills Wichita; Stafford County Flour Mills (Hudson); Farmer Direct Foods (New Cambria) | HRW wheat, especially high protein/quality | Protein premiums, identity-preserved programs | Narrow specs; may buy via terminal rather than direct |
| Feed mill / feedlot / dairy | Co-op feed mills (Halstead, Two Rivers Ark City), Pratt-area feedyards, western KS dairies | Sorghum and corn outlet; alfalfa for dairies | Local demand for milo; can pay near corn value | Small volumes per buyer, informal bids, credit risk |
| Direct to end user | Seed companies, food-grade, specialty (white wheat, non-GMO, IP sorghum, sunflower) | Niche, contract-grown | Premiums 20-100+ cents/bu (program dependent, verify) | Contracts, segregation, cleaning, audits |
| On-farm storage then sell later | Farm bins, sell by truck to any of the above | Capture post-harvest basis and carry | Avoid harvest basis lows; flexibility; blending | Capital, interest, shrink, quality risk, insurance |

### How cooperatives work (Kansas practice)

| Element | How it works | Notes / sources |
|---|---|---|
| Membership | Open to agricultural producers; buy a common share (often USD 25-100, varies by co-op) | Check each co-op's bylaws |
| Patronage dividends | Net savings allocated to members in proportion to business (bu delivered, USD of inputs). Paid part cash (commonly 20-50%, co-op decides), rest as qualified or non-qualified allocated equity | Qualified allocations are taxable to the member in the year received even if not cash; non-qualified taxed when redeemed |
| Equity retirement | Allocated equity revolves back in cash on a board-set schedule (age-of-patron, e.g., at 65-72, or years-revolving, e.g., 10-20 years) | Long revolving periods mean the retained portion is a near-zero-return asset; treat its PV at a discount |
| Voting | One member, one vote (not by shares); district boards | Boards set storage rates, patronage split |
| Bundled services | Agronomy (fertilizer, seed, chemical, application), fuel/propane, feed, grain | Input purchases also earn patronage; prepay discounts in Dec/Jan |
| Grain marketing LLCs | Many Kansas co-ops market grain through alliances: CoMark Equity Alliance (Enid OK; used by Conway Springs, Two Rivers, Halstead co-ops), Kanza via StoneX brokerage, MKC via Producer Ag with CHS (venture ending per 2025-10-10 announcement; rail terminals stay jointly owned) | Source: ceagrain.com; kanzacoop.com/services/grain; chsinc.com 2025-10-10 |
| Section 199A(g) pass-through (old DPAD) | Co-op may compute a 199A(g) deduction and pass part to patrons on Form 1099-PATR box 6; patron deducts it. Patron must also reduce their own 199A QBI deduction by the lesser of 9% of QBI from co-op sales or 50% of W-2 wages allocable to those sales. Net effect is often small for farms with no W-2 wages | CALT, Iowa State; verify current-year rules with tax preparer. Grain sold to a co-op vs. a terminal can differ in after-tax value by a few cents/bu |

### Contract types and when to use each

Price = futures (reference month) + basis. Each contract fixes different parts.

| Contract | Fixes | Leaves open | When to use | Costs / risks |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Spot cash sale | Futures, basis, delivery today | Nothing | Harvest overflow; cash need; when basis is unusually strong | Usually the weakest basis of the year at harvest |
| Forward cash contract | Futures + basis for future delivery | Nothing | Pre-harvest when both futures and basis are attractive relative to cost of production; lock a profit | Production risk (must deliver or buy out at market); sell no more than insured bushels (common rule 30-50% of APH) |
| Hedge-to-arrive (HTA) | Futures price only | Basis (set later), delivery month fixed | When futures rally but basis is weak (common pre-harvest in KS wheat) | Service fee (commonly USD 0.01-0.05/bu, verify locally); rolling fee if delivery month changes; basis risk |
| Basis contract | Basis only | Futures (set later) | When basis is strong (e.g., post-harvest, export pull) but futures expected to rise | Must price before delivery month; buyer may pay advance |
| Delayed / deferred price (DP, "price later") | Nothing; title passes at delivery | Futures and basis | Dump at harvest without storage, price later | Service charge (examples 2025-26: USD 0.13/bu in-charge then 0.045/bu/month; or 0.0025/bu/day = 0.075/bu/month at Kansas Ethanol); UNSECURED credit to the elevator |
| Deferred payment | Price fixed, payment date deferred (tax-year management) | Nothing | Shift income to next tax year | Unsecured credit; K.S.A. 34-2,111 disclosure required |
| Minimum price contract | Floor price (cash + put option) | Upside | When wanting floor with upside | Option premium (often USD 0.15-0.40/bu depending on volatility, verify) |
| Average price / managed pricing programs | Sells increments over a window (e.g., Jan-Jun) at average | Timing | Discipline for a portion of the crop | Fee; cannot beat the average |
| Grain bank | Deposit grain, draw feed later | Price | Livestock operations | Storage fee after free period (examples: 3-4 cents/bu/month) |
| Warehouse receipt storage then sell | Nothing; grain stays yours | Everything | Capture carry and basis appreciation; keep bond protection | Storage fee (commonly USD 0.03-0.06/bu/month, verify locally), in/out charges, shrink to base moisture |

### How pricing differs between buyers

| Factor | Country co-op elevator | Terminal / shuttle | Processor | Source / note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Futures reference | KC HRW (KE) for wheat; CBOT corn (ZC) for corn and sorghum; CBOT soybeans (ZS); nearby month, rolled before delivery month | Same | Same | Sorghum is priced as basis off corn futures; CME announced (2025-07-21) a sorghum basis futures contract using Kansas rail elevators as delivery points (verify launch status) |
| Basis setting | Terminal bid minus truck freight to terminal minus elevator margin (handling, shrink, interest) | Export/rail-driven; strongest in the region most weeks | Plant-specific; soybean crushers and ethanol plants often above terminal | AgManager "Knowing and Managing Grain Basis" |
| Protein premiums (HRW) | Often none at country elevators, or only above 12-13% | Protein scales common: e.g., ordinary (11.0-11.9%) vs 12%+, in 0.2-point steps at some buyers | Mills pay explicit protein scales | CHS schedule shows 0.2-point steps; 2026 KS crop averaged 12.1% protein, 60.3 lb/bu (KGIS preliminary, 2026-09) |
| Discount schedules | Posted per elevator; typical bases: wheat 13.5% moisture, 58 lb test weight (60 lb at some); corn 14.5-15.0%, 54 lb; sorghum 14.0%, 55-56 lb; soybeans 13.0%, FM 1% | Stricter; may reject | Strictest; moisture caps (e.g., 17% corn at ethanol) | Common discount items: moisture (per 0.5 point), test weight (per lb), damage, FM/dockage, sprout, aflatoxin (corn, often reject above 20 ppb; verify), vomitoxin (reject above 4 ppm on CHS wheat schedule) |
| How often bids change | Posted at open and updated through the day off 10-minute delayed futures (CEA sites); basis changed by merchandiser as needed | Continuous during trading hours | Daily or intraday | ceagrain.com note: "cash bids based on 10-minute delayed futures" |
| Where bids are posted | Co-op websites (farcoop.com, kanzacoop.com, mkcoop.com, mulvanecoop.com), CEA grain bid pages, Barchart/Farmbucks aggregators, text alerts, some co-op apps (MKC has a mobile app; verify others) | Company sites (cargillag.com/check-prices; bartlettco.com), AMS Kansas Daily Grain Bids (report 2886, by region not city) | Plant websites | Aggregator data can lag; call before hauling |

### Storage and handling differences

| Item | Typical structure | Example figures (date, source) |
|---|---|---|
| Commercial storage | Flat rate per bu per month, often with first 30-90 days included at harvest, or a flat harvest charge then monthly | Examples 2025-26 (non-KS co-ops): USD 0.045/bu/month after USD 0.13/bu in-charge; USD 0.03-0.04/bu/month grain bank. Kansas co-op schedules: not retrieved, verify locally |
| In/out (handling) charges | Receiving charge, load-out charge if grain is removed rather than sold to that elevator | Load-out often USD 0.05-0.15/bu (verify) |
| Drying charges | Per point of moisture removed per bu, often tiered | Examples 2025: USD 0.04-0.05/point/bu (non-KS). Many Kansas elevators do not dry sorghum or do so only at some locations; wheat is not dried commercially |
| Shrink | Moisture shrink to base moisture plus handling shrink | Common corn factor 1.4%/point (e.g., 17% to 15% = 2.8% of bushels); handling shrink 0.5-1.0% added at many elevators |
| Blending | Elevator blends high/low quality; farmers cannot usually blend on the pit | On-farm storage lets the farm blend protein/test weight before hauling |
| Condo storage | Farmer buys permanent space in elevator bins | Rare in south-central KS; not confirmed at any local buyer |
| DP storage waivers | Delayed-price grain often gets free storage for a period in exchange for title | Kansas Ethanol: 10-day grace then USD 0.0025/bu/day, price by 2027-02-26 (2026 harvest program) |

### Delivery logistics

| Item | Country elevator | Terminal | Processor |
|---|---|---|---|
| Harvest hours | Typically 7 am to 9-10 pm during wheat harvest, Sunday hours vary; shorter off-season | Often 7 am to 5-7 pm; some 24-hr during peak | Weekday business hours (Kansas Ethanol: Mon-Fri 7:30-4:00, dated page, verify) |
| Wait times | 10-60 min at peak; multiple pits at larger co-op sites | Can exceed 1-2 hr at harvest | Variable; appointment systems at some |
| Scales / pits | Truck scale, probe sample, dump pit speed roughly 5,000-15,000 bu/hr per leg (facility-specific) | Faster legs, more pits | Single pit common |
| Rail | Country sites may load single cars or none; grain is trucked to shuttle | Shuttle loading 100-120 cars; basis reflects train schedule | n/a |

### Payment terms and counterparty risk (Kansas)

| Topic | Fact | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Who regulates | Kansas Department of Agriculture Grain Warehouse program enforces the Kansas Public Warehouse Law; examines state-licensed warehouses at least annually. Any entity storing grain for the public must be state or federally (USDA FSA, U.S. Warehouse Act) licensed | KDA grain warehouse pages |
| Bond | Licensee files a surety bond or irrevocable letter of credit in favor of the state for the benefit of claimants (K.S.A. 34-229). Bond protects grain in STORAGE (warehouse receipts, open storage) | ksrevisor.gov |
| Not covered | Deferred payment and delayed/deferred price contracts are "an unsecured extension of credit" outside the bond; the elevator must disclose this in writing in the contract (K.S.A. 34-2,111) | ksrevisor.gov; RFD-TV Firm to Farm |
| Producer remedy | Seller may void a deferred-payment/deferred-price sale if not paid on demand or paid with a dishonored check, within 15 business days (K.S.A. 34-2,112) | ksrevisor.gov |
| Indemnity fund | Kansas has NO grain indemnity fund (14 other states do); proposals have been discussed but none enacted as of 2026-10-31 (verify annually) | National Agricultural Law Center grain indemnity page |
| Failure history | Kansas has had elevator failures where bond proceeds were distributed (Legislative Post Audit 04-22 cites a 2001 case with USD 750,000 bond paid out); a Kansas bankruptcy court has held stored-grain depositors have priority over the warehouseman's creditors | KLPA 04-22; RFD-TV |
| Payment speed | Spot sales normally paid by check or ACH same day to within a few days; ask for the elevator's policy; take payment at delivery when the counterparty is unfamiliar | Practice |
| Public records | KDA posts licensed warehouse information; USDA FSA posts federally licensed warehouses. Check both before storing or contracting | KDA licenses page; FSA |

## How this affects the plan
- Channel choice changes the net price assumption per crop: terminal/processor basis is typically 10-40 cents/bu better than country basis but costs 15-40 miles of extra freight (roughly USD 0.08-0.20/bu at USD 4-5/loaded mile and 900-1,000 bu loads; verify freight cost in `knowledge/machinery/`). The planner must compute net farm-gate price per crop per channel.
- Storage strategy: with on-farm bins the plan can target post-harvest basis and carry; without them, harvest spot or DP are the defaults, and DP concentrates counterparty risk.
- Pre-harvest marketing: HTA and forward contracts are how the plan locks margins at planting; limits tied to crop insurance coverage keep delivery risk bounded.
- Co-op patronage and 199A pass-through add a small after-tax value to co-op sales (cents per bu) that should be included when channels are otherwise close.
- Sorghum and soybean channel depth matters for acreage decisions: sorghum demand swings with export programs (China), soybean demand with crush margins.

## Current state and outlook (as of 2026-10-31)
- KC HRW December 2026 futures around USD 7.36/bu (2026-10-09 settle, WEAT/rallies.ai report); Kansas terminal ordinary-protein wheat bids roughly 60-110 cents under December in western regions per AMS Kansas Daily Grain Bids summaries (2026-10-26); south-central (Wichita) basis historically runs stronger than western Kansas because freight to the terminal is shorter.
- Kansas 2026 wheat crop small (USDA July 2026: 196 million bu vs 347 million in 2025), 2026 corn large (792.6 million bu), sorghum and soybeans about 164 million bu each (USDA October 2026). Small wheat crop supports post-harvest wheat basis; big corn crop pressures fall corn basis.
- Cooperative consolidation continues (FCEC into Kanza 2024-09-01; Progressive Ag into MKC 2022-05-16; Andale into Kanza 2016; MKC/CHS marketing venture unwinding from 2025-10; Wellington Scoular facilities sold to a four-co-op partnership 2026-05). Fewer, larger co-ops mean fewer independent bids within 30 miles; track entity names annually.
- Indicators to watch: BNSF shuttle basis at Wichita; sorghum export sales (China re-entered January 2026); crush margins at Cargill Wichita (plant restarted after March 2026 dryer fire); Cardinal Colwich operating status; any Kansas indemnity fund legislation.

## Decision rules
1. Never haul to a buyer without a same-day quote in writing (text/email or screenshot of posted bid with time) and the discount schedule on file.
2. Compare at least three bids (nearest co-op, Wichita terminal, relevant processor) on a net farm-gate basis using the worksheet below before each sale of 5,000 bu or more.
3. Terminal or processor direct haul is chosen only if net gain after freight and extra wait is at least USD 0.05/bu AND trucks are not the harvest bottleneck; otherwise dump at the nearest co-op and use its contracts.
4. Pre-harvest sales: cap forward/HTA commitments at 40% of expected production (insured bushels) for wheat and 30% for fall crops until July crop condition is known; use HTA rather than flat forward contract when harvest basis is weaker than the 5-year average for that week (check AgManager basis tool).
5. Post-harvest: hold unpriced grain only in (a) on-farm bins or (b) warehouse-receipted/open storage at a licensed, bonded elevator. Delayed-price and deferred-payment balances with any single counterparty are capped at USD 150,000 and 60 days unless the counterparty is a cooperative or major with audited financials, and even then at 25% of crop value.
6. Verify the KDA (or USDA) warehouse license and bond of any new buyer before first delivery; re-verify annually in August.
7. Sorghum: price against the Wichita shuttle bid, not only the local co-op; when the export program is active, basis differences of 20+ cents/bu have occurred between tiers (verify with the basis tool each season).
8. Soybeans: default comparison is Cargill Wichita crush bid vs nearest co-op; use the co-op when the plant line exceeds 2 hours at harvest.
9. Wheat: keep protein and test weight samples by bin/field; sell high-protein lots (12.5%+) to buyers with posted protein scales; blend low test-weight lots on farm before hauling.
10. Keep patronage equity statements in `data/reference/`; count only the cash portion of patronage in the current-year margin and discount deferred equity at the farm's cost of capital.
