# Local grain buyers and basis, Wichita / Sedgwick County

## Summary
Within about 75 road miles of Wichita the farm has four tiers of buyers: (1) three large
cooperatives that now cover most of Sedgwick, Harvey, Kingman, Sumner and Reno counties
after a decade of mergers: Kanza Cooperative Association (absorbed Andale Farmers Co-op
2016 and Farmers Cooperative Elevator Co. of Garden Plain 2024-09-01), Mid-Kansas
Cooperative/MKC (absorbed Progressive Ag of Wellington 2022-05-16), and the smaller
CoMark-affiliated co-ops (Farmers Cooperative Elevator Co. Halstead; Farmers Cooperative
Grain Association Conway Springs; Two Rivers Coop Arkansas City) plus Mulvane Cooperative
Union; (2) Wichita rail-shuttle terminals (Bartlett Grain; the former Gavilon house now
under Bunge/Viterra, verify; Cargill) that set the regional basis; (3) processors:
Cargill soybean crush (Wichita), Cardinal Colwich ethanol (restarted after the Element
receivership; verify operating status), Kansas Ethanol (Lyons, ~80 mi), Ardent Mills
(Wichita), with Bartlett Cherryvale and Bunge Emporia crush just outside the radius;
(4) feed outlets (co-op feed mills, Pratt-area feedyards). Current cash bids must be
pulled live from the co-op/CEA pages, the Wichita terminal pages and USDA AMS report 2886;
nothing in this file is a live bid. Historical weekly basis by town comes from the K-State
AgManager Interactive Crop Basis Tool and should be refreshed into `data/processed/`
before each marketing decision.

Observations supported by the above and by AgManager basis methodology (treat as
working hypotheses until the tool is pulled):
- Wichita terminal wheat basis is typically 20-40 cents stronger than country points
  25-50 miles out (Wichita -60 vs Newton -85 in the same 2025 table) and stronger than
  western Kansas regions (-60 to -110 in Oct 2026) because freight to the Gulf/mill is
  shorter and mills are local.
- Seasonal pattern, HRW wheat: weakest basis at harvest (mid-June to mid-July) when
  elevators are full; typically improves 20-50 cents into Sep-Nov and again in late
  winter when export and mill demand draws grain; protein scarcity years (low-protein
  crop) widen premiums for 12%+ protein. Verify magnitudes with the tool.
- Seasonal pattern, corn and sorghum: weakest at fall harvest (Oct-Nov); sorghum basis
  depends heavily on the China/Mexico export program at Wichita and Gulf shuttles and
  can move 20-40 cents in weeks; corn basis near Colwich is supported by the ethanol
  plant when operating.
- Seasonal pattern, soybeans: weakest at harvest (Oct); Wichita crush plant tends to
  bid above country elevators; spring basis often strongest.
- Spreads between tiers (terminal vs co-op) narrow when terminals are full at harvest and
  widen in winter when terminals chase bushels for trains.

Where to get current basis (do this before any sale):
1. USDA AMS Kansas Daily Grain Bids, report 2886 (regional terminal bids, daily).
2. USDA AMS Kansas City daily wheat bids and Grain Transportation Report (weekly) for rail freight context.
3. K-State AgManager Interactive Crop Basis Tool (historical weekly by town), Grain Basis Maps (monthly, 3-yr deviation), Seasonal Cash Grain Prices spreadsheet (1973-present).
4. Co-op and terminal bid pages listed above; CEA pages update off 10-minute delayed futures.
5. Save each pull to `data/raw/markets/basis/YYYY-MM-DD/` and summarize into `data/processed/`.

## How this affects the plan
- Default harvest outlets by crop for a Sedgwick County farm (subject to field location): wheat and milo to the nearest Kanza (Andale/Colwich/Garden Plain/Cheney), Halstead FCE (Mt Hope/Bentley) or Mulvane site, with Wichita Bartlett/Bunge as the direct-haul alternative; corn to Cardinal Colwich or Kanza/Halstead; soybeans to Cargill Wichita or Kanza.
- Planner must carry three channel prices per crop (country co-op, Wichita terminal, processor) and the freight cost between them; the spread is a planning input in `models/`.
- Co-op consolidation means contracts with Kanza and MKC concentrate counterparty exposure; the DP/deferred-payment caps in `marketing-channels-and-coops.md` apply per legal entity.
- Sunflower and alfalfa have no elevator channel here: acreage requires a signed delivered-price contract or an identified dairy/feedyard buyer first.
- Wellington Grain Terminal LLC (2026) and the MKC/CHS venture unwind may change the number of independent shuttle bids south of Wichita; re-verify before 2027 harvest.

## Decision rules
1. Before each marketing season (June for wheat, September for fall crops) refresh this file's entity table by checking kanzacoop.com, mkcoop.com, farcoop.com, ceagrain.com, mulvanecoop.com and KDA licensed warehouse records; log changes.
2. Pull AgManager basis for Wichita, Andale/Colwich (or nearest listed town), Halstead, Mulvane, Wellington for all four crops and store 5-year weekly averages in `data/processed/markets/basis_5yr.csv`; the planner uses those, not the point values in this file.
3. Direct haul to a Wichita terminal or Cargill crush only when posted bid minus country bid exceeds freight plus USD 0.05/bu and the harvest truck cycle allows it (see companion file rule 3).
4. Any new buyer (independent, feedyard, sunflower/birdseed, hay broker) requires KDA/USDA license-and-bond check or, if unlicensed (feedyards, brokers), payment at delivery or a letter of credit.
5. Treat all Farmbucks/Barchart aggregator numbers as stale until confirmed by the buyer's own page or phone.
6. Sorghum: obtain the Wichita shuttle bid (Bartlett, Bunge) on the same day as the co-op bid; if the spread exceeds 15 cents/bu, haul or ask the co-op to match.
7. Wheat protein: ship 12.5%+ lots to the buyer with a posted protein scale (terminals/mills); ordinary protein to the nearest co-op.
