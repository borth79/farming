## What changed from the first-pass models
- Wheat: year-5 base acres cut 50 → 40 and sell-through 90 → 75 % (Wichita specialty pool ~300k lb/yr; prior plan took 28 %).
- Sorghum: sell-through cut to 25/35/45/55/60 % (Wichita sorghum-flour pool ~45k lb/yr; shelf ceiling 4–10k lb for a new brand).
- Oil: blend price $4.75 → $4.25/lb; years 3–5 sell-through capped 80–90 % (Wichita premium oil pool ~8–15k gal/yr; regional distribution needed beyond).
- Birdseed: price $0.50 → $0.46/lb; bulk floor $0.195 → $0.21/lb (AMS Jamestown KS bid 2026-10-23).

## Trust constraint (applies to all)
The land is in a family trust. Default structure for every finished-goods vertical: a
separate LLC outside the trust buys raw crop from the trust farm at documented market
prices and processes it in leased or owned space off trust land. Each business plan's
capital should therefore include facility rent or purchase rather than assume a free farm
building. Anything built on trust land needs trustee approval, a lease clause covering
improvements, and a check on agricultural-use and §2032A status. See
`knowledge/operations/family-trust-constraints.md`.

## Common disqualifiers
- Letters of intent below the stated floor volume and price after sampling ~20 accounts.
- Licensed or allergen-free space cannot be built under the stated cap.
- An established competitor already serving Wichita at or below the plan's price.
- Crop quality (protein, mycotoxin, popping yield, oil recovery) fails spec two seasons.

## Local produce channel analysis (2026-11-16)
Competitor analysis scores: grocery 2.6, mainstream restaurants 3.0, chef-driven 3.8.
Lead channel is chef-driven restaurants buying cured storables (specialty potatoes,
alliums, hardneck garlic, sweet potatoes, winter squash, popcorn) in the October–April
window when local supply is thin. Grocery chains need GAP/GFSI audits and $3M liability
cover and pay $0.50–1.00/lb for storables; off the table until 8+ acres. The produce
model's $1.60/lb blend holds only for chef-direct sales; its 9,000 lb/acre is low for
storables (15,000–25,000). First verification: 20 chef visits Jan–Feb 2027 with a winter
price sheet, needing six letters of intent at $60/week or more.

## Local produce note
Three acres of sweet corn, pumpkins, melons, tomatoes and flowers sold at the two Wichita
markets. Gross per acre is 10–50× grain, but labor is 40–50% of revenue. Its strategic
value is the customer list and market table that sells flour, popped sorghum, oil and
birdseed. Stage 0 is attending both markets this winter to count vendors and prices.

## Order of attack
1. Sorghum flour pilot (highest score on economics, lowest breakeven acres) — gated on
   5 LOIs ≥ 8,000 lb/yr at $2.25/lb.
2. Wheat flour stage-0 market test (<$1,000) in parallel; bakery stage is where the value is.
3. Sunflower: restaurant oil test with toll-pressed oil before any press; birdseed only
   as a cheap learning stage.
