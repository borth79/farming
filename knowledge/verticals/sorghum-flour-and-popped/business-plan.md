# Food-grade white sorghum → gluten-free flour and popped sorghum snacks

## 1. Concept
Grow identity-preserved, food-grade white (tannin-free) grain sorghum on 10–20 acres of the
farm's dryland rotation, mill it in a dedicated wheat-free room into whole-grain
gluten-free sorghum flour, and hot-air pop a share of the grain into bagged snacks.
Sell to gluten-free bakeries, health-food stores and independent grocers in the Wichita
metro (~650k people), plus e-commerce, then scale through co-packing and regional
distribution.

Why this farm: sorghum is already in the rotation and suited to south-central Kansas
dryland; the farm is 190 miles from the dominant U.S. food-sorghum processor (Nu Life
Market, Scott City) and close to a metro with no local gluten-free flour brand; the
processing steps (clean, mill, sift, pop, bag) are mechanical and automatable; and the
line shares a sales channel and some equipment learning with the wheat-flour vertical
(but **not** the mill room — see sec. 6). It is listed as candidate #2 in
`knowledge/finance/vertical-integration-options.md`: "second product on the mill business."

Value uplift (sec. 8): food-grade grain is worth ~$0.09/lb; whole-grain GF flour sells
wholesale at $1.95–3.10/lb and popped sorghum retails at $22–40/lb (snack bags).

## 3. Market
**Demand.** Global gluten-free flour market estimates for 2025 range USD 2.9–7.9 B with
7–8.4 % CAGR (four research firms; estimates disagree by a factor of ~2.5, so use the
growth rate, not the size). Sorghum-flour-specific estimates are unusable (USD 0.8–12.8
B) but agree on 5–6 % growth. Within reach: roughly 1 % of the U.S. population has celiac
disease and 6–7 % report gluten avoidance (**verify** with Beyond Celiac / NIH figures);
applied to the Wichita MSA (~650k) that is ~6,500 celiac and ~40–45k gluten-avoiding
consumers, plus the Kansas City (~2.2 M), Oklahoma City (~1.5 M) and Tulsa (~1 M) metros
within a 3-hour delivery radius.

**Target customers.**
- Wholesale (first): gluten-free and allergen-free bakeries in Wichita/KC/OKC; natural
  and health-food stores; independent grocers and co-ops; restaurants with GF menus;
  school and hospital food service (GF items). Pack 25 lb and 50 lb.
- Retail through others: 2 lb flour pouches and 4–5 oz popped bags in Wichita grocers
  (Dillons local-vendor program, independents, farm stores), Kansas-made gift boxes.
- Direct: e-commerce (own store, Amazon later), farmers markets (Old Town, Kansas Grown!),
  farm pickup.

## 4. Raw material and agronomy link
- **Acres by stage (base):** stage 1 10 ac (12 favorable); stage 2 30 ac; stage 3 45 ac.
  Ten acres at 70 bu/ac yields 39,200 lb grain → ~33,000 lb flour + ~3,500 lb popped.
- **Hybrids.** Food-grade = white pericarp, tan plant (no purple/red glume staining),
  no pigmented testa (tannin-free), hard endosperm preferred for milling and popping.
  Confirmed candidates: Warner Seeds P2775 (medium-full, 115–120 d) and W-902-W (full,
  high weathering resistance; maturity may be long for a June planting in Sedgwick
  County — **verify** days to half-bloom); Legend Seeds LGS 5508W (early); Sorghum
  Partners/S&W list food-grade hybrids (2021 guide, current line **verify**); Richardson
  Seeds (Vega TX) food-grade line **not confirmed**. Ask Nu Life Market which hybrids it
  accepts; a buyer-approved hybrid keeps the surplus-grain outlet open. Popping quality
  is hybrid-specific (47–94 % popping yield across cultivars): test-pop 3 hybrids in
  year 0 before choosing.
- **Yield.** Dryland trend 70 bu/ac base, 45 adverse, 90 favorable
  (`knowledge/crops/grain-sorghum.md`); food-grade whites may give up 5–10 % vs. top red
  hybrids (**verify** in K-State SRP south-central entries).
- **Quality specs at intake (self-imposed, aligned to USDA White sorghum class and food
  buyers):** ≥98 % white pericarp kernels, ≤2 % other classes, no pigmented testa;
  moisture ≤13 % for storage (harvest ≤14 % and aerate); test weight ≥56 lb/bu;
  damaged kernels ≤2 %, no sprouted or weathered (gray/black) kernels; foreign material
  <0.5 % after cleaning; aflatoxin <20 ppb (FDA action level), DON <1 ppm (FDA advisory
  for finished wheat products, applied by analogy; **verify** buyer specs), fumonisin
  <2 ppm; no live insects; no treated-seed or chemical contamination. Avoid pre-harvest
  glyphosate on food-grade acres (buyer and consumer sensitivity; **verify** buyer rules).
- **Identity preservation (IP).** Food-grade white acres on fields not adjacent to red
  sorghum (or with border rows discarded); planter, combine, trucks, augers and bins
  cleaned and documented before use; dedicated sealed hopper bin or poly totes; lot
  numbers from field to bag; retain samples per lot for 12 months. One red kernel in
  white flour is visible to customers; mixed lots lose the White class and the premium.
- **Whole-grain vs. dehulled.** Stage 1 is whole-grain only: higher extraction (~96 %
  of clean grain), simpler equipment, stronger "whole grain" claim, and matches the
  Bob's Red Mill benchmark. Decortication loses 10–25 % of mass (up to 40 % for very
  low-fat flour) and needs a dehuller ($5,000–30,000 depending on origin and build);
  consider at stage 2 if bakers ask for a lighter flour. Popped sorghum is always whole
  grain.
- **Storage.** Cool, dry, aerated; whole grain holds far better than flour, so mill to
  order monthly and keep flour inventory ≤6 weeks.
- **Off-spec grain.** Red-contaminated, weathered, or mycotoxin-positive lots go to the
  commodity elevator or feed at commodity price; sound surplus food-grade grain is
  offered to Nu Life Market or other food buyers.

## 5. Process and equipment
**Flow.** Harvest (≤14 % moisture, slow cylinder to avoid cracking) → aerate/dry to ≤13 %
→ IP storage → air-screen clean + destone (+ optional color sort later) → split: 88 % to
mill / 12 % to popper (model allocation) → stone mill (dedicated) → sifter scalps coarse
bran → bag (2/5/25/50 lb), lot code, retain sample → gluten lateral-flow screen in-house,
ELISA + mycotoxin to lab per lot → release → cool storage → delivery.
Popping branch: temper grain to ~13–14 % moisture (**verify** optimum; popping is very
moisture-sensitive) → hot-air pop → screen unpopped kernels (to feed) → cool → season in
tumbler (flavored only) → fill, seal (oxygen absorber), label → case.

non-mill surfaces (mill itself is dry-cleaned).

sifting, bagging and cleanup (one operator, batch mode) → $0.36/lb at $18/h; popping
~10 lb popped per hour including seasoning and bagging → ~$2/lb popped (inside the
net popped price). Stage 2 automation (auto bagger, continuous popper) roughly halves
labor per lb. Owner labor is priced at wage in the model.

**Facility and food-safety design.** Dedicated room, dedicated tools and clothing,
no wheat/barley/rye anywhere in the room or its air path; sealed and pest-proofed;
smooth cleanable surfaces; allergen-control, sanitation, pest-control, glass/brittle
plastic and recall SOPs; lot traceability one step back (field) and one step forward
(customer). Design for a GFCO and KDA inspection from day one.

## 6. Regulatory and food safety
- **Kansas:** KDA Food Safety and Lodging food processing plant license (annual license
  statutory cap $200 for <5,000 sq ft; application fee up to $350; KDA schedule shows
  tiered amounts — **verify** current figures; licenses expire March 31). Plan review
  before build-out. Cottage-food exemptions do not cover wholesale or flour claims.
- **FDA:** Food facility registration (free; renew biennially Oct–Dec of even years —
  2026 window is open now). FSMA Preventive Controls for Human Food: as a very small
  business (<$1 M sales) we are a "qualified facility" with modified requirements
  (attestation and adherence to state law) but we will still write a food-safety plan
  because GFCO and grocers expect one (**verify** qualified-facility thresholds). Allergen
  labeling (FALCPA) applies to flavored popped products with dairy.
- **Gluten-free claim:** FDA rule 21 CFR 101.91 — <20 ppm gluten; voluntary claim, but
  we must be able to prove it. GFCO certification requires ≤10 ppm, annual plant audit,
  ingredient review, a finished-product testing plan and allergen controls; fees set by
  company size and product risk (quote after application; audit travel extra). Model
  carries $3,500/yr (**verify**). Sorghum itself is naturally gluten-free; the risk is
  cross-contact in the field (volunteer wheat in a sorghum field after wheat — control
  it), at harvest (combine used on wheat), in storage and in the mill. This is why the
  mill room and mill are dedicated and the wheat-flour vertical's mill is never shared.
- **Testing per lot:** in-house lateral-flow gluten screen (each milling day), gluten
  ELISA at an accredited lab per lot ($55–200/sample), mycotoxin screen per grain lot
  (aflatoxin, DON, fumonisin; $36–135). Modelled at $0.08/lb flour.
- **Labeling:** statement of identity, net weight, ingredients, allergens, nutrition
  facts (small-business exemption may apply; **verify**), name/address, lot code,
  "gluten-free", GFCO mark once certified, "whole grain" (Whole Grains Council stamp
  optional, fee). "Non-GMO" is truthful for sorghum; certification is optional.
- **Recordkeeping:** field, harvest and cleanout logs; lot traceability; test
  certificates; sanitation logs; complaint and recall records; retained samples.
- **Insurance:** product liability $1–2 M (grocers and distributors require it),
  property, spoilage; est. $2,200–4,500/yr.
- **Weights and measures:** KDA registration of the packing scale.

## 7. Operations plan
- **Staffing.** Stage 1: owner/operator ~8–12 h/week average (milling 2 days/month,
  popping/bagging weekly, deliveries weekly); seasonal help at harvest. Stage 2: one
  part-time (20 h/wk) sales/admin plus production hours; stage 3: one full-time
  production/sales employee.
- **Schedule.** Harvest Sep–Oct; clean grain Nov; mill monthly to order; pop weekly;
  deliver Wichita route one day/week; e-commerce ships twice weekly. Peak demand
  Oct–Dec (holiday baking) — build flour inventory in September from the prior crop.
- **Logistics.** Own pickup/van for Wichita; LTL pallets to KC/OKC distributors at stage
  2; carrier for e-commerce (flat-rate boxes, 2 lb and 4 lb flour, 2- and 4-bag popped
  bundles).
- **Packaging.** Stage 1 digitally printed stand-up pouches (MOQ 500–1,000 at
  $0.35–0.80 each for 2 lb/4 oz) and stock paper sacks with printed labels; move to
  rotogravure at 25k+ units (stage 2). Oxygen absorbers in popped bags.
- **Inventory.** Grain in IP bin (12 months); flour ≤6 weeks; popped ≤8 weeks; FIFO;
  retained samples 12 months.

## 8. Financials
Detail: `assumptions.csv` → `sorghum-flour-and-popped-financial-model.xlsx`
(regenerate with `python3 scripts/build_vertical_model.py knowledge/verticals/sorghum-flour-and-popped`).

**Model mapping (important).** The model has one `product` and one `byproduct` stream.
Flour is the product; popped sorghum is carried as the byproduct: 12 % of raw grain is
allocated to popping, so `conversion_pct` = 88 % × 96 % recovery ≈ 84 (flour lb per 100
lb raw) and `byproduct_pct` = 12 % × 75 % popped-weight yield ≈ 9 (popped lb per 100 lb
raw). Because the model applies `operating_variable` costs and the sales ramp only to
the product stream, `byproduct_price_per_lb` is a **net** price (wholesale ~$11–16/lb
minus ~$4.50/lb popping-line pouches, seasoning, labor and shipping ≈ $6.50 base; $3.00
adverse to cover unsold/discounted volume; $8.50 favorable) and assumes all popped output
sells. This flatters the popped line slightly in the base case (no ramp) and is the
reason the adverse net price is set low. Variable costs on flour are charged on all flour
produced, not only on flour sold, which is conservative in the other direction.

## 10. Risks and mitigations
| Risk | Likelihood / impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Gluten cross-contact (volunteer wheat, shared combine/auger, mill room) → failed test, recall, loss of GFCO | Medium / severe | Dedicated room and mill; no sorghum-after-wheat fields without volunteer control; documented cleanouts; lateral-flow every milling day, ELISA per lot; recall plan and insurance |
| Mycotoxin (aflatoxin in drought/heat years, fumonisin, DON in wet harvests) | Medium / high | Hybrids with good weathering, timely harvest, dry to 13 %, clean and destone, test every grain lot before milling; off-spec to feed |
| Red-kernel contamination / loss of White class | Medium / medium | Field isolation, border rows, cleanouts, visual + sample checks at intake |
| Sales ramp slower than plan (the dominant financial risk) | High / high | LOIs before planting; start at 10 acres; stage gates; surplus grain outlet; do not build stage 2 without 50 % sell-through |
| Price compression from national brands (Bob's at $2.10/lb bulk) | Medium / medium | Sell provenance and service to small accounts; avoid competing on 25 lb bulk price; push 2 lb bags, popped and e-commerce where margins are higher |
| Popped sorghum: hybrid pops poorly, equipment not rated for small kernels, breakage | Medium / medium | Test-pop in stage 0; buy popper only after a trial; adjust moisture; screen unpopped kernels |
| Crop failure (dryland drought, 2023-style 43 bu/ac county yield) | Medium / medium | Carry 12 months grain inventory; buy food-grade grain from Nu Life or another IP grower in a failure year to keep accounts |
| Whole-grain flour rancidity / short shelf life | Medium / medium | Mill to order, cool storage, shelf-life test, date codes |
| Regulatory change (FDA gluten rule, FSMA thresholds, KDA fees) | Low / low–medium | Annual review; GFCO keeps us ahead of FDA |
| Key person (owner does production and sales) | High / high | Written SOPs; cross-train one family member/employee by stage 2; sales person at stage 2 |
| Weather and logistics (ice, flooding on delivery routes) | Low / low | Buffer inventory at accounts |

  cumulative cash below −$90,000; or any confirmed gluten result >20 ppm in released
  product (recall) — stop and reassess controls before any restart.

  regenerated model with actual year-1 and year-2 numbers.
