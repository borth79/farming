# Competitor and market-share analysis: GF sorghum flour and popped sorghum

## Summary
The US gluten-free (GF) flour market is roughly USD 0.5 B at retail (one US-specific
estimate; global estimates run USD 5.6-7.9 B) growing 5-8 %/yr; sorghum flour is a small
slice (US estimate ~USD 0.33 B including ingredient use; growth ~5 %). Popped sorghum is a
niche within a ~USD 1.0 B US ready-to-eat (RTE) popcorn category and has no reliable size
estimate (<USD 20 M is our guess, verify). Reachable demand for retail and bakery sorghum
flour in the Wichita MSA is on the order of 25,000-85,000 lb/yr (base ~45,000 lb) and
~4,000-12,000 lb/yr of popped sorghum (base ~7,000 lb, ~28,000 four-ounce bags); the
3-hour regional radius (KC, OKC, Tulsa, rest of Kansas) is roughly 9x that. The dominant
competitor is Nu Life Market (Scott City, KS, ~215 road miles west), the largest dedicated
US sorghum miller with GFCO/SQF/organic certifications, contract growers and national
ingredient customers; it is weak only at the Wichita shelf and small-bakery service level,
which is the gap this vertical targets. A realistic obtainable share is ~6,000-9,000 lb
flour in year 1, 30,000-45,000 lb in year 3 and 60,000-90,000 lb in year 5 — the
assumptions.csv base volumes of 11,500 / 69,100 / 126,000 lb are 30-60 % above that, and
the popped line assumes 100 % sell-through of 3,500-15,900 lb/yr, which exceeds Wichita-only
demand from year 1. Verdict: 3.0/5 — conditional go on a stage-1 pilot, gated on signed
letters of intent before planting.

## 1. Market definition and size

### 1.1 Definitions
- **Product market A — gluten-free sorghum flour**: whole-grain and pearled sorghum flour
  sold as a single-ingredient GF flour (retail 1.5-5 lb bags; wholesale 25-50 lb sacks) to
  households, GF bakeries, restaurants and food service. Excludes sorghum flour used as an
  ingredient inside packaged goods (that is Nu Life's core business and is not reachable
  by a farm-scale mill).
- **Product market B — popped sorghum snacks**: RTE bagged popped whole-grain sorghum
  (3-5 oz single-serve to 16 oz), competing within the RTE popcorn and "better-for-you"
  puffed-grain snack set.
- **Geography**: Wichita MSA (~650k) for direct sales; 3-hour delivery radius (Kansas City
  ~2.2 M, Oklahoma City ~1.5 M, Tulsa ~1.0 M, rest of Kansas ~1 M) for distributor-fed
  sales; national for e-commerce.

### 1.3 Wichita-reachable demand (derivation; all inputs flagged above)

**Step 1 — GF consumers in the Wichita MSA (650,000 people).**
Celiac 0.75-1.0 % → 4,900-6,500. Diagnosed-or-not gluten avoiders on a GF diet 2-6 % →
13,000-39,000. Total GF-diet consumers: **18,000-45,000** (base 30,000).

**Step 2 — per-capita GF flour purchases.** US GF flour retail USD 547 M (2025) ÷ (335 M ×
~3 % GF-diet consumers ≈ 10 M) ≈ USD 55/consumer/yr. At a blended retail USD 4/lb that is
~14 lb/yr of all GF flours (blends, rice, oat, almond, sorghum). Single-ingredient sorghum
flour is a minority of that basket; we assume **8-12 % of GF flour weight → 1.1-1.7 lb
sorghum flour per GF consumer per year** (verify against scanner data or a retailer's
category report).

**Step 3 — household (retail) demand.** 18,000-45,000 × 1.1-1.7 lb = **20,000-76,000 lb/yr**
(base 30,000 × 1.4 ≈ **42,000 lb**, i.e. ~21,000 two-pound bags). Channel check: Dillons has
~30 Wichita-area stores (verify) and the natural channel ~7-8 doors (Whole Foods 1, Natural
Grocers 2, GreenAcres 2-3, Natural of Course 1, Sprouts status unclear). At the natural-
retail "survival line" of ~1 unit/store/week a new 2 lb SKU in all ~38 doors sells only
~4,000 lb/yr; the bulk of the 42,000 lb is already being bought as Bob's Red Mill, Thrive
Market, Nu Life (online) and in blends. **A new brand's practical retail ceiling in
Wichita is therefore ~4,000-10,000 lb/yr unless it displaces Bob's on shelf.**

**Step 4 — bakery and food-service demand.** Wichita has 3-5 dedicated GF bakeries/cafés
(Cottage Crumbs, XO Desserts, Simple Life Sweets, plus home-based/cottage bakers) and a
larger number of GF-friendly restaurants. A small dedicated GF bakery uses roughly 50-150 lb
of flour/week, of which sorghum is typically 20-30 % of a house blend → 500-2,300 lb
sorghum/yr each (estimate, verify by asking each bakery). 4-5 bakeries ≈ 4,000-9,000 lb;
restaurants, schools and hospitals with GF items add perhaps 2,000-5,000 lb. **Bakery/food
service: 6,000-14,000 lb/yr (base ~9,000 lb).** This is the segment where a local supplier
has a real edge (fresh, small drops, no pallet minimum).

**Step 5 — total Wichita reachable sorghum flour: 25,000-85,000 lb/yr, base ~45,000 lb**
(about 60-200 raw-grain acres' worth of demand is NOT the constraint; shelf access is).

**Step 6 — regional radius.** KC + OKC + Tulsa + rest of Kansas ≈ 5.7 M people ≈ 8.8x
Wichita → **~220,000-750,000 lb/yr, base ~400,000 lb**, reachable only through
distributors (KeHE/UNFI or regional) or direct bakery routes; KC has at least 3 dedicated GF
bakeries (Littlest Bake Shop, Morgana's, Emily Kate's); OKC and Tulsa showed none dedicated
in search (GF-friendly only).

**Popped sorghum.** US RTE popcorn ~USD 1.0 B ÷ 335 M ≈ USD 3/person/yr (~0.6 lb at
USD 5/lb). Popped sorghum is a substitute bought mostly by GF/"better-for-you" shoppers.
Assume 18,000-45,000 GF consumers × 1-3 four-ounce bags/yr plus an equal volume from
general natural-channel snackers → 36,000-270,000 bags... but shelf reality (≈8 natural
doors + Dillons local sets at ~1-3 units/store/week) caps a new brand at **~10,000-50,000
bags/yr (2,500-12,500 lb)**, base **~28,000 bags ≈ 7,000 lb**. Regional ×8.8 → ~60,000 lb.
Treat all popped numbers as very low confidence; no brand-level data exists.

## 4. Differentiation

**What we can credibly claim (and competitors cannot):**
- Single-farm, single-field, identity-preserved Kansas-grown white sorghum — Sedgwick
  County, <30 miles from the Wichita customer. Nu Life is Kansas-grown too but
  multi-farm and 215 miles away with no local face; Bob's is anonymous national supply.
- Traceability to a lot/field and harvest date on every bag; milled to order (weeks, not
  months, from mill to shelf) — whole-grain sorghum flour's bran oil goes rancid, so
  freshness is a real sensory difference versus distribution-aged Bob's.
- Service to small bakeries: 25 lb drops, weekly route, no pallet minimum, custom grind
  (coarse vs fine), the farmer in the door.
- Hot-air popped, oil-free plain SKU (most sorghum brands are oil-popped).
- Kansas From the Land / Kansas Grown! placement and Dillons local-vendor program eligibility
  — distant brands cannot use these.
- Food-safety story: single ingredient, single farm, dedicated wheat-free room, lot testing
  (contrast with the GF Blends 2026 Class I recall caused by a certified-GF purchased
  ingredient — our risk is lower because we buy no ingredients).

**What we cannot claim (until earned):**
- "Certified gluten-free" / GFCO mark until the audit passes (year 1-2); we can say
  "tested gluten-free, <20 ppm, milled in a dedicated wheat-free facility" per 21 CFR 101.91
  if testing supports it. Natural Grocers and Whole Foods buyers will ask for GFCO.
- Price parity with Nu Life or Bob's at 25 lb (USD 1.95-2.10/lb) — our floor is
  USD 1.14-1.51/lb variable and our price needs USD 2.25+. We compete on freshness and
  service, not price.
- "Organic" — not certified (3-year transition; Nu Life, Great River, Arrowhead, Thrive and
  most popped brands are organic). Consider "no post-harvest chemicals" / "non-GMO (all
  sorghum is)" only where factually supportable.
- "Regenerative" — Nu Life already owns this story in Kansas sorghum; only claim with data.
- "Largest/leading" anything.

## 6. Disqualifiers — do NOT pursue if any of these holds
1. A dedicated, physically separate, wheat-free milling room (own entrance, air, tools)
   cannot be built or leased for ≤ USD 32,000 (adverse capex) — contamination risk is
   otherwise uninsurable in practice.
2. GFCO certification + mandatory testing exceeds **10 % of year-2 base revenue**
   (year-2 base ≈ USD 85-95k → cap ≈ USD 9k/yr; current estimate USD 3.5k GFCO +
   ~USD 2.6k testing ≈ 6-7 %). If the GFCO quote comes back > USD 6k/yr, stop and re-run
   the model.
3. Fewer than **5 signed letters of intent totalling ≥ 8,000 lb flour/yr** from Wichita
   bakeries/stores before the 2027 planting decision (plan sec. 3 target), or fewer than
   **3 paying wholesale accounts after 12 months** of sales.
4. Nu Life Market (or another food-grade buyer) offers a **multi-year contract at
   ≥ USD 1.50/bu over commodity on ≥ 40 acres**: that yields ~USD 105/ac of near-riskless
   margin vs the vertical's base year-2 added margin of ~USD 1,180/ac — it does not beat
   the vertical on paper, but if combined with disqualifier 3 (no LOIs) it is the better
   use of the acres. If the offer is ≥ USD 3/bu (unlikely) take it regardless.
5. Test-pop of the chosen hybrid yields < 60 % popped weight or unacceptable hull
   fragments → drop the popped line (flour-only case still viable at > 16,300 lb/yr).
6. Any candidate hybrid cannot be confirmed as tannin-free, white-pericarp (USDA "White
   sorghum" class) with seed available for 2027 by Feb 2027.
7. Dillons/Kroger launches or announces a Kansas-sourced private-label sorghum flour
   (likely via Nu Life) before our launch — retail leg collapses; keep to bakery-only
   model or stop.
8. First-year lateral-flow/ELISA results cannot consistently show < 20 ppm gluten (i.e.,
   farm dust/logistics contamination is not controllable).
