# Sunflower vertical competitor and market-share analysis

## Summary
Black-oil birdseed is a large, mature, price-led commodity category: US wild bird feeding is roughly $3-5 billion and 3-4 billion lb of seed a year, served by a handful of national packers (Global Harvest, Central Garden's Pennington and Kaytee, Red River, Wagner's, Lyric, Wild Delight, Cole's) through big-box stores that retail 40-lb black-oil bags at $0.54-0.72/lb. Wichita-metro black-oil demand is on the order of 1.5-2.5 million lb a year; the farm's 25-acre line (34,500 lb bagged) is ~2% of it and ~6-10% of the independent-store slice, which is obtainable in volume but only at a wholesale price ($0.40-0.50/lb) that leaves 2-9 cents a pound of contribution. The birdseed line is strategically useful (cleaner, brand, store relationships) but is not a profit business unless most of it sells direct at $0.60+/lb.

Cold-pressed high-oleic oil is a thin specialty slice (well under 2% of ~92 million gal US sunflower oil use, verify) inside a commodity oil market where refined HO sunflower sells to restaurants at $14-22/gal and canola at $9-10/gal. Direct specialty competitors are far away (Smude's MN, Colorado Mills CO, Hudson Valley NY, Fresh Press Farms GA) except one: Wright Farms, Bird City KS, already sells Kansas-grown cold-pressed sunflower oil in ~30 Kansas stores, so "the only Kansas sunflower oil" cannot be claimed. Wichita-reachable premium demand is estimated at 8,000-15,000 gal/yr; stage-2 output (~2,800 gal) fits, stage-3 output (~5,000 gal) does not fit Wichita alone and needs KC/OKC/Tulsa distribution. Verdict: oil is worth a staged test (3.0/5), birdseed is a 2.4/5 learning project. The single most important verification is the restaurant price test: get 10 Wichita chefs to buy (not sample) a gallon at $25 or more before any press is bought.

## Key facts and numbers

### A. Birdseed

#### A1. Market definition and size

Market: shelled-in black-oil sunflower seed sold as wild bird food, in 5-50 lb bags and bulk bins, through mass retail, farm/hardware/garden stores, specialty wild-bird stores, grocery, e-commerce, and farm-direct. Blends and other straight seeds (safflower, nyjer, millet, peanuts, suet) are adjacent, not in scope.

Demand math (base; adverse and favorable in parentheses):

- Kansas: 1,204,374 hh x 38% feeding = 457,700 feeding households x 60 lb = 27.5M lb all seed/yr; black-oil at 30% = **8.2M lb/yr** (adverse 25% share x 50 lb = 5.7M lb; favorable 40% x 70 lb = 12.8M lb).
- Wichita metro (Sedgwick, Butler, Harvey, Sumner): 264,000 hh x 38% = 100,300 feeding hh x 60 lb = 6.0M lb all seed; black-oil at 30% = **1.8M lb/yr** (adverse 1.25M; favorable 2.8M). Adding Reno, Cowley, Kingman, McPherson, Harper counties within a 60-mile delivery radius adds roughly 50,000 households and ~0.35M lb black-oil, so "Wichita-reachable" = **~2.1M lb/yr (range 1.5-3.2M)**, worth ~$1.3M/yr at a $0.60/lb blended retail.
- Channel split (working estimate, verify by store interviews): mass/big-box and club 65-75% of lb; farm, hardware and garden chains (TSC, Atwoods, Orscheln/Bomgaars) 10-15%; independents, Wild Birds Unlimited and grocery 10-20%; online and direct <5%. The independent slice the farm can actually sell into is therefore **~250,000-450,000 lb/yr**.

Mismatch flags against birdseed-only/assumptions.csv:
- Volume: plausible. 34,500 lb is ~860 forty-lb bags; 10 independent accounts at 10-40 bags/week over 26 weeks is 2,600-10,400 bags of capacity, so shelf space is not the constraint.
- Price blend is the stretch: the $0.50/lb base assumes 50% of volume (17,000 lb, ~430 bags) sells direct at $0.62/lb. Direct black-oil sales at that scale need a farm stand or market presence every winter weekend plus online pickup orders; comparable online farm sellers top out around $0.56-0.66/lb for 50 lb. A more defensible year-1 blend is 25% direct / 55% wholesale / 20% bulk = **$0.44/lb**, which is below the $0.41 floor plus fixed cost and makes the line cash-negative (consistent with business-plan.md section 8: breakeven ~49 ac or $0.60/lb).
- Wholesale price: store buyers facing Walmart at $0.54 and Costco at $0.62 retail will pay $0.40-0.48/lb for a local 40-lb bag and retail at $25-28; the $0.45 assumption is reasonable, the $0.50 blended average is optimistic.
- Bulk floor moved: AMS Jamestown KS bid was $21.50/cwt on 2026-10-23 versus the $19.50 in the file; update grain_price_per_lb to ~$0.21 (adverse $0.145, the July 2026 print).

#### A4. Differentiation (birdseed)

Can claim: Kansas grown, single farm, harvest-fresh (new-crop seed in October, not last year's warehouse stock), cleaned to <=2% foreign material and <=10% moisture with a posted spec, no dyes or fillers, farm name and field on the bag, local delivery in the farm's pickup, 40-lb bag at a price between Walmart and WBU.
Cannot claim: organic (not certified; organic Peredovik sells at $53.60/50 lb but requires certification and separate fields), price leadership against Walmart or Costco, national brand trust, "no sprout" or heat-treated, higher oil than other black-oil (all black-oil is ~38-42% oil), that birds prefer it (no evidence), "only Kansas black-oil" (CMC and store own-bags exist; verify origins).

#### A6. Disqualifiers (birdseed): do not pursue, or stop, if

1. Independent stores will not pay at least **$0.42/lb** ($16.80 per 40-lb bag) for a written seasonal order, or fewer than **3 accounts** sign by 1 October of the launch year.
2. Direct sales (farm stand, markets, online pickup) cannot clear **200 bags** in the first winter at **$22 or more per 40 lb** ($0.55/lb).
3. A regional packer or elevator (Jamestown KS, Akron CO, Lubbock TX) offers a bulk bid within **$0.15/lb** of the achievable bagged net (bagged net = price minus $0.20/lb variable cost); at that gap the bagging line does not pay for its capital and labour.
4. Stage-1 capex cannot be held under **$20,000** using used equipment, or the cleaner cannot reach <=2% foreign material.
5. KDA classifies the product so that labelling or licensing costs exceed **$1,000/yr**.
6. After two seasons the realised blend is below **$0.48/lb** and acres cannot be grown to 45; then revert to bulk sales and keep the cleaner for the oil line only.

### B. Cold-pressed high-oleic oil

#### B1. Market definition and size

Market: edible sunflower oil in the US, with the relevant slice being unrefined (cold-pressed or expeller-pressed, filtered, not deodorised) high-oleic oil sold as a premium cooking and finishing oil in 250 ml to 1 gal retail packs and 1 gal to 35-lb foodservice jugs. Refined HO sunflower oil (Cargill Clear Valley, ADM, Stratas, Ventura, importers) is the price reference for restaurants; avocado, olive, canola and beef tallow are the substitutes.

Demand math (Wichita-reachable premium sunflower oil, gallons per year):

- Total frying/cooking oil used by ~1,250 metro restaurants: fast food ~30-35 gal/week, full-service 5-20 gal/week; assume average 12 gal/week x 50 weeks = **~750,000 gal/yr**, almost all commodity canola/soy/palm at $9-12/gal. This is the market the farm cannot serve on price.
- Premium-addressable restaurants: chef-driven independents, farm-to-table, upscale (the Yelp "organic" list, downtown and Bradley Fair concepts, country clubs, caterers): working count **80-120 establishments**. Use of a $25+/gal oil is for saute, dressings, finishing and a few premium fryers: 1-3 gal/week each, 50 weeks = 5,000-15,000 gal/yr if all adopted; at a realistic 25-35% adoption = **~2,500-5,000 gal/yr**. Add 3-6 premium fryer users (burger/fry concepts leaning into "seed-oil-free" or "single-origin" stories) at 15-20 gal/week = 2,500-5,000 gal/yr but only at $18-22/gal.
- Retail: 264,000 metro households; working 5% buy a premium (non-commodity) cooking oil quarterly = 13,200 hh x 4 x 0.13 gal (500 ml) = ~7,000 gal/yr of premium oils across avocado, specialty olive, nut and seed oils; sunflower's slice at 10-15% = **700-1,000 gal/yr**, more if the farm converts avocado-oil buyers.
- Wichita-reachable premium HO sunflower demand: **~8,000-15,000 gal/yr at $18-25+/gal**, of which the $25+/gal bottle-and-finishing slice is ~3,500-6,000 gal. Kansas City, Oklahoma City and Tulsa metros together are roughly 6x Wichita, so stage-3 regional reach lifts the pool to ~50,000-90,000 gal/yr but adds a distributor margin (25-35%) and competition from Smude's (already Midwest grocery) and Colorado Mills.

#### B4. Differentiation (oil)

Can claim: grown and pressed on one named Sedgwick County farm (field-to-bottle traceability); Kansas grown; fresh-pressed in small batches with press dates on the label; unrefined (no hexane, bleaching or deodorising); high-oleic (post lab: ~80%+ oleic, verify each lot) with the oxidative stability and ~450 F smoke point that follows; neutral flavour; non-GMO (all sunflower is); local delivery and chef relationship; a credible answer to the seed-oil backlash ("the seed oil that is not industrial").
Cannot claim: certified organic; price parity with refined bulk HO oil ($14-22/gal) or canola ($9-12/gal); national brand trust or shelf history (Smude's, La Tourangelle); "only Kansas sunflower oil" (Wright Farms); heart-health or AHA certification without the programme; "extra virgin" is unregulated for sunflower and invites challenge; superior nutrition versus other HO sunflower oils (vitamin E retention is plausible but needs a lab result); "no risk of rancidity" (unrefined oil keeps 12-24 months only if filtered, kept cool and dark).

#### B6. Disqualifiers (oil): do not pursue, or stop, if

1. Fewer than **10 Wichita restaurants** place a repeat (second) order at **$25/gal or more** (or $22/gal in 35-lb JIB) within 90 days of the sales test using toll-pressed or purchased HO oil.
2. Fewer than **3 specialty grocers** (Whole Foods Wichita, GreenAcres, Natural Grocers, Viva La Olive, farmers market) commit to shelf space at **$5.50 wholesale per 500 ml**.
3. The food-grade room plus KDA licensing cannot be built for **under $35,000**, or KDA will not license the proposed space.
4. Lab results on the farm's seed and press show oleic below **75%** or peroxide value above 10 meq/kg at 6 months in storage (oil will not hold the stability claim).
5. Colorado Mills (or another crusher) offers an HO seed contract at **$27/cwt or more** delivered while the realised oil blend is below **$3.75/lb**; at that point the margin over grain is under ~$300/ac and does not cover the capital at risk.
6. Press recovery in a vendor trial on the farm's seed is below **28%** filtered oil.
7. Realised bottle share of volume after year 2 is under **30%** and the blended price under **$3.50/lb**; then cap the line at stage 2 (45 ac) or exit.

Conclusion:
- Birdseed (2.9-3.0/5): worth doing only as the smallest-capex learning stage, sized to signed orders, with the explicit expectation of roughly break-even to a small loss.
