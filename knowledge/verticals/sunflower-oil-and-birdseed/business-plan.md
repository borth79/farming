# Sunflower oil and birdseed

> Status: compiled 2026-10-31 from web-search summaries only. Direct page fetches were
> blocked, so every price and equipment figure is a listing snapshot or an estimate
> marked **verify**. Nothing here is a quote. Section 12 lists what must be confirmed
> before spending money.

## 1. Concept
Two products from one crop, built in sequence:

- **Line A, birdseed (stage 1).** Grow conventional black-oil sunflower, clean it to a
  98% purity / 10% moisture birdseed spec on-farm, and sell it (a) bagged in 20- and
  40-lb bags to Wichita-area feed stores, garden centers, hardware stores, direct from
  the farm and online, and (b) in bulk to regional birdseed packers. Lowest effort,
  almost no regulation, uses equipment (cleaner, scale, bagger) the farm can reuse for
  seed wheat.
- **Line B, cold-pressed high-oleic (HO) sunflower oil (stages 2-3).** Grow HO hybrids,
  clean, cold-press on a 40 kg/h screw press, settle and filter, bottle, and sell to
  Wichita restaurants, specialty grocers, farmers markets and online. The pressed cake
  (meal) goes to local livestock feeders.

Why this farm: sunflower already fits the rotation as a broadleaf break after wheat
(`knowledge/crops/sunflower.md`), but the crop's main disqualifier in Sedgwick County is
market access: the nearest HO crush point is Colorado Mills in Lamar CO, ~330 road
miles, with no sunflower crusher left in Kansas since Scoular converted Goodland to
soy/canola. Pressing on-farm removes the freight ($1.00-1.50/cwt) and the dependence on
one distant buyer, and the Wichita metro (~650k people) is a large enough local market
to absorb a few thousand gallons of specialty oil and a few hundred tons of birdseed.
Birdseed is the cheap first step that teaches the crop and the retail channel before the
oil capital is committed.

## 3. Market

### Birdseed
- **Retail price benchmarks (Oct 2026 listings, verify locally):** 40-lb black-oil bags
  $16-22 at Walmart/Chewy (Pennington on sale $16.13; Wagner's $39.98-42.47 at Chewy and
  Home Depot), $23-28 at farm and garden stores (Bomgaars $22.99, Bayside Garden Center
  $26.99, Ocean State $27.99). 50-lb bags $25-43 at feed stores (Countrymax $24.99, Armor
  Animal Health $26.00, Mackey's $29.98, Sutherlands C&S $37.99, Southridge $42.95).
  Range: **$0.40-1.00/lb retail**, with big-box promotions at the bottom and
  specialty/wild-bird stores at the top.
- **Wholesale to stores:** no public price found. Working estimate: stores buying at
  **$0.40-0.50/lb** ($16-20 per 40 lb) to retail at $25-28 (verify with 3 store buyers).
- **Bulk to packers:** the only public bid is the USDA AMS Jamestown KS conventional
  bird-food bid of **~$19.50/cwt delivered (~$0.195/lb)**, ~Aug 2026. Black Sea export
  black-oil seed was ~565 EUR/t FOB Odesa in Oct 2026, confirming birdseed trades close
  to oilseed value. Bulk is a price floor, not a business.
- **Competitors:** national brands (Pennington, Wagner's, Kaytee, Morning Song, Lyric,
  Cole's, Audubon Park) and regional packers (e.g. Commodity Marketing Co black-oil seen on
  Woodard Mercantile in Wichita; Red River Commodities; Wild Birds Unlimited's own
  regional blends). They win on price per bag and distribution; the farm wins on
  freshness, "Kansas grown" and relationships with independent stores.
- **Wichita outlets (confirmed names only):** Wild Birds Unlimited (9738 E 21st St N),
  Valley Feed & Seed (1903 S Meridian), Woodard Mercantile (316-722-0216), Tractor Supply
  #325 (9425 W Kellogg) and other TSC stores, Lowe's x3, Home Depot, Walmart, Atwoods,
  Orscheln/Bomgaars, independent garden centers (Johnson's and others, **verify**). Chains
  buy centrally and are not stage-1 targets; independents and WBU are. Working target:
  **8-15 independent accounts** in Sedgwick, Butler, Harvey, Reno and Sumner counties by
  year 2 (count of stores not verified; build the list by phone).
- **Volume check:** 25 acres x 1,500 lb = 37,500 lb = ~940 forty-lb bags. A busy
  independent store might sell 10-40 bags of black-oil a week in winter; 10 accounts could
  move the whole crop in a season. Ramp: year 1 70% sold, year 2 85%, year 3 100%.
- **Seasonality:** 70%+ of birdseed sells Oct-Mar; harvest (Sep-Oct) lands at the right
  time, and summer storage is minimal.

  500 ml at $9. Blend **$4.75/lb** base, $3.25 adverse, $6.00 favorable. All **verify**.
- **Market size within reach:** a restaurant frying or finishing with sunflower oil uses
  5-35 gal/week. Stage 2 output (~2,800 gal/yr) is 10-20 restaurant accounts plus

  farm story); farmers markets (Old Town, Kansas Grown) for direct sales and feedback;
  specialty grocers (local co-op/natural food stores, **verify names**); online store.

### Meal
Local cattle, sheep, goat and poultry feeders within 30 miles. USDA ERS puts solvent
sunflower meal at $135/short ton (Feb 2026, ~$0.068/lb); expeller cake with 8-12% fat is
worth more as an energy-protein feed in small lots: working price **$0.085/lb** ($170/ton),
verify with two feeders and a lab test.

## 4. Raw material and agronomy link

- **Acres by stage:** stage 1 birdseed 20-30 ac black-oil; stage 2 add 40-50 ac HO;
  stage 3 80-100 ac HO (plus toll pressing for others). All dryland full-season after
  wheat; irrigated branch only if water rights are confirmed (`farm-profile.md`).
- **Yields (dryland, `sunflower.md`):** 1,500 lb/ac trend, 800 adverse, 2,200
  favorable on deep silt loam; 1,100 on shallow upland. Field selection matters more than
  hybrid.
- **Hybrid choice, and can one hybrid serve both lines?** Agronomically yes: K-State notes
  birdseed sunflower is grown with the same practices as oil-type, and HO hybrids are
  black-oil seeds of the same size and test weight; at least one farm brand (Forsyth Family
  Farm) sells HO seed as birdseed. Birds do not care about fatty-acid profile. So **an HO
  hybrid can feed both lines**, which is the simplest plan: one hybrid, one lot, the
  cleaner splits it into pressing seed and bagging seed. Reasons to still run two hybrids:
  (1) HO seed costs more ($340-420 per 200k bag, verify) and some HO seed is sold under
  contract-only terms; (2) if the crop ends up sold in bulk, a bird-food packer pays no HO
  premium and a crusher pays no premium for a conventional lot, so the hybrid decides
  which fallback market exists. Rule: **stage 1 conventional/NuSun black-oil (cheapest seed,
  bird-food fallback); from stage 2, all acres HO** so any surplus can go to Colorado
  Mills under an AOG contract while the pressing line ramps.
- **Quality specs:** birdseed <=10% moisture, <=2% FM, >=28 lb/bu, no live insects, free
  of noxious weed seed (feed law). Pressing seed: 40-46% oil (HO hybrids), <=8% moisture
  for seed held into summer (NDSU), free of heating and mold (mold raises FFA and ruins
  flavor). Sclerotinia-rotted heads and bird-damaged seed go to the bulk/feed stream.
- **Identity preservation:** HO and conventional lots in separate bins; clean the combine
  and cleaner between lots; keep bin tags and a lot number that follows seed to the bottle
  (traceability for KDA recall records).
- **Storage and insect control:** sunflower is light (28-32 lb/bu) and bins fill by
  volume; fines and trash heat, so clean before long storage. Aerate to below 60 F in the
  fall; hold 9-10% moisture for winter birdseed and <=8% for summer pressing. Indian meal
  moth is the main warehouse pest of bagged seed: pheromone traps, cool dry storage (~50
  F / 50% RH where possible), FIFO, sealed bags, and diatomaceous earth in empty bins.
  Phosphine fumigation works on sunflower seed (needs seed >=10 C, 3-30 day desorption,
  certified applicator); plan on not needing it for a 6-month inventory.
- **Off-spec grain:** heated, moldy or high-FM seed is sold bulk to a bird-food packer or
  fed; never pressed for food.

## 5. Process and equipment

**Birdseed flow:** combine -> aerated bin -> air-screen cleaner (scalp sticks and heads,
sift fines, air-lift dust and light seed) -> clean-seed hopper -> gross-weigh bagger ->
sewn or heat-sealed bag -> pallet -> store delivery or farm pickup. Screenings -> feed.

**Oil flow:** cleaned HO seed -> hopper -> screw press (seed temp <120 F for a
"cold-pressed" claim; UVM found ~15% more oil at half drive speed) -> crude oil to
conical settling tanks 7-14 days (sunflower throws the most sediment of the oils UVM
tested) -> plate-and-frame or cartridge polish filter -> stainless holding tank -> filler
-> cap -> label -> case. Cake -> auger -> totes/wagon -> feeders within days.

**Throughput:** one 40 kg/h press handles 67,500 lb seed in ~770 hours (about 32 days
of 24-h running or 4 months at 8 h/day), so stage 2 is a one-press, campaign-style
operation from Oct to Feb. Stage 3 adds a second press for redundancy and toll work.

**Labor (estimates):** birdseed ~200 h/yr for 25 ac (cleaning 25, bagging 16, selling and
delivery ~160). Oil ~800 h/yr for 45 ac (press tending 150, filtering/bottling 250, sales
and delivery 400). Press runs unattended with a full hopper; the hours are in selling.

**Utilities:** 240 V single-phase is enough for the KK40 (3 kW) and cleaner; ~2,300 kWh/yr
for pressing (~$285 at ~12.3 c/kWh). Potable water, hand sink, washable surfaces, sealed
floor, pest exclusion, separate meal storage.

**Automation:** hopper-fed press and gravity settling are the automation; bottling stays
semi-automatic until volumes exceed ~5,000 gal/yr.

## 7. Operations plan

- **Staffing:** owner-operator plus one seasonal part-time helper (Oct-Mar) at stage 1-2;
  stage 3 adds a part-time sales/delivery person.
- **Production calendar:** plant May-June; harvest Sep-Oct at 10-12% moisture; clean and
  bag birdseed Oct-Nov for the winter season, re-bag from bin as orders come; press HO seed
  Oct-Feb in campaigns, settle two weeks, filter and bottle to order (keeps oil in bulk
  tanks, minimises packaging inventory and shelf-life exposure).
- **Logistics:** pallet drops to stores on a weekly Wichita route (pickup and trailer at
  stage 1-2, used van at stage 3); parcel shipping for online oil; meal picked up by
  feeders or delivered in totes.
- **Packaging:** printed poly-woven bags (order 2,000-3,000 for price), 500 ml glass with
  tamper-evident cap and label, 1-gal HDPE jugs, 35-lb JIB. Reorder points at 25% of
  seasonal need.
- **Inventory:** clean seed in aerated bin (not bagged) until ordered; filtered oil in
  stainless tanks under nitrogen or minimal headspace; finished bottles <=60 days ahead of
  sales.

Reading the numbers honestly:

- **Birdseed at 25 acres does not pay on its own at a $0.50/lb blend.** Contribution is
  ~9 cents a pound against $6,200 of fixed cost and depreciation, so breakeven is ~49
  acres, or 25 acres at a price blend of ~$0.60/lb (mostly direct sales at $25 per 40-lb
  bag). The value of stage 1 is cheap market learning and reusable equipment, not profit.
  It should be run with the smallest capex that works (used cleaner, mechanical bagger) and
  sized to the orders in hand.
- **The oil line carries the economics.** Base case roughly breaks even on an NPV basis
  over 5 years and is cash-positive from year 4; the result is driven almost entirely by
  (1) sell-through rate and (2) the share of oil sold in bottles rather than food-service
  jugs. Price floor is ~$2.90/lb (~$22/gal), so food-service-only pricing at $25/gal leaves
  very little margin; bottles at $9-12 per 500 ml are where the money is.
- **Adverse stacks everything bad at once** (800 lb/ac, 28% recovery, $3.25/lb, 35-85%
  sell-through, $101k capex) and shows the downside is real: ~$350k cumulative loss if the
  farm builds the line and the market does not come. Mitigation is in section 9: do not
  buy the press until a sales test with toll-pressed or purchased oil proves 1,000 gal of
  demand.
- **Return on capital (base, oil line):** year 4-5 EBITDA ~$52k on ~$118k capital = ~44%
  cash return once ramped, but only after three loss or near-zero years.
- **Comparison with the same acres sold as grain:** the models already charge the seed at
  its grain value, so the added margin over grain equals EBITDA: oil line +$52k/yr on 80
  ac at full run rate (~$650/ac added, base), roughly zero in years 1-2; birdseed about
  -$50/ac at 25 ac base, +$350/ac favorable. For reference, HO seed delivered to Lamar
  nets ~$375-383/ac gross revenue at trend yield (`sunflower.md`).
- **Freight advantage vs Lamar:** $1.00-1.50/cwt on 1,500 lb/ac = $15-23/ac, or
  $700-1,000/yr on 45 ac. Real but small; the vertical's case rests on retail oil margin,
  not freight.

## 10. Risks and mitigations

| Risk | Effect | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Market: oil sells slower than planned (the dominant risk) | adverse case, -$350k | sales test before press purchase; bulk tanks so unsold oil stays unpackaged; AOG contract for surplus seed; year-by-year acreage |
| Market: birdseed price war with big-box bags at $16-20 | contribution to zero | focus on independents and direct; 20-lb premium bags; stop bagging and sell bulk when blend <$0.42/lb |
| Crop: drought (800 lb/ac), head moth, blackbirds, Sclerotinia | half the seed | field selection, 4-yr rotation, scouting per `sunflower.md`; press line sized for 45 ac so a short crop still covers fixed cost partly; buy HO seed from a neighbor to keep the press running (toll) |
| Operational: press downtime, screw wear, oil quality (sediment, FFA) | lost campaign, rancid oil | spare screw and nozzles; two smaller presses at stage 3; settle fully; store oil cool, low headspace; test every batch |
| Food safety: rancidity, foreign material, mislabel | recall, reputation | lot traceability, PV/FFA limits, label review by KDA, recall plan, insurance |
| Regulatory: KDA treats wild bird seed as commercial feed; food license delays | fees small, delay large | apply 6 months before first sale; budget for both licenses |
| Weather/storage: hot summer storage of seed or meal | heating, rancid meal | <=8% moisture, aeration, sell meal within weeks |
| Key person | one operator sells, presses, farms | written SOPs; seasonal helper trained on cleaner and bottling |
| Capex overrun on food-grade room | +$15k | two contractor quotes and KDA pre-inspection before build |
| Price of competing refined HO oil falls (world sunflower oil weak, record Black Sea crop 2026) | restaurant price pressure | sell the story and the bottle, not the jug |

## 11. Decision rules

- **Go to stage 1** only with a signed or verbal commitment from 3+ store buyers and a
  field that passes the `sunflower.md` disqualifier list.
- **Go to stage 2** only after the oil sales test (section 9) and with an HO AOG contract
  covering at least the pressing acreage as fallback. Do not buy the press to "see if it
  sells".
- **Kill birdseed bagging** if two consecutive seasons blend below $0.42/lb or sell-through
  below 60%; keep growing for bulk only if a contract exists.
- **Kill the oil line** if year-2 sell-through <60% or blended price <$3.50/lb; sell the
  press (resale market exists) and revert to AOG contract seed.
- **Review dates:** 1 March each year (post-season sell-through, before planting
  decisions); 15 October (contract window for next year's HO acres, per `sunflower.md`).
