# Wheat → stone-ground flour (→ bread): competitor and market-share analysis

## Summary
No stone mill operates inside the Wichita metro today; the nearest craft mills are Janzen
Milling (Hillsboro, ~55 mi), Farmer Direct Foods (New Cambria, ~95 mi, industrial-scale
stone mill, 150,000 lb/day), Hudson Cream (Hudson, ~100 mi, roller mill) and Chisholm Trail
Milling (Enid OK, ~110 mi). The Wichita-reachable demand for specialty (non-commodity,
≥$0.90/lb) flour is estimated at **~300,000 lb/yr (range 200,000–450,000)**, out of roughly
1.5–2 million lb/yr of bagged flour bought by independent bakeries, pizzerias and
restaurants. A new Sedgwick County mill can realistically sell **10–20k lb in year 1,
40–60k in year 3 and 70–100k in year 5**, i.e. 5% → 15–20% → 25–30% of that specialty
pool. The model's base case (19k / 61k / 115k lb) is inside this range in years 1–3 but
**~15–40% above it in year 5** unless a third of year-5 volume comes from outside Wichita
(Kansas City, Oklahoma City, e-commerce). The vertical scores 3.2/5: attractive unit
economics and a genuine local gap, offset by a thin specialty pool, a strong Kansas
incumbent (Farmer Direct) and a sales-constrained ramp.

## 1. Market definition and size

### Definition
The product competes in **specialty flour**: stone-ground, whole-grain, high-extraction,
identity-preserved or heritage flour sold at ≥$0.90/lb wholesale (≥$1.80/lb retail), bought
on provenance, freshness and flavor. It does **not** compete in commodity bakery flour
($0.30–0.60/lb in 50-lb bags), which is the price most Wichita bakeries pay today and
which no farm-scale mill can match (business-plan.md section 8: price floor $0.38/lb before
any fixed cost).

### Regional (Kansas / Plains)
Kansas is the largest HRW state and hosts large industrial mills in Wichita itself (Ardent
Mills, 715 E 13th St; Grain Craft, 701 E 17th St N) plus Newton, McPherson and Rosedale
(Kansas Wheat Commission directory, dated). Craft/stone capacity in the region is dominated
by one firm, Farmer Direct Foods (150,000 lb/day stone capacity, ~12 million lb/yr normal
output in 2020, expanding), with a handful of farm-scale mills each probably under
100,000 lb/yr (Janzen, Stone & Sparrow, Chisholm Trail, Marion Milling; volumes **verify**).
Kansas City and Tulsa have visible "local grain" bakery scenes (Startland News 2025; OSU
Cowboy Journal 2026); Wichita does not yet.

Rounded: **~300,000 lb/yr specialty demand reachable from Sedgwick County, range
200,000–450,000**. For scale, that is one-sixth of Farmer Direct's 2020 output and about

30 account interviews recording current flour, price paid and weekly bags.

### Growth trends and the home-baking / sourdough pattern
- Specialty flour demand grew 8–11%/yr in the summaries above; total flour is flat to
  down. The growth is in whole-grain, high-extraction (T85-style), heritage (Turkey Red,
  Rouge de Bordeaux), and "traceable" flour.
- Home baking spikes are event-driven: Farmer Direct's output jumped from ~12 to ~20
  million lb in 2020 and then normalized (Food Business News). Sourdough interest peaked
  again in 2025 and is still rising in 2026 (AMF). Home bakers buy 2–5 lb bags at
  $2.50–4.50/lb from craft mills (Janzen $3.00–4.00/lb, Stone & Sparrow $2.80/lb,
  Marion $3.30–4.67/lb, Farmer Direct $1.20–2.26/lb in grocery), which is the margin-rich
  retail segment in the model's favorable case.
- Home milling (Mockmill $890, ~200 g/min) is growing from a tiny base and converts a few
  heavy home bakers from flour buyers into **wheat-berry buyers**, which this farm can
  serve directly (stage-2 product line).
- Bakery in-house milling (Great Harvest Wichita mills Montana wheat in store; Country
  Bird Bakery in Tulsa started on a used mill before switching to Chisholm Trail) is a
  substitute for the largest accounts but also creates a clean-grain market.

## 3. Market share: obtainable volume

| | Year 1 | Year 3 | Year 5 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Specialty pool (lb/yr, point estimate; grows ~8%/yr) | 300,000 | 350,000 | 410,000 |
| **Realistic obtainable sales (lb)** | **10,000–20,000** | **40,000–60,000** | **70,000–100,000** (incl. 15,000–30,000 outside Wichita) |
| Share of Wichita-reachable specialty pool | 3–7% | 11–17% | 15–20% (Wichita portion) |
| Accounts implied | 1–2 anchors (one 50-lb bag/day ≈ 12,000–18,000 lb/yr) + markets | 2 anchors + 8–12 smaller + 3–5 grocer doors | 3–4 anchors + 15–20 accounts + e-commerce + 1–2 KC/OKC bakeries |
| **assumptions.csv base case (acres × 2,550 lb/acre × sales %)** | **19,125** (15 ac × 50%) | **61,200** (30 ac × 80%) | **114,750** (50 ac × 90%) |
| Model as % of specialty pool | 6% | 17% | 28% |
| **Verdict** | Within range (upper half) | At top of range | **Mismatch: 15–40% above the realistic range** |

Reasoning:
- A new mill with no brand can expect to convert the handful of bakeries already paying
  for specialty flour (perhaps 5–8 in Wichita) plus a few that will trade up for a
  marketing story. Year-1 volume is bounded by sales calls, not by demand; 19,000 lb in
  year 1 requires an anchor signed before the first milling season, which the stage-0 gate
  already demands.
- By year 3, 17% of a 350,000-lb specialty pool is achievable only if the farm has become
  the default "local flour" in Wichita and Farmer Direct has not responded on price. It
  is plausible but should be treated as the favorable edge of base.
- Year 5 at 114,750 lb is 28% of the Wichita specialty pool. Cairnspring, the best
  craft-at-scale example, needed a 4-million-person Seattle market to reach 7 million lb;
  Farmer Direct reaches 12 million lb only by co-packing for King Arthur and shipping
  nationally. A Wichita-only mill capping at ~80,000–100,000 lb/yr is the better planning
  figure; the remaining 15,000–35,000 lb must be explicitly assigned to Kansas City/OKC
  bakeries, e-commerce or a bakery partner (stage 3), or the acreage cap should be 40 not
  50.
- Recommendation for assumptions.csv: keep year-1–3 base; lower year-5 base
  `sales_pct_of_output` to ~75% (≈95,000 lb on 50 acres) **or** hold base acres at 40 in
  year 5 (102,000 lb × 90% = 91,800); add a row `sales_share_outside_wichita` (base 20%
  in year 5) so the regional dependency is auditable. The adverse ramp (75% of 30 acres =
  57,000 lb) is consistent with this analysis.

## 4. Differentiation

### What this farm can claim and competitors cannot
| Claim | Who else can say it in Wichita | Value to a bakery |
|---|---|---|
| **Single-farm, Sedgwick County grown and milled** ("20 miles from your oven") | Nobody today; Janzen (Hillsboro) is single-farm but 55 mi and not Sedgwick County | Menu-board story; "Wichita wheat" loaf; farm visits for bakery staff and customers |
| **Milled this week, to order** | Janzen, Marion (KC) do it; Farmer Direct ships from inventory via distributor | Flavor and aroma in whole-grain and T85 flours; shorter shelf-life is a feature if deliveries are weekly |
| **Variety-specific lots** (e.g. KS Hatchett, Zenda) with protein, falling number and DON on the bag | Heartland and Farmer Direct give specs, not field lots | Bakers can tune hydration; pizzerias can get a lower-protein lot |
| **Turkey Red heritage option** (2–5 acres) | Farmer Direct, Janzen, Stone & Sparrow, Marion all sell Turkey Red; the differentiator is local, not the variety | Premium 5-lb retail at $3.50–4.50/lb; limited volume |
| **Wheat berries and bran from the same lot** | Janzen sells berries | Serves in-house and home millers; bran to brewers/bakers |
| **Farm-to-loaf co-branding** with one partner bakery (stage 3) | Nobody | Exclusive story for the anchor |

### What it cannot claim
- **Price**: cannot approach $0.30–0.75/lb commodity flour, and is only at parity with
  Farmer Direct's $1.56/lb whole wheat and Cairnspring's $1.30/lb craft bread flour.
- **Organic**: the farm is conventional; Heartland, Janie's, Central Milling and Marion
  own that segment. "No additives", "non-GMO" (true of all wheat) and "regenerative"
  (only if practices are documented) are the available adjacent claims.
- **Consistency**: year 1 will have lot-to-lot variation in protein, ash and water
  absorption; Farmer Direct and the nationals blend across growers. Mitigate with specs on
  every bag and a bakery test loaf per lot.
- **Technical support and credit**: distributors offer next-day delivery, net-30 and
  bakery technologists; the farm offers one delivery day a week.
- **White patent flour**: a stone mill with a sifter cannot make a true patent flour; the
  farm competes in whole wheat, T85 and "artisan AP", not in croissant or cake flour.

## 6. Disqualifiers (do not pursue, or stop, if any holds)
1. **Fewer than 2 Wichita bakery/pizzeria letters of intent at ≥$1.25/lb for 50-lb bags totaling ≥15,000 lb/yr** after samples to 20 accounts (stage-0 gate, business-plan.md section 11), or fewer than 5 accounts expressing willingness to pay ≥$1.00/lb.
2. **An existing stone mill already serving Wichita bakeries at scale** is discovered (e.g. Farmer Direct is found in ≥3 Wichita bakeries through Scavuzzo's at ≤$1.25/lb, or a licensed Sedgwick County stone mill exists) — the differentiation then rests on "single farm" alone, which is worth less than the $0.60–1.00/lb premium required.
3. **Farm wheat tests below 12.0% protein or above 1 ppm DON in two consecutive harvests** on the candidate mill fields, or falling number <300 s; the farm then has no reliable raw material and would be a toll-miller of bought grain.
4. **No existing building passes KDA plan review for ≤$45,000 of fit-out**, or firm stage-1 quotes exceed $125,000 (adverse capital $167,200 gives NPV −$274,000 with no payback).
5. **Wichita's two or three largest artisan accounts mill in-house or are franchise-sourced** (Great Harvest is) and the remaining independents together cannot absorb 15,000 lb/yr at the target price.
6. **Farm cannot carry a year-1 cash draw of $80,000–140,000** without stressing operating credit (finance-controller sign-off).
7. **Nobody on the farm is willing to make 30 sales calls and one Wichita delivery run per week for two years**; this is a sales business with a mill attached.

**Conclusion.** The vertical is a sales problem with excellent gross margin: ~75% on flour
at $1.50/lb, a real and currently unoccupied "Sedgwick County single-farm" position, and a
raw material the farm already produces. Against that, the Wichita specialty pool is small
enough that the model's year-5 base (114,750 lb, 28% of the pool) is optimistic by
15–40%, Farmer Direct can reach any Wichita bakery through Scavuzzo's at ~$1.56/lb (or
less if it chooses), and the default competitor is a $0.40/lb commodity bag that most
bakeries will keep. The economics are robust at 60,000–90,000 lb/yr on 25–40 acres
(breakeven 35,700 lb base), so the right posture is to size the plan to ~40 acres and
~90,000 lb at year 5, keep the 50-acre case as favorable, and treat Kansas City/OKC and
e-commerce as explicit, separately gated volume rather than implied.

**The single most important thing to verify first:** sit down with 20–30 Wichita bakeries,
pizzerias and in-house-bread restaurants with a 5-lb sample and a spec sheet, and record
for each: current flour and supplier, price paid per 50 lb, bags per week, and whether they
would sign a 12-month letter of intent at $1.25/lb or more. If fewer than two will, or if
Farmer Direct is already on their shelves at ≤$1.25/lb, the vertical fails disqualifiers
1–2 and nothing else in this file matters.
