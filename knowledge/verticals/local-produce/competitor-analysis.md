# Local produce: competitor and market-share analysis for three Wichita wholesale channels

## Summary
Wichita-metro grocery stores buy roughly **$60–200 million of fresh produce a year (base ~$130M)**;
restaurants and foodservice roughly **$35–85 million (base ~$55M)**; the chef-driven tier is
perhaps 20–25 restaurants whose *local* produce spend together is only **~$0.2–0.7 million
(base ~$0.35M)**. Local farms hold well under 2% of the first two channels; the supply is
owned by Dillons' Hutchinson warehouse, Walmart's DC network, and broadliners run from
Olathe (Sysco), Lenexa (US Foods), Springfield/Kansas City (PFG) and Edmond OK (Ben E. Keith),
with Liberty Fruit (Kansas City KS) the produce specialist that reaches Wichita. The local
competitors are small: Firefly Farm/RISE Farms (30–38 restaurant accounts, heirloom tomatoes),
Golden Prairie Farm, Serenity Farm (40 ac CSA), Elderslie Farm (grows for its own
restaurant), Four Dogs Farm (Newton; GreenAcres), Home Grown Kansas, plus 100+ vendors at
each of two Saturday markets. **None of them sells storable crops in volume in winter**, and
that is the gap. Realistic obtainable wholesale sales across the three channels are about
**$15–30k in year 1, $60–110k in year 3, $110–200k in year 5**, which is 0.01–0.1% of the
grocery and restaurant channels and 5–15% of the chef-driven local pool. The chef-driven
channel should lead (score 3.8/5), independent restaurants second (3.0), grocery last (2.6):
grocery pays commodity prices (onions $0.42–0.60/lb, red potatoes $0.35–0.80/lb at terminal,
Nov 2026) and Dillons/Walmart require audits and $1–3M liability before a pallet moves.
The $1.60/lb blend in assumptions.csv does not survive a storable-crop, wholesale mix; expect
$0.80–1.10/lb at grocery/distributor and $1.30–1.80/lb chef-direct. The first thing to verify
is whether 8–12 Wichita chefs will sign letters of intent for weekly winter deliveries
(Oct–Apr) of specialty potatoes, alliums, garlic and winter squash at ≥$1.50/lb.

Size arithmetic (three methods, all flagged):
- Bottom-up, IFPA benchmark: ~50 stores × $1.12M average produce sales per store (2025) ≈ **$56M**.
  The IFPA average mixes small and large stores and reads low for supercenters.
- Bottom-up, FMI benchmark: $668,377 average weekly supermarket sales × 52 = $34.8M per store
  × 10% produce = $3.5M per store × ~45 supermarkets ≈ **$155M**.
- Top-down, per capita: FMI supermarket sales ~$1T × 10% produce = ~$100B ÷ ~340M people
  ≈ $295 per person × 664,000 ≈ **$195M**.
- **Working range $60–200M; base ~$130M per year.** Produce is 10–11% of store sales (FMI
  2024–25) and grew ~3.3%/yr per store (IFPA 2025); fresh is the growth department.

Local share today: Kansas-wide intermediated local sales (retail, institutions, hubs) were only
**$6.9M across all products in 2022** (Census of Agriculture), so local produce in Wichita
grocery is at most **$1–3M, ~1–2%**, concentrated in summer melons, sweet corn, pumpkins and
tomatoes through Dillons' warehouse and GreenAcres/independents. Reachable share for all
local growers combined could be 3–5% in season; in Nov–Apr it is near zero because nobody
local stores crops at scale. Seasonality: store produce sales peak Jun–Aug and at holidays;
local supply Jun–Oct.

blend and should be split by crop; sell-through 65–85% is plausible for storables (storage
loss 10–20% replaces market-day waste).

### Differentiation
Claimable: Sedgwick County grown (the only in-county storable supply in winter), single
identified farm, harvest-to-shelf 1–3 days for fresh items, cured and stored potatoes, onions,
garlic, sweet potatoes and squash available Nov–Mar when no other local grower has them,
family trust farm story, "From the Land of Kansas" mark once enrolled. Not claimable:
organic (not certified; Natural Grocers excluded), year-round variety, price parity with the
DC (the farm will be 20–60% above terminal), GAP-certified until audited.

### Disqualifiers
- Dillons/Kroger DC or Walmart: disqualified for stages 1–2 (GFSI/Harmonized GAP+ plus
  $3M GL plus PTI labelling cost > 10% of channel revenue below ~$40k/yr).
- Any grocer whose offered price for potatoes, onions, sweet potatoes or squash is below
  **$0.60/lb** delivered, or below **$0.30/lb** for pumpkins: decline (below cost at this scale).
- Natural Grocers: disqualified (organic-only).
- Fewer than **3 independent stores** willing to take weekly pallets in year 2: drop the
  channel to opportunistic sales only.

## Channel 2: Mainstream restaurants and foodservice

### Market definition and size
Full-service (NAICS 722511) and limited-service (722513) restaurants plus caterers,
schools, hospitals and universities in the Wichita MSA.

Counts (directory and ratio, verify with CBP): Kansas 5,531 restaurant locations (NRA, 2024);
Sedgwick County is ~18% of Kansas population → ~1,000; metro ~1,100–1,300. Directories
give Wichita city 962–1,475. **Working figure 1,200 restaurant locations**, split roughly
550 full-service / 650 limited-service on the national establishment ratio (flag). Perhaps
40–45% are independents; the rest are chains on contracted broadline supply.

Spend arithmetic:
- Average sales per location ~$1.2M (Kansas NRA sales ÷ locations; verify) → food purchases
  at 30% food cost ≈ $360k.
- Produce share of food purchases: full-service 15–20%, limited-service 8–12% (vendor
  benchmarks; flag).
- Full-service: 550 × $360k × 17% ≈ **$34M**; limited-service: 650 × $360k × 10% ≈ **$23M**;
  restaurants total ≈ **$55M (range $35–85M)**; adding institutions and caterers (~+30%)
  gives foodservice produce of roughly **$70–80M**.
- Local share today: Firefly/RISE Farms (30–38 accounts), Golden Prairie, Home Grown
  Kansas and a few others supply perhaps **$0.3–0.8M, i.e. ~1%**, almost all Jun–Oct and
  almost all to the chef-driven tier. Potential local share in the mainstream tier is low
  (price-driven, chains locked in) — 2–3% of independents' produce at best.
- Growth: Wichita openings are mostly chains (Chipotle #9, Cheesecake Factory, Sept 2026);
  independents churn; flat to +2%/yr real.
