# Vertical integration options

## Summary
At about 1,000 acres, verticals that pay are small, automated, mechanical processing
steps applied to crops the farm already grows, sold to nearby buyers who pay for
provenance. Each candidate below can start on 10–50 acres and grow with sales. The
binding constraint is selling, not producing: a 50-acre line produces more product than
a farmers-market presence can absorb, so each plan starts with wholesale accounts.

Criteria for an online product: shelf stable, light to ship, a Kansas or farm story, and
an automatable processing step. Fresh produce, meat and cold-chain products do not fit.

## Line 1 in detail: wheat to flour to bread
### Volumes
- 50 acres × 45 bu = 2,250 bu = 135,000 lb grain.
- Whole-wheat extraction ~95%: ~128,000 lb flour. Sifted ~75%: ~100,000 lb flour + bran.
- A 50-lb bag per bakery per day is ~18,000 lb/yr per account; 100,000 lb needs ~5–6 daily-bag bakeries or a retail brand.

### Stage plan
1. **Flour wholesale (year 1–2)**: 10–20 acres identity-preserved, high-protein variety;
   grain cleaner, mill, sifter, 50-lb and 5-lb bagging. Sell to Wichita bakeries,
   pizzerias, restaurants, farmers markets, local grocery programs. Labor: one part-time
   operator. Target: prove 20,000–40,000 lb/yr of sales.
2. **Add products (year 2–3)**: cornmeal and grits (food-grade corn), sorghum flour on a
   dedicated line, wheat berries, bran to feed.
3. **Bakery (year 3+)**: either partner with an existing bakery (co-brand, supply flour)
   or build a licensed bakery with automated mixing, dividing, proofing and deck ovens.
   Direct bread sales under Kansas cottage-food rules can test demand before building.
4. **Scale**: add acres and mill capacity as accounts grow; consider regional
   distribution and e-commerce for shelf-stable flour.

### Economics sketch (verify)
| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| 50 acres as grain | ~$13,000 at $5.75/bu |
| Same as flour at $1.50/lb wholesale, 100,000 lb | ~$150,000 gross |
| Milling cost per lb (power, bags, labor, depreciation) | ~$0.25–0.45 |
| Realistic year-1 sales | 20,000–40,000 lb → $30,000–60,000 gross |
| Breakeven on a $80,000 mill line | ~3–5 years at year-2 volumes |

### Risks
- Sales effort and account churn.
- Food safety: moisture, mycotoxin testing (DON/vomitoxin on wheat), pest control.
- Variety and protein consistency year to year; weather may force buying grain in.
- Competition from established regional mills.

## Line 3 in detail: sunflower oil and birdseed
- 50 acres × 1,500 lb = 75,000 lb seed. Oil at ~40% recovery by cold press ~30,000 lb
  (~3,900 gal) plus ~45,000 lb meal for feed.
- Press 50–100 kg/hr class runs unattended with hopper feed; filtration and bottling
  semi-automatic.
- High-oleic oil markets: restaurants, specialty grocers, farmers markets, online.
- Birdseed: clean and bag black-oil seed; sell in 20–40 lb bags to Wichita feed and
  garden stores and direct. Minimal regulation.

## Line 4 in detail: small-square hay and straw
- Small-square alfalfa (50–70 lb bales) to horse owners; wheat straw to landscape,
  construction erosion control, bedding.
- Accumulator and bundler eliminate hand stacking; bundles move with a loader.
- Quality and dust matter more than yield; storage under roof is required.

## Licensing checklist (Kansas, verify current)
- Kansas Department of Agriculture food processing or food establishment license for
  wholesale flour, oil, baked goods.
- FDA food facility registration and preventive controls (small-business exemptions may
  apply).
- Kansas cottage-food rules: direct-to-consumer sales of non-perishable baked goods
  without a license, with labeling; no wholesale.
- Weights and measures for packaged products; nutrition labeling thresholds.
- KCIA for certified seed; TTB and state ABC only if alcohol is produced.

## Decision rules
1. Start one line (flour) on no more than 20 acres; add a second line only after
   first-line sales exceed production for two consecutive quarters.
2. Any line must reach gross margin after labor above the grain-only margin on the same
   acres within three seasons or be wound down.
3. Prefer lines whose processing step runs unattended; avoid lines where labor is the
   main input unless a partner supplies it.
4. Buy used equipment for stage 1; buy new only when volume is proven.
5. Keep the vertical's books separate from the farm so its true return is visible.
