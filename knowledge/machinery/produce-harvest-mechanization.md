# Harvest mechanization for non-commodity crops

## Summary
The farm's tractors can power pull-type and three-point harvesters for root, bulb and
vine crops; the combine handles only grain-like specialty crops. Fresh-market fruit
vegetables (tomatoes, peppers, cucumbers, melons, greens) are hand-picked for grocery
sale everywhere; the mechanization available is a harvest aid that moves pickers and totes.
Because labor is this farm's binding constraint for any produce enterprise, the
mechanizable crops are the realistic candidates.

## Mechanizable with tractors (candidate list)
| Crop | Implement | Used price range (verify) | Labor after machine | Storable | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Potatoes | 1-row or 2-row chain digger, 3-pt or pull-type; 2-row with conveyor/bagger | $2,000–6,000 (1-row); $8,000–25,000 (2-row) | hand pickup behind a 1-row; loading only with 2-row conveyor | yes, cool dark storage | Colorado potato beetle; new potatoes early |
| Sweet potatoes | same digger, deeper setting | same | hand pickup | yes after curing | heat-loving; fits Kansas |
| Onions, garlic, shallots | undercutter/lifter bar; top-lifter or digger; windrow loader | $3,000–10,000 | windrow cure then pickup | yes | garlic planted in fall fits grain calendar |
| Carrots, beets, turnips | 1-row top-lifting harvester | $10,000–40,000 | minimal | yes | uniform stands and sandy loam |
| Pumpkins, winter squash | vine windrower + boom conveyor loader into wagon | $10,000–30,000 | 2–3 people on the conveyor | yes (squash months) | easiest vine crop; agritourism option |
| Green beans | 1-row tractor-mounted picker | $15,000–40,000 | sorting | no | once-over; viable above ~3 acres |
| Sweet corn | 1-row tractor-mounted picker | $20,000–60,000 | bagging | days | justified above ~5 acres; hand pick below |
| Popcorn, dry beans, food-grade sorghum, sunflower, heritage wheat, millet | existing combine with corn head or flex/draper header | existing | cleaning | yes | the combine's only produce-aisle role |

## Hand-harvested with harvest aids
| Crop | Aid | Price (verify) | Effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tomatoes, peppers, cucumbers, summer squash, okra, melons, strawberries, greens | tractor-pulled conveyor or picking platform with tote carriers; shade | $5,000–20,000 | roughly doubles output per picker; removes carrying |
| Melons | boom conveyor into wagon | $8,000–20,000 | pickers hand fruit to conveyor |
Processing-grade harvesters exist for tomatoes and cucumbers but produce unsellable
fresh-market quality; not applicable.

## Implications for crop selection
- With no dedicated labor, prefer the mechanizable and storable set: potatoes, sweet
  potatoes, onions and garlic, pumpkins and winter squash, popcorn and dry beans.
- Add hand-picked crops only when a person is dedicated to the enterprise; buy the
  harvest aid before expanding them.
- Share one digger across potatoes, sweet potatoes, onions and garlic; one conveyor
  loader across pumpkins, squash and melons.
- Buy used; most small vegetable implements are simple, durable and available at farm
  auctions and from growers exiting.

## Decision rules
1. Any produce crop over one acre must have its harvest method and implement priced
   before planting.
2. Hand-picked crops are capped at what one person can pick in 20 hours a week until a
   second person is committed.
3. Implement purchases are justified against the custom or hand-labor cost they replace
   over three seasons.
