# Operating within the family trust

## Summary
The land is held in a family trust. That means the trustee, not the operator, holds
legal title and must act within the trust's terms and fiduciary duties. Most day-to-day
farming is unaffected. The constraints appear when the plan calls for anything that
changes the land, encumbers it, uses it for a non-farm business, shifts income among
beneficiaries, or risks a tax recapture. This file sets out what to learn from the trust
document, the constraints that most often bind a farm, how each planned activity in this
project maps to them, and the operating structure that usually resolves most issues: an
operating entity that leases the land from the trust under a written lease.

## Constraints that most often bind a farm, and what they touch
| Constraint | Typical source | What it blocks or changes | Planned activities affected |
|---|---|---|---|
| Land must remain in agricultural use | trust terms; §2032A qualified use; Kansas ag-use property tax | non-farm commercial use of a building or plot can breach terms or trigger recapture and reassessment | mill, bakery, oil press, retail stand, agritourism |
| §2032A recapture (10 years after death; cash rent to non-family or cessation of qualified use triggers additional estate tax) | prior estate election | the farm must be operated by qualified heirs with material participation; cash leasing to an unrelated operator can trigger recapture | lease structure, custom farming arrangements, who operates |
| Limits on leases (term, to whom, rent basis) | trust terms; trustee duty of impartiality | long leases needed to justify tenant investment may not be allowed; rent must be fair to remainder beneficiaries | operating entity lease; produce plot; grazing lease to a cattle owner |
| No mortgage or pledge of trust land | trust terms or missing power | equipment and operating loans cannot use land as collateral; lenders look to the operating entity | equipment financing, storage, structures |
| Capital improvements belong to the trust; tenant cannot remove or be reimbursed | default landlord–tenant law unless lease says otherwise | long-payback structures built by the operator are at risk | bins, high tunnel, mill room, pond, terraces |
| Income must be distributed; cannot be retained | distribution terms | the trust cannot self-fund improvements; the operator must | all capital plans |
| Trustee fiduciary duty: prudence, impartiality, no self-dealing | Kansas Uniform Trust Code | a trustee who is also the operator must document fair rent and arm's-length terms, or get beneficiary consent | every lease and transaction between operator and trust |
| Kansas corporate farming law | K.S.A. 17-5904 | limits which entities may hold agricultural land; family farm trusts and authorized trusts generally qualify; a new LLC acquiring land must qualify | any land acquisition by an operating LLC |
| USDA program eligibility and payment limits | 7 CFR 1400 | trusts and their beneficiaries count as persons; actively-engaged and AGI tests; payment limitation per person | ARC/PLC, EQIP, CRP, disaster programs, crop insurance subsidies |
| Water rights tied to the land and to a specific use | Kansas water law | changing from irrigation to another use, or irrigating a new plot, needs DWR approval; the right belongs to the land owner | irrigated corn, produce plot |
| Liability exposure of trust assets | general | a food business operated directly by the trust exposes the land to product liability claims | all verticals, agritourism, u-pick |

## Recommended structure (to confirm with counsel)
1. **Operating entity**: a family-owned LLC (or the existing operating entity) farms the
   land under a written lease from the trust. The LLC owns equipment, inventory, prepaid
   inputs, bins it builds (by lease clause), and all vertical businesses. Liability from
   processing and retail stays in the LLC, not on the land.
2. **Lease terms that matter**: a term long enough to amortize operator-built
   improvements (or a reimbursement schedule on termination), explicit permission for
   named structures, a rent basis that a trustee can defend as fair (cash, flexible, or
   crop share benchmarked to Kansas State and KFMA rent surveys), assignment rules, and
   who holds and reports government program payments.
3. **Verticals**: processing and retail in a second LLC if the trust or §2032A status makes
   non-agricultural use on trust land risky; site the mill room or press on an owned
   non-trust parcel, a leased commercial space, or a corner of trust land under a
   separate commercial lease if permitted.
4. **Documentation**: trustee resolutions for each lease, improvement and program
   enrollment; annual accounting to beneficiaries; appraisal or survey support for rent.
5. **Review triggers**: any new structure, lease change, borrowing, program enrollment,
   water change, or business line goes through the compliance checklist before money is
   spent.

## Tax items to raise with the CPA
- Whether a §2032A election is in force and how many years remain in the recapture
  period; what counts as qualified use and material participation for the current
  operator.
- Grantor versus non-grantor status and where farm income is taxed.
- Basis step-up at the grantor's death and how improvements built by the operator are
  treated.
- Self-employment tax and Social Security effects of cash rent versus crop share versus
  material participation by a trustee-beneficiary.
- Section 199A treatment of co-op patronage and the operating entity's income.
- Kansas agricultural-use property valuation and any effect of a processing building.

## Decision rules
1. No permanent structure, long-term lease, easement, loan secured by land, water change,
   or non-agricultural business starts on trust land without a written trustee approval
   that cites the trust power relied on.
2. Every transaction between the operator and the trust is priced at a documented market
   rate.
3. Verticals operate through the LLC, never directly through the trust.
4. The compliance officer runs this file's checklist on every new plan element; the
   finance controller confirms capital ownership and payback against the lease term.
5. Unknown trust terms are treated as prohibitions until read.
