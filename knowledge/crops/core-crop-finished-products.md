# Finished products from the core crops (beyond the planned verticals)

## Summary
Nothing on this list is a large business. The products with high uplift per acre (decorative
wheat, cornmeal and grits, edamame, small-pet hay, wheat berries, heirloom corn) are
limited by the market to 0.2–2 acres each, so each one adds roughly $2,000–9,000 a year. The
products that can use 20–40 acres (deer corn, milo and wildlife bags, wheat straw small
squares, horse-hay small squares, alfalfa pellets made by a custom mill) add only $190–1,150
per acre. Three of them (deer and wildlife bags, straw, custom-milled pellets) are worth
testing because they need little capital, can be done by machine, and do not need a
dedicated person. Tofu, tempeh, soy nuts, sunflower butter, roasted in-shell sunflower,
extruded soy, an owned feed mill and a corn maze all fail or are disqualified. The usual
reasons are fixed costs on a tiny market, daily perishable labor, allergen rules, or harvest
labor needed at the same time as the grain harvest. Wichita's fresh masa and tortilla trade
(19 % of the city is Hispanic, about 82 % of that Mexican-origin) pays well per acre on paper.
But it needs about 344 hours per acre of daily production, which makes it a job, not a side
line. The realistic corn entry is selling food-grade white corn to tortillerias that already
exist.

## Method and conventions
- **Raw baseline (net over direct costs, Oct 2026 cash prices, from the crop files):** corn
  110 bu × $4.35 − $385 = **$94/ac** (raw value $478.50); soybeans 36 bu × $11.50 − $255 =
  **$159/ac** ($414); alfalfa 3.75 t × $180 − $435 = **$240/ac** ($675); wheat 50 bu × $6.40
  − $215 = **$105/ac** ($320); sorghum 75 bu × $3.80 − $206 = **$79/ac** ($285); sunflower
  1,500 lb × $25.25/cwt − $330 = **$49/ac** ($379). Cotton has no crop file (reopened
  2026-11-23 as a commodity candidate), so it has no baseline.
- **Uplift** = the processing LLC's margin per acre processed. That is product revenue minus
  variable cost minus FAMILY labor ($11/hr loaded) minus the fixed-cost share minus the raw
  crop bought from the trust farm at market value. **net_per_acre_equiv** = raw net + uplift,
  meaning farm and LLC together. This reading satisfies both formulas in
  `data/reference/finished-products-schema.md`.
- Fixed share = fixed cost per year ÷ stage-1 acres processed (the smaller of the market
  ceiling and the stage-1 plan). Capital is stage 1 only. Everything is in USD in 2026.
- **Hired-labor check (labor-rates rule 3):** replacing FAMILY with HIRED_SEASONAL costs
  $7/hr more, so subtract 7 × (labor hours per acre) from the uplift. The tables show the result.
- Default structure: a separate LLC off trust land buys at documented market prices
  (`family-trust-constraints.md`). Capital therefore includes rented or licensed space.
- Already covered elsewhere and **not redone**: wheat flour, sorghum flour and popped
  sorghum, sorghum syrup, sunflower oil and birdseed mixes, spirits, produce (including
  popcorn and pumpkins), cut flowers (including ornamental sunflowers), and mushrooms.
  Cornmeal, grits and wheat berries are listed as **stage-2 line items in the wheat-flour
  plan** without unit economics. They are costed below so that plan can use the figures.

## 5. Finished products

### Corn (raw: 110 bu = 6,160 lb/ac; $4.35/bu; net $94/ac)

**Stone-ground cornmeal and grits.** Process: clean and sort food-grade corn, check
aflatoxin and fumonisin on each lot, grind on dedicated stones (a 12-in Meadows mill, $7,440–7,600
per the wheat-flour assumptions), sift into meal and grits, and bag in 2 lb and 25 lb sizes.
Yield is about 85 % (5,236 lb/ac). Price: retail stone-ground runs $2.60–7.60/lb at small
mills (Homestead Gristmill $4.35/$7.60, Cotton Hills $2.60, Ancient Wheatcraft $2.00) and
$0.52–3.20/lb in 25 lb bags (Weisenberger $0.52, War Eagle organic $3.20). Model blend:
**$1.75/lb**, mixing farm-market retail at ~$2.50 with chef 25 lb bags at ~$1.25 (verify).
Variable cost $0.35/lb (bags, tests, power). Labor 63 hr/ac, which is $693. Capital is $15k
standalone (mill, cleaner, sealer, scale) or ~$7.5k as the wheat-flour stage-2 mill. Uplift
**~$4,660/ac**, but the market reaches only ~2 acres (5–10k lb/yr Wichita specialty
cornmeal, **verify**). Regulatory: the K.S.A. 65-689(d)(4) exemption covers shelf-stable
sales direct to consumers. Wholesale needs a KDA food processing plant license (≤$200/yr
under 5,000 sq ft plus an application ≤$350). FDA facility registration and the FSMA PCHF
qualified-facility rules apply. Competitors: national brands at $0.40–0.70/lb, Weisenberger
and War Eagle online. No Kansas single-farm stone-ground cornmeal was found (verify).
**Verdict: test, as a line inside the wheat-flour mill stage 2. A standalone corn mill is
not justified.**

**Food-grade white corn to tortillerias.** Wichita has about 76,000 Hispanic residents,
mostly of Mexican origin (Census QuickFacts 2025; Neilsberg). Many tortillerias run on Maseca
masa harina, but those that cook their own nixtamal need food-grade white corn. That means
hard endosperm, few stress cracks, no high-heat drying, and low mycotoxin. Benchmark: KC
truck white corn $4.70/bu and St Joseph $4.60 on 2026-11-05, against a Wichita yellow cash
price near $4.35. Model: **$5.20/bu delivered in small lots** (verify), $0.25/bu for
segregation and totes, and no capital. The white-hybrid yield penalty is not yet in the
numbers (verify 0–10 %). Uplift ~$44/ac. One mid-size tortilleria might use ~6,500 bu a
year, about 60 acres (verify). **Verdict: test.** This winter, list the Wichita tortillerias
that nixtamalize in-house and ask for their spec, volume and price. No Wichita tortilleria
names were found in this search. That is a gap.

**Fresh masa and nixtamal tortillas (own production).** Process: cook corn in lime, steep
overnight, wash, grind on stone (grinder $2,939–11,436), then press and bake (corn line
$12,350 for ~1,440 tortillas/hr, or a head at $6,089–6,549). Masa is a perishable
time/temperature-controlled (TCS) food. Yield is about 1.4 lb tortillas per lb of corn
(verify), or ~8,600 lb/ac. Price: Wichita tortillas compete at commodity levels. Artisanal
nixtamal tortillas carry ~$1.25/lb over conventional (La Milpa de Rosa, NY). Model: $3.00/lb
wholesale to restaurants (verify). Capital ~$75k, including commissary build-out. Fixed cost
~$24k/yr. Uplift ~$11,600/ac on paper, but the 344 hr/ac means ~1,700 hr/yr at 5 acres of
daily production. The cooking water (nejayote) also needs wastewater handling. **Verdict:
skip. This is a full-time food job and no family member has one free (farm-profile.md).**
Revisit only if someone wants that job, or if a tortilleria wants a co-branded single-farm
tortilla made in its own plant.

**Heirloom and blue corn (open-pollinated, e.g., Blue Cónico or Hopi-type blue).** Dryland
yield ~40–60 bu (verify), so 2,600 lb sold at 50 bu. Price: Masienda wholesale Blue Cónico
$1.75/lb (55 lb), San Jose Farms $2.40/lb (50 lb, stale page), blue masa harina $6.36/lb
retail, blue grits $10.67/lb retail. Model: **$1.50/lb** whole kernel or meal to chefs and
masa makers (verify). Uplift ~$2,260/ac on ≤2 acres. It must be isolated from the farm's
yellow and white corn, at 660 ft or more or by planting date, because cross-pollination
contaminates both crops (verify). It fits the chef-direct storables channel already planned
(popcorn sits there). **Verdict: test 1 acre once chef accounts exist.**

**Corn for local feed mills.** The Halstead FCE feed mill (28 mi NW) and the Two Rivers Ark
City mill (55 mi S) bid at or near the elevator price (local-elevators-and-basis.md). This is
a marketing outlet, not a product: ~$0.05/bu (verify). **Verdict: pursue as a bid to
compare at every sale. No capital.**

**Deer corn (40 lb bags).** Process: screen whole corn (remove fines and broken kernels),
test aflatoxin, bag with a gravity bagger and scale, sew, and add a registered label.
Retail price at Tractor Supply in Nov 2026 was $8.99–12.99 per 40 lb (Big Tine $8.99,
treeline $10.99–11.99, Record Rack $12.99). Model: **$7.50/bag** blended across feed-store
wholesale and farm-direct (verify). Variable cost $0.80/bag (bag, thread, label, screenings
loss), 146 bags/ac, 5 hr/ac. Capital ~$10k (used screener, bagging scale, sewer) for a line
shared with milo and the wildlife blend. Fixed cost $1,500/yr. Uplift **~$370/ac**. The
market ceiling is ~30 acres (~4,400 bags/yr through 2–3 feed stores and direct sales,
verify). Regulatory: a Kansas commercial feeding stuffs license ($10/yr), a label with
guaranteed analysis, the $0.09/ton tonnage fee (minimum $15, semi-annual), FDA registration
and the PCAF qualified-facility rule. Check that Kansas deer-baiting rules allow feeding on
private land (KDWP, verify). Competitors: Tractor Supply's house brands and national brands,
Atwoods, co-op stores. Sales are seasonal (Sep–Jan). **Verdict: test with 500 bags in fall
2027.** Bagging the farm's own grain is close to agricultural handling, but sales and the
label belong in the LLC.

### Soybeans (raw: 36 bu = 2,160 lb/ac; $11.50/bu; net $159/ac)

**Non-GMO identity-preserved and food-grade (tofu) contracts.** The 2025 premium averaged
~$2.53/bu across food uses. Tofu takes 41 % of non-GM food-bean use. Contracted share was
67 % in 2025. A $4.50/bu 2026 IP program found is Canadian and is not a Kansas benchmark.
Buyers that list Kansas among sourcing states are SK Food and Clarkson; Scoular runs IP
programs. Delivery points are far (IA, MN, ND, IL). Costs against the premium: conventional
weed control +$25/ac (Palmer amaranth and kochia make this the real risk), freight ~$0.75/bu
(verify), and yield drag ~2 bu (verify). Net uplift is **~$4/ac**, so it is break-even.
**Verdict: test only if a buyer offers a Kansas or Nebraska delivery point and a premium of
at least $3.50/bu.**

**Edamame.** This is a vegetable crop, not grain. Benchmarks: WSU 5,000–6,000 lb pods/ac;
Virginia direct ~$2.39/lb and Richmond $3 wholesale/$4 direct (2011); Kentucky processing
$0.37/lb with returns of −$2,000 to +$2,200/ac. Hand harvest is the labor driver; mechanical
harvest saves >$1,100/ac. Model: 3,000 lb sold at $3.00/lb to chefs and markets, 200 hr/ac,
uplift ~$4,000/ac on ≤0.5 ac. It needs irrigation like the produce plots. There are no Kansas
data. **Verdict: test 0.1–0.25 ac inside the produce plan (`knowledge/crops/produce/`), not
as a soybean product.**

**Roasted soy nuts.** Process: soak, roast, salt, pack. Bulk 30 lb cases sell at
$4.53–6.99/lb and retail at $7–9/lb. Model: $4.50/lb, 1,850 lb/ac, $25k capital, $6k/yr
fixed for a licensed room. The market (~1 acre) cannot carry the fixed cost, so uplift is
**−$174/ac**. Soy is a major allergen, and it would cross-contact the gluten-free sorghum
room. **Verdict: skip.**

**Tofu and tempeh (honest assessment).** Model: about 2.2 lb tofu per lb of beans (verify),
$4.00/lb wholesale, 240 hr/ac. It needs a cooker, grinder, presses, walk-in cooler, and
licensed space with wastewater for whey and okara, ~$90k in all. Fixed cost is ~$30k/yr
against a Wichita artisan market of ~1–2 acres of beans (verify). National brands sit at
Dillons and Walmart, and Asian grocers stock imported brands. Tofu is perishable, needs daily
production, and is a soy-allergen food. Tempeh adds dehulling and culture incubation. Uplift
**−$7,700/ac**. **Verdict: skip.** It would make sense only as a supply contract to an
existing tofu maker, which is the IP row above.

**Extruded full-fat soybeans for local livestock.** A used Insta-Pro JR 600 costs $20,500 and
a refurbished Insta-Pro 2000 $27,000. The minimum is ~400 kg/hr. The product is ~36 % CP and
~18 % fat, for hogs, poultry, show stock and dairy. No published price was found. The model
assumes **$14 per 50 lb bag ($560/t; verify with dealers)**. With ~$7.5k/yr ownership and
rent spread over 30 acres (~30 t), uplift is **−$172/ac**. Quality control matters: urease
activity and moisture (Insta-Pro guidance). **Verdict: skip, unless one livestock buyer
commits to at least 100 t/yr. Then recost as bulk custom extrusion.**

### Alfalfa and hay (raw: 3.75 t/ac; $180/t blended farm gate; net $240/ac)
The links to `alfalfa-and-forage.md` and the produce files apply here. Every row assumes
alfalfa is in the crop plan, which it is not yet (farm-profile.md).

**Small squares for the horse market.** Kansas Hay Report 2026-11-19: small-square horse hay
~$15/bale, one entry $18–20. Earlier 2026 reports ran $14.50–18. At ~60 lb a bale (verify)
that is roughly $500–600/t, against $180 for rounds. Only clean, weed-free, beetle-free
cuttings qualify, usually the 2nd and 4th, which is 60 % of tonnage in the model. Blister
beetles in summer cuttings are a liability with horses (alfalfa-and-forage.md). Model:
125 bales/ac at a blended $12 (60 % at $15, 40 % at ~$8), $1.75/bale for twine, fuel and
repairs, and 10 hr/ac to stack and load customers. Capital $40k (used small-square baler,
accumulator and grapple, barn access). Fixed $11k/yr over 30 acres, with a $60/ac credit for
round-bale custom costs avoided. Uplift **~$190/ac** (only ~$120 with hired labor). Barn
storage is required. Competitors: many local small-square sellers on Facebook Marketplace
and Craigslist, and feed stores. **Verdict: test one cutting on ~10 acres through
custom-hired small-square baling before buying a baler.** The baler can be shared with straw.

**Alfalfa cubes and pellets.** Retail: Standlee 40 lb pellets $19.99–21.49, Standlee 40 lb
cubes $20.99, Greenway 50 lb cubes $22.99–24.99. An owned line (grinder, conditioner,
pellet die, cooler) costs $40–80k. Small flat-die mills handle alfalfa fiber poorly. The test
route is toll pelleting: the Kansas Custom Rates 2026 grind, mix and pellet rate averages
$2.23/cwt ($44.60/t). Model: 178 bags/ac at $13 wholesale to feed stores (retail minus ~35 %,
verify), $1.69/bag for toll fees and bags, $5k capital, $2k/yr fixed. Uplift **~$1,150/ac**
on ≤20 ac. This is the best per-acre figure at scale on this list. It is unproven until a
mill confirms it will pellet straight alfalfa, and until a feed store gives shelf space
against Standlee. **Verdict: test by calling toll mills and 3 Wichita feed stores.**

**Hay for rabbits and small pets (retail packs).** Retail runs ~$2.84–4.50/lb: Oxbow 9 lb box
~$2.84/lb, Small Pet Select $3.23–3.50/lb, farm-direct 5 lb box $4.15/lb, 20 lb $4.50/lb.
Alfalfa suits young or growing animals only; adults eat grass hay (timothy and orchard
grass), so alfalfa is a minority SKU. Model: 4,500 lb of leafy hay per acre, net $2.00/lb
after channel and shipping, $0.35/lb for boxes and labels, 90 hr/ac for hand sorting and
boxing. Uplift ~$3,530/ac on ≤1 acre. Kansas feed law: packages ≤1 lb pay $15 per product a
year in lieu of tonnage. **Verdict: test small (local pet stores, rabbit breeders, 4-H)
only if alfalfa is grown.** Weigh a grass hay before any packaging spend.

**Straw for bedding and decor:** see wheat straw below.

### Wheat (raw: 50 bu = 3,000 lb/ac; $6.40/bu; net $105/ac)

**Wheat straw small squares (landscaping, erosion control, fall decor).** This is a co-product:
the grain is still sold. Baled ~1.2 t/ac, ~60 bales of 40 lb (verify). Prices:
farm-direct $5.00–5.71/bale (Allhay; Livestock Market $120 per 21-bale bundle), decor retail
$23–29 (Walmart), Wichita garden-centre price not found. Model: **$7/bale** blended
(pumpkin patches, garden centres, landscapers and farm-direct in Sep–Oct). The $0.97/bale
cost includes twine, fuel and the nutrients removed (~$18/t, verify). Labor 3 hr/ac. Capital
$12k (a used small-square baler, shared with hay). Uplift **~$254/ac** on ~40 ac. Hidden
costs: residue and soil-moisture loss in no-till (wheat-hard-red-winter.md), and baling
right after harvest **competes with double-crop soybean planting**. Erosion jobs on public
land often need KDA weed-free forage certification. The eight Wichita pumpkin patches are
also buyers. **Verdict: test on ~20 ac of wheat stubble not going to double-crop.**

**Wheat berries for sprouting, microgreens and home milling.** These are listed in the
wheat-flour stage 2 and costed here. Prices for 25 lb: IFS $1.00/lb conventional; Country Life
organic $2.08; Palouse glyphosate-residue-free $2.32–2.84. Model: $1.50/lb blended across
25 lb and 5 lb bags. Clean, test DON and germination, and for sprouting seed test for
pathogens following FDA guidance on seeds for sprouting. Uplift ~$2,500/ac on ≤2 ac.
**Verdict: test now. It is the cheapest stage-0 market test for the wheat-flour vertical**:
sell berries before buying a mill.

**Wheatgrass.** Fresh trays sell to juice bars, and seed sells to growers. It was not
costed because the search budget ran out. Indoor tray growing is a daily-labor business like
the shelved mushrooms. **Verdict: skip.** At most, sell cleaned berries as wheatgrass seed
under the berries row.

**Wheat weaving and decorative bundles.** Wholesale $4.10–4.35 per ~8 oz, 28–32 in bundle
(Branches Wholesale); retail $18 per 1 lb bundle (DriedDecor). Tall or bearded varieties are
hand-cut at late dough, bunched and dried. Model: ~7,000 bundles per acre-equivalent at
$4.50 and 40 bundles/hr (verify). Uplift is ~$22,900 per acre-equivalent, but the market
(florists, weddings, fall decor) takes perhaps 1,000–1,500 bundles a year (~0.2 ac, ~$4,600/yr).
**Verdict: test a 0.1 ac strip as an add-on to the cut-flower plan** (`cut-flowers.md`
already sells dried wreaths). Hand cutting falls in wheat-harvest weeks.

**Feed wheat.** This is a salvage outlet for low-test-weight or sprouted lots to the Halstead
and Ark City feed mills or to feedyards. Uplift ~$0. **Verdict: pursue as a fallback
outlet.**

### Grain sorghum (raw: 75 bu = 4,200 lb/ac; $3.80/bu; net $79/ac)

**Whole-grain sorghum for pet-food makers.** Sorghum is now in the top 30 US pet-food
ingredients. The Checkoff lists 15 companies and 130–150 products. Kansas pet-food plants
include Hill's at Topeka and Emporia; whether they buy sorghum needs checking. Buyers value
consistent quality and reliable supply, which suits bulk contract supply rather than bagging.
Premium assumed $0.25/bu (verify), uplift ~$4/ac. It shares the white, low-tannin,
food-grade spec with the sorghum-flour vertical. **Verdict: test with calls. No capital.**

**Milo bird and wildlife feed bags (50 lb).** These are straight-milo bags. Birdseed mixes are
covered in the sunflower vertical. Prices: feed-grade $12.95–17.99, bird-grade $24.44 (online).
Model: 80 bags/ac at $9 wholesale or direct, on the deer-corn line. Uplift ~$288/ac on ≤10 ac.
Regulatory: same as deer corn. **Verdict: test as a second SKU on the deer-corn line.**

### Sunflower (raw: 1,500 lb/ac; $25.25/cwt; net $49/ac)

**Roasted in-shell confection snacks.** These need confection hybrids at ~1,300 lb/ac
(verify). Only large sizes go in-shell: ~55 %, or 715 lb. The rest goes to birdseed. Raw
confection value is $0.376/lb (RMA 2026 projected). Prices: distributor in-shell roasted
$2.13–2.49/lb (Nutstop 11 lb case), retail $8.99/lb, global bulk $0.22–1.07/lb. National
brands own the shelf. Model: $3.00/lb (blended $3.17 including the birdseed fraction),
$30k capital (sizer, batch roaster, bagger), $5k/yr fixed, uplift **−$513/ac**. It turns
positive only as an SKU in the sorghum popped room once fixed costs are shared (~+$650/ac,
≤3 ac). **Verdict: skip standalone.**

**Sunflower butter.** SunButter 16 oz sells for $7.16–9.29 retail; reseller cases run
$54–80 for 6. Its market is the allergen-free (school-lunch) claim. That needs a dedicated
top-9-free room, impossible beside wheat flour, soy or sesame. It also needs a dehuller, the
missing small-scale step. Kernel recovery is ~50 % (verify). Uplift **−$602/ac**. **Verdict:
skip.**

**Ornamental sunflowers** are covered in `knowledge/crops/produce/cut-flowers.md` (reference
only).

### Cotton (reopened 2026-11-23 as a commodity candidate; no crop file yet)
- **Cottonseed for dairy:** NASS price received was Kansas $226/t (2025 MYA) and Texas $234/t
  (Aug 2026). The gin normally keeps and markets the seed and credits the grower, so there is
  no farm-level processing step. Southwest Kansas dairies are the end market. **Verdict:
  skip as a product.** Settle it inside the gin contract.
- **Gin trash compost:** the gin owns the trash. A soil amendment sold for money needs KDA

- **Handcrafts (bolls, wreaths):** these belong to cut flowers or decor if anything. **Skip.**

### Cross-crop
**On-farm feed mill and custom blends.** An owned hammer mill, mixer and bagger cost ~$50k
(no current dealer prices found; verify). Fixed costs, including a nutritionist and
insurance, run ~$16.5k/yr. Model: 176 bags of 50 lb per corn acre-equivalent (70 % corn), at
$14/bag (verify), with $4.55/bag of bought ingredients and bags. Uplift ~$393/ac across 25
acres. Kansas 2026 custom rates are low (grind + mix $0.86/cwt, or $17.20/t), so co-op toll
milling is cheaper than owning. Every formula label must be registered. The FSMA PCAF
current good manufacturing practices (CGMPs) apply. Avoid medicated feeds (VFD).
Competitors: the Halstead and Ark City co-op mills, Tractor Supply and other brand feeds.
**Verdict: skip an owned mill. Test demand by toll-milling one show-feed or poultry ration.**

**Deer and wildlife feed (multi-grain blend: corn, milo, soybeans, sunflower).** It runs on
the deer-corn line at $8.50 per 40 lb bag wholesale (between plain and flavored corn
retail). Uplift ~$476/ac on ~20 ac. It is the best mechanical, low-labor product here.
**Verdict: test with deer corn and milo as one three-SKU line ($10k shared capital,
~$23k/yr uplift at the combined ceiling, verify).**

**Agritourism (corn maze, harvest tours).** The Wichita market is crowded: Applejack ($13–14),
Walters' ($16.95–20.95), Klausmeyer ($9–12; maze $10), Mr. Gourdman's ($10), Pumpkin Pants

visitors at $12 (verify), Rutgers design cost ($1,500–1,900), ~660 hr of weekend staffing,

staffing collides with corn, sorghum and soybean harvest and wheat planting. Public access
on trust land raises liability, zoning and ag-use questions. The trust checklist requires an
LLC lease of the plot, Kansas Agritourism Promotion Act registration with prescribed warning
signs (which reduce liability but are not a full shield), and $1M+ general liability
coverage (verify). **Verdict: skip.** Harvest tours are possible only as a free
relationship-building event under the LLC's insurance.

**Farm-store retail combining products.** This is the retail face already planned in the

approval, zoning and a KDA retail food establishment license for any licensed foods.
**Verdict: defer.** Sell deer corn, wildlife bags, straw, berries and cornmeal from the

## 6. Risks and disqualifiers
- **Market ceiling, not margin, binds the high-uplift items.** Wheat bundles, cornmeal,
  berries, heirloom corn and pet hay each reach only 0.2–2 acres.
- **Daily perishable production** (masa, tortillas, tofu) means a full-time job, and no
  family labor is assigned (farm-profile.md 2026-11-16).
- **Harvest-season labor collisions:** straw baling against double-crop soybean planting;
  wheat bundles during wheat harvest; the corn maze in October against fall harvest.
- **Allergen cross-contact:** soy and sunflower butter cannot share the gluten-free sorghum
  room under an allergen-free claim.
- **Mycotoxin:** deer corn and wildlife feed need an aflatoxin result on every lot. A bad
  year can remove the product entirely.
- **Brand competition at shelf:** Standlee (pellets and cubes), Oxbow and Small Pet Select
  (pet hay), Tractor Supply house brands (deer corn), and David's or Bigs (sunflower) set the
  price ceilings.
- **Trust:** bagging, retail, public access and any building belong in the LLC off trust land
  (`family-trust-constraints.md`). The corn maze is the item most exposed to liability.

## 7. Decision rules
1. Start the three-SKU feed line (deer corn, milo, wildlife blend) only after two Wichita-area
   feed stores give written orders of at least 300 bags each at $7.00 or more, and one
   aflatoxin result under 20 ppb is in hand (verify that threshold against KDA and FDA).
2. Pellets: proceed only if a toll mill confirms straight-alfalfa pelleting at $50/t or less
   and a feed store offers $12 a bag or more. Otherwise drop.
3. Straw: bale only fields not planned for double-crop. Stop if a Sep–Oct sell-through test of
   300 bales fails to clear $6 a bale.
4. Wheat berries and cornmeal run inside the wheat-flour stage-0 and stage-2 gates. Add no
   separate capital.
5. Masa and tortillas: reopen only if a named person commits to at least 1,500 hr/yr of food
   production, or a tortilleria offers a white-corn supply contract for at least 2,000 bu/yr
   (the supply contract is the preferred route).
6. Never site public-access agritourism on trust land without the trust checklist, trustee
   approval and counsel's written view on §2032A and ag-use valuation.
