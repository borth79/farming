# Hops (fit check)

## 1. Fit for this farm
**Verdict: skip. Wichita summers (frequent 100 °F+ days, warm humid nights) cut cone
quality and yield. The national market is oversupplied at $5.38/lb. An 18-ft trellis is
a 15–20 year trust-land improvement. At most, a 0.1-acre fresh-hop partnership with one
Wichita brewery, if a brewer asks.**

- **Climate.** Hops want long summer days, moderate heat and dry air: 45–50° N in the
  Pacific Northwest. Wichita at 37.7° N has shorter peak day length (lower yields, early
  flowering) and sustained heat above 100 °F, which degrades cones (lower alpha acids,
  browning). Humid nights favor downy and powdery mildew. Eastern Kansas has small
  yards, but no evidence of commercially competitive Kansas yields was found.
- **Water.** Hops use ~24–30 in of water in season (**verify**). Drip is mandatory, and
  irrigation status is undetermined.
- **Wind and hail.** South-central Kansas winds stress 18-ft trellises and strip bines.
- **Equipment.** Tractor and sprayer help (the grain sprayer cannot reach 18 ft
  canopies). Needs a trellis (poles, cable, anchors), a stringing lift, a stationary
  harvester (Wolf, Hopster, or similar; **verify** price), an oast or dryer, a baler or
  vacuum sealer, freezer storage, and custom pelletizing. The combine has no role.
- **Labor.** Spring stringing and training (training clockwise, 2–3 passes), and
  harvest in mid-August to September, which overlaps nothing on a grain farm but
  happens in heat. ~150+ hr/ac (estimate). The farm has no dedicated labor.
- **Perennial and trust.** **The crowns live 10–20 years and the trellis 15–20 years.**
  The poles and anchors are a permanent structure on trust land and need written trustee
  approval, a lease term covering payback (base year ~10, adverse never) and a removal
  clause. The drying and pelletizing building, if built, belongs to the LLC off trust
  land.

## 2. Agronomy summary
- **Varieties.** Unknown for Kansas. Cascade, Chinook, Nugget, Centennial and Zeus are
  the Midwest standards. Newer proprietary aroma varieties (Citra, Mosaic) are not
  licensed to small growers. Use virus- and viroid-tested plants.
- **Establishment.** ~900–1,000 plants/ac on 18-ft trellis, 3.5 × 14 ft (**verify**).
  Drip. Year 1 is a baby crop, years 2–3 reach full production.
- **Pests.** Downy mildew (the key disease in humid climates), powdery mildew,
  two-spotted spider mites in heat, hop aphid, Japanese beetle.
- **Harvest and processing.** Cut bines at 20–25 % dry matter, strip cones, dry to
  8–10 % moisture within hours, cool, bale or vacuum-pack, cold-store. Lab-test alpha,
  beta and oil content. Pelletize (T90) through a custom pelletizer.

## 3. Yield and raw-crop economics
Basis: 1 acre, raw = dried whole cone sold direct to Wichita brewers. **Establishment
$10,000/ac** (estimate, top of the general $6,000–10,000 range because of Kansas wind
anchoring). Mature costs $2,800/ac/yr (string, training, sprays, irrigation, lab tests)
plus $2.75/lb for harvest, drying and packing (hired labor $18/hr, energy; estimate).
Yield 800 lb/ac mature (estimate: Pennsylvania 1,100–1,500 lb less heat and day-length
penalties; Midwest Farm Report heat penalty 10–20 %; **verify**). Price $8/lb (low end of
$8–20 local craft range). Amortized at 6 % over 12 years ($1,193/ac/yr).

**Payback: year 10 (base), never in the adverse case.** The harvester and dryer are
excluded. Adding even a $20,000 used harvester and dryer for a 1-acre yard would push
payback past the crowns' life.

**Versus dryland sorghum.** Sorghum nets ~$80/ac over direct costs (county trend) and
$295/ac at the K-State base (`grain-sorghum.md`). Base hops ($207) is about equal to
sorghum, with far more capital, labor and risk. **Hops do not beat sorghum on a
risk-adjusted basis.**

## 4. Raw-crop market
- **National.** US 2025 production was 83.1 million lb at an average of $5.38/lb.
  Acreage fell 16 % in 2024 and 7 % in 2025. BarthHaas calls the market structurally
  oversupplied. There is "not really a spot market" for surplus. Big brewers contract
  Pacific Northwest supply years ahead.
- **Local.** Wichita has 10+ craft breweries (`spirits-and-beverages/business-plan.md`).
  Craft brewers buy pellets with lab analysis from merchants (YCH, Hopsteiner, Yakima
  Chief, BSG). Local whole-cone hops are a novelty, mainly for one or two harvest
  fresh-hop beers a year. A brewery uses ~1–2 lb of pellets per barrel (**verify**), so
  the local market for Kansas hops is a few hundred pounds a year at most.
- **Midwest growers.** Many small Midwest yards from the 2010s boom have closed (not
  documented in this pass; **verify** with Great Lakes Hop Working Group and Kansas
  growers).

## 6. Risks and disqualifiers
1. **Climate (top disqualifier).** Heat degrades cone quality, short day length limits
   yield, and humidity drives downy mildew.
2. **Market.** National oversupply; local demand of a few hundred pounds; brewers want
   lab-tested pellets of proprietary varieties.
3. **Trust.** An 18-ft pole trellis is a long-lived structure on trust land. It needs
   trustee approval and a lease covering payback (~10 years base).
4. **Capital.** Harvester, dryer and cold storage are uneconomic below ~5–10 acres
   (**estimate**).
5. Wind and hail damage to the trellis.

## 7. Decision rules
1. Do not plant hops commercially.
2. Revisit only if (a) K-State or a Kansas grower publishes 3+ years of competitive
   yields and alpha-acid data, and (b) a Kansas brewer or maltster contracts ≥ 1,000 lb/yr
   at ≥ $10/lb.
3. A 0.1-acre fresh-hop collaboration is acceptable only on a non-trust or
   trustee-approved site with removable poles.
