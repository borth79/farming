# Gourds and ornamental corn

> Research note (2026-11-23): this is fall décor produce, closer to the pumpkin file than to
> grain. Gourd and ornamental corn yields, Wichita wholesale prices and hand-labor hours are
> estimates **(verify)**. The CSV carries two crops from this file: `ornamental gourds` and
> `ornamental corn`, each with its own raw row.

Benchmark: dryland grain sorghum ≈ **$79/ac** return over direct costs. Any acre here is
a garden-scale acre: the market, not the land, is the limit (1–2 acres each).

## 1. Fit for this farm
- **Climate**: both grow well in south-central Kansas summers (pumpkin trials at Haysville,
  Sedgwick County). Gourds are cucurbits — squash bug, cucumber beetle (bacterial wilt),
  powdery mildew; drip irrigation is strongly preferred, and dryland gourds are a gamble in a
  dry August. Irrigation is undetermined on this farm; a produce plot with a water source is
  assumed (see the produce files).
- **Ornamental corn**: grown like field corn on the farm's planter, but it must be isolated
  from neighbors' yellow field corn, sweet corn and popcorn so foreign pollen does not change
  kernel colors (xenia) — by distance or planting date (verify distances). Sorghum-surrounded
  fields help. Raccoons, deer, birds and ear molds in a wet fall cut marketable ears.
- **Equipment**: planter and sprayer usable; harvest is entirely by hand (ears picked with
  husks attached and pulled back; gourds cut with stems); the grain combine is not used.
  Needs a dry, ventilated curing space (barn or shed), wash station for gourds, bins and
  pallets. A corn binder would help stalk bundles (not owned).
- **Herbicide carryover**: atrazine and other sorghum residuals injure cucurbits; place gourds
  only on fields with clean history (pumpkin file applies). Ornamental corn tolerates the
  sorghum program.
- **Labor**: hand harvest and bunching ≈ 190 hr/ac ornamental corn and ≈ 100 hr/ac gourds
  (estimates) in September–October, colliding with grain harvest and wheat planting.
- **Trust**: growing and selling whole produce is agriculture; retail, a farm stand and
  agritourism raise the trust and liability questions in the pumpkin file; run sales through
  the LLC.

## 2. Agronomy summary
| Item | Gourds (small ornamental Cucurbita pepo; craft Lagenaria) | Ornamental corn |
|---|---|---|
| Types | mixed small ornamentals (warted, striped, spoon, pear); hard-shell Lagenaria (bottle, dipper, birdhouse) for crafts | Indian/flint multicolor, strawberry and mini types, broom-color husk types |
| Planting | late May–early June, direct seeded, 6–8 ft rows; plastic mulch and drip preferred | mid-May–early June, ~10 lb seed/ac (seller guide), 30-in rows |
| Season | 90–100 days small gourds; 120+ days Lagenaria (Kansas season is adequate, verify) | 100–115 days (verify) |
| Pests | squash bug, cucumber beetle, powdery mildew, fruit rot on wet soil | corn earworm, raccoons, deer, birds, ear molds |
| Harvest | hand cut with stems when rind hardens, before frost; wash in dilute bleach; cure 1–2 weeks | hand pick when husks dry; pull husks back; dry ears; bunch 3 ears |
| Storage | small gourds hold through the season; Lagenaria dry 3–6 months to craft-ready | dried ears keep 1+ year; protect from rodents |

## 3. Yield and raw-crop economics
### Ornamental gourds
| Case | Marketable lb/ac | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Adverse | 2,000 | dry August without drip, beetles |
| Base | 4,000 | ~2/3 marketable of MSU 3 t/ac average |
| Favorable | 7,000 | MSU 4–5 t/ac |

Raw sale = bulk bins to garden centers and pumpkin patches at $0.40/lb (verify; a $180 bin
at 800–1,000 lb ≈ $0.18–0.22/lb; terminal half-bushel cartons $17.50–19.50 at Atlanta ≈
$1.30–1.60/lb at the receiver). Base: $1,600 revenue − $800 non-labor cost (seed, fertilizer,
insecticide, fungicide, mulch; below the pumpkin wholesale $1,050–1,210) − 100 hr × $11 =
**≈ −$300/ac** (HIRED ≈ −$1,000). Bulk gourds lose money; only packaged and retail
channels pay.

### Ornamental corn
| Case | Marketable 3-ear bunches/ac | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Adverse | 1,500 | wildlife, mold, off-color ears |
| Base | 3,000 | ~18,000 plants, ~50% marketable ears (estimate, verify) |
| Favorable | 4,500 | |

Raw sale = bunches wholesale to garden centers and pumpkin patches at $1.50 (verify; the 2025
Philadelphia auction average was $2.66, range $1.75–3.25, on only 145 units). Base: $4,500 −
$350 non-labor − 190 hr × $11 = **≈ $2,060/ac** (HIRED ≈ $730). Far above sorghum per acre,
but the Wichita wholesale market may absorb only about one acre's output (verify).

## 4. Raw-crop market
- Buyers: Wichita-area garden centers and nurseries, independent grocers, pumpkin patches
  (Klausmeyer, Applejack, P&M, Cedar Creek, Meadowlark — `pumpkins-and-winter-squash.md`),
  florists, church and school fall events, Tractor Supply-type stores (corn stalks).
- Season: September 1 to October 31; unsold product after Halloween is worth little (corn
  ears hold to Thanksgiving décor).
- Competitors: the pumpkin patches' own gourds and corn; regional wholesale growers
  (Missouri, Ohio shellacked gourds); big-box décor.
- Volume ceiling: ≈ 2 acres gourds, ≈ 1 acre ornamental corn for a new seller (verify with
  five buyer calls).

## 5. Finished products
Default: the LLC buys gourds at $0.40/lb and corn at $1.50/bunch and packs and sells off
trust land (or at the farm stand under the produce plan's rules). Convention: uplift = LLC
margin; FAMILY $11 (HIRED $18 shown).

**(a) 3-lb mesh bags of mixed gourds to garden centers and grocers.** Washed, cured gourds in
branded mesh bags. 1,333 bags/ac at $4 to the store (verify), $0.25 bag and label, 53 hr,
capital $3,000 (wash and cure station, scale, bag closer), fixed $600/yr over 2 acres. Margin
≈ **$2,516/ac** (HIRED ≈ $2,145); market ≈ 1.5 acres. Regulatory: none for whole uncut
produce; retailers may ask for product liability insurance. Verdict: **test**.

**(b) Gourds retail at farmers markets and the farm stand.** Half the crop (4,000 gourds) at
$1.00 each (verify), selling time 50 hr, market fees $400 share. Margin ≈ **$2,170/ac**
(HIRED ≈ $1,820). Shares booths and hours with the pumpkin and produce lines. Verdict:
**test**.

**(c) Dried craft hard-shell gourds (Lagenaria).** Grown, field- or barn-dried 3–6 months,
cleaned, graded and sold to gourd artists, crafters and birdhouse makers, largely online.
1,500 marketable dried gourds/ac (lower end of Virginia Tech 2,000–5,000 after drying losses)
at $4 average (verify; no price found), 75 hr, capital $1,500 (drying racks), fixed $300/yr;
raw value set equal to the ornamental-gourd acre ($1,600) as the opportunity. Margin ≈
**$2,975/ac** (HIRED ≈ $2,450), market ≈ 1 acre. Verdict: **test** on 0.25 acre — price is the
biggest unknown.

**(d) Ornamental corn bunches retail at markets and the farm stand.** 1,500 bunches/ac sold at
$4 (retail benchmark $3 for a 3-ear bundle; verify Wichita), rest sold wholesale in the raw
line; 38 hr selling; fixed $400. Margin ≈ **$2,782/ac** (HIRED ≈ $2,516). Verdict: **test**.

**(e) Corn-stalk décor bundles.** After ear harvest, cut and tie 5–8 stalks; sold to garden
centers and stores at $3 (verify; Tractor Supply sells bundles of 5). 500 bundles/ac
(market-limited), $0.10 twine, 40 hr, no capital (hand cut). Margin ≈ **$1,010/ac**. Verdict:
**test** — by-product, uses stalks that are otherwise residue.

**(f) Not costed separately — fall-décor agritourism.** Gourds, ornamental corn, broomcorn
bundles and sweet sorghum syrup sold at the pumpkin-patch agritourism option already modeled
in `knowledge/crops/produce/pumpkins-and-winter-squash.md` (600 visitors × $10 example;
Kansas Agritourism Promotion Act; trustee consent). Treat these products as add-on sales
there rather than a second event.

## 6. Risks and disqualifiers
- **Hand labor in September–October**, the farm's busiest months, with no dedicated labor —
  the top disqualifier.
- Hard sales deadline (Halloween) and perishable demand; one wet October ruins sales days.
- Cucurbit pests and disease; dryland gourds fail in drought without drip.
- Xenia from neighbors' corn; wildlife damage to ornamental corn.
- Small, relationship-driven local market; pumpkin patches grow their own.

## 7. Decision rules
1. Grow only with the produce plot (water and pest program shared) and only after two
   garden-center buyers and one pumpkin patch confirm interest and price in writing.
2. Year 1: ≤ 0.5 ac gourds and ≤ 0.5 ac ornamental corn; record hours per acre and
   sell-through by October 31.
3. Expand only if sell-through ≥ 80% and FAMILY-rate net per labor hour ≥ $15.
4. Ornamental corn field ≥ the verified isolation distance from any neighbor's corn, or
   planted ≥ 2 weeks off their pollination date.
