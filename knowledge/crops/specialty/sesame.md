# Sesame

## 1. Fit for this farm
- **Climate and water:** sesame is a hot-season, drought-tolerant crop. South-central Kansas is
  at the northern edge of commercial production (Texas, Oklahoma, limited Kansas). It needs
  70 F soil to plant (OSU), so the window here is late May to late June, and it dries down
  after a killing freeze (Wichita average first freeze late October; verify), with harvest in
  November.
- **Soil:** well-drained loams; poor on crusting or waterlogged soils. Emergence is the weak point
  (small seed, shallow placement 0.75–1.5 in).
- **Rotation:** fits the summer slot where sorghum or soybeans would go, after wheat harvested the
  previous year. It is not a realistic double-crop after wheat here: a late-June planting meets
  frost before maturity in most years (verify with Sesaco maturity data). It gives a broadleaf,
  non-grass break for sorghum pests (no sugarcane aphid) and is not a host for most grass weeds'
  insects.
- **Herbicide carryover:** a serious constraint. Atrazine from the prior sorghum or corn crop is a
  known risk to sesame (sensitivity not confirmed in this research, **verify** against the Sesaco
  and OSU guides). Plan sesame only on fields with no atrazine the previous season, and check
  wheat herbicide plant-back intervals (sulfonylureas, e.g. Finesse-type products) before
  committing.
- **Equipment:** the grain combine works for shatter-resistant varieties (lower cylinder speed,
  seal leaks for a small seed). Grain drill or planter with depth bands, sprayer: owned. Nothing new
  needed for the raw crop beyond sealing ($1,500 allowance, estimate).
- **Trust and labor:** raw sesame is ordinary agriculture under contract; no trust issue. Labor is
  the same as sorghum, but harvest in November competes with sorghum harvest.
- **Commitment:** annual.

## 2. Agronomy summary
- **Varieties:** shatter-resistant (indehiscent or improved non-dehiscent) lines from Sesaco are the
  only practical genetics available in 2026. Sesaco seed is sold only with a Sesaco Marketing
  Agreement. Equinom has suspended U.S. operations until about 2027 (Trostle 2025), so it is not
  a 2026–2027 option. **Consequence:** the farm can't legally grow contract genetics to sell outside
  the contract. Any finished-product plan needs written permission from Sesaco or a non-contract
  seed source (**verify**).
- **Planting:** late May to late June at 70 F+ soil; 3–6 lb/ac (verify with Sesaco), drilled or 30 in
  rows; seed 0.5–0.75 in below the moisture line.
- **Fertility:** low; K-State notes low fertilizer need. Working assumption 40–60 lb N, P to soil
  test (estimate).
- **Weeds:** the main agronomic weakness. Burndown glyphosate + 2,4-D, Dual Magnum pre-emergence,
  and no post-emergence broadleaf options (OSU). Clean, no-till fields with low pigweed pressure
  only. Yield losses up to 65% without early weed control (Kansas extension, via search).
- **Pests and diseases:** relatively few in Oklahoma; whitefly late in the season in the south;
  charcoal rot in drought (verify).
- **Harvest:** after frost and field dry-down, direct combine. Buyer moisture spec and test methods
  are in the contract (**verify**). Clean, dry storage; sesame is light and flows like water, so
  seal bins and trucks.

- Price basis: $0.32/lb 2026 crop (NC State fact sheet, buyer not named), less an estimated

- Direct cost (ESTIMATE, verify): seed $20–30 at Sesaco prices; N/P $35; burndown + Dual $30; fuel
  and repairs $35; crop insurance by written agreement $10; hauling $13; interest and misc. $15.
- **Versus dryland sorghum on the same acre:** sorghum nets about **$79/ac over direct costs** (75
  bu × $3.80 Oct-2026 central Kansas cash − $206.48 K-State 2026 SC direct cost;
  `grain-sorghum.md`). Base sesame ($35) trails sorghum by ~$44/ac. Sesame wins only when sorghum
  is below ~$3.20/bu cash or the field yields 800+ lb of sesame.
- Insurance: Sedgwick County was listed for sesame coverage by written agreement (RMA PM-18-057).
  Ask an agent whether 2027 coverage is available and at what contract price.

## 4. Raw-crop market
- **Sesaco** (Austin TX; processing at Hobart OK) is the only confirmed contractor within reach. It
  has a production manager for northern Oklahoma and Kansas (Farm Flavor). It offers fixed-price
  and deferred-price acreage contracts, with seed sold only under the marketing agreement.
- **Equinom:** U.S. operations suspended to about 2027. It is not a buyer for 2027 planning.
- **Volume ceiling:** set by contract acres Sesaco offers. The farm cap is risk-based: no more than
  160 acres until two years of on-farm results.
- **Volatility:** price has declined for three years (NC State). World sesame trades on Sudan,
  Nigeria, India and Ethiopia supply, so prices move with African crops and Chinese demand.
- Spot market: none. Do not plant without a signed contract.

## 5. Finished products
Default structure: a separate LLC buys cleaned sesame from the trust farm at the documented
contract-equivalent price and processes it off trust land
(`knowledge/operations/family-trust-constraints.md`). Labor is at FAMILY $11/hr loaded, with
HIRED_SEASONAL $18/hr shown for sensitivity (`knowledge/finance/labor-rates.md`).

**Two blockers apply to every product:**

1. **Allergen.** Sesame is a major food allergen. Putting sesame through the screw press and room
   planned for sunflower oil turns that allergen-free room into an allergen facility. That would
   need allergen labeling, validated cleaning or a dedicated press, and precautionary statements
   on the sunflower oil. This contradicts the sunflower plan's "no other foods in the room"
   assumption.
2. **Seed rights.** Contract genetics are tied to delivery to Sesaco. Without a release, there is no
   farm sesame for the LLC to buy.

**Schema note:** `net_per_acre_equiv` already charges the raw crop at market value, and the
schema's `uplift` then subtracts `raw_net_per_acre` again. The LLC's real added margin over selling
raw is therefore `net_per_acre_equiv`; the schema figure is shown for consistency with the
cross-crop sheet.

### 5a. Cleaned natural (unhulled) sesame seed for bakeries
- **Product:** food-grade cleaned natural sesame in 25-lb bags for bagel shops, bun bakeries and
  pizzerias (topping seed).
- **Process and equipment:** air-screen cleaner (shared with the sunflower line), destoner or
  gravity table, optional optical sort, bagger, metal detection. Salmonella lot testing; a toll
  steam-pasteurization step if buyers require it (**verify** availability).
- **Capital:** stage 1 ~$30,000 (used gravity table and destoner, bagger, lab start-up).
- **Labor:** ~12 hr per acre-equivalent (cleaning 4, bagging 3, sales and delivery 5) = $132
  FAMILY, $216 HIRED.
- **Raw crop per unit:** 650 lb field-run → ~550 lb food grade (85%, estimate) per acre.
- **Price and channel:** $2.00/lb wholesale to bakeries (estimate). Foodservice hulled sesame lists
  at $70–110 per 25 lb ($2.80–4.40/lb), and natural seed usually trades below hulled.
- **Margin:** revenue $1,100; variable $165; net $368/ac-equiv (schema uplift $333; $249 at the
  hired rate).
- **Regulatory:** KDA food processing license; FDA registration and 21 CFR 117; allergen labeling;
  pathogen testing records.
- **Competitors and market size:** national foodservice distributors (Sysco, US Foods) carry cheap
  imported sesame. Wichita bakery demand is perhaps 10,000–15,000 lb/yr (estimate), or about
  20–25 acres.
- **Verdict: test**, only if Sesaco releases seed for identity-preserved food use, and starting
  with 2–3 bakery letters of intent.

### 5b. Toasted or cold-pressed sesame oil
- **Product:** 250 ml toasted sesame oil for Asian restaurants, specialty grocers and online sales.
- **Process:** roast (for toasted), screw press, settle, filter, bottle. Uses the sunflower-plan
  press line (KK40 class) plus a roaster and allergen changeover.
- **Capital:** ~$15,000 incremental (roaster, dedicated filter and tanks, allergen validation).
  About $70,000 if standalone, as in the sunflower plan's stage 2.
- **Labor:** ~35 hr/ac-equiv = $385 FAMILY.
- **Raw crop per unit:** 550 lb clean seed → ~220 lb oil (40% recovery, estimate) → ~440 × 250 ml.
  The ~330 lb of cake is credited as feed in the variable cost.
- **Price and channel:** $5.00 wholesale per 250 ml (retail $9–10). The benchmark is hard: Kadoya
  sells at $14.99 per 22.1 fl oz and $11.95 per 250 ml at Walmart (about $5.80 per 250 ml for the
  large bottle).
- **Margin:** net $230/ac-equiv (schema uplift $195). It turns negative (-$15) at the hired rate.
- **Regulatory:** KDA license; FDA label with a sesame allergen statement.
- **Market:** small. Perhaps 2,000 bottles/yr locally, or about 5 acres.
- **Verdict: test** only as an add-on SKU once the sunflower press line exists and an allergen plan
  is approved. Never ahead of sunflower oil.

### 5c. Tahini
- **Product:** stone-ground hulled sesame paste in 16 oz jars.
- **Process:** dehulling (wet or mechanical), roasting, stone or colloid milling, filling.
- **Capital:** ~$80,000. The hulling line is the uncertain item (**verify**; small hullers are
  rare).
- **Labor:** ~42 hr/ac-equiv = $468.
- **Yield:** 550 lb → ~440 lb hulled → ~425 jars.
- **Price:** $7.00 wholesale per jar (estimate). The premium brand Villa Jerada wholesales at
  $10.68/lb on Faire, while trade tahini is ~$3/lb (Tridge U.S. samples ~$6.6/kg, 2026).
- **Margin:** net $232/ac-equiv (schema $198); -$65 at the hired rate.
- **Competitors:** Soom, Seed + Mill and Al Arz, plus imported Ethiopian Humera seed tahini (the
  quality benchmark).
- **Verdict: skip.** It's capital-heavy for 10 acres, and hulling is unproven at small scale.

### 5d. Sesame brittle or snack bars
- **Product:** honey-sesame brittle in 8 oz bags.
- **Process:** a confectionery kitchen (kettle, slab, cutter, sealer). A licensed shared kitchen is
  possible.
- **Capital:** ~$25,000.
- **Labor:** ~122 hr/ac-equiv = $1,342.
- **Yield:** 550 lb seed → ~1,220 lb brittle (sesame ~45% of weight) → ~2,440 bags.
- **Price:** $5.00 wholesale (estimate, **verify**).
- **Margin:** net ~$3,235/ac-equiv on paper, but the market ceiling is ~2 acres (~5,000 bags/yr).
  The seed is under 2% of revenue.
- **Verdict: skip as a reason to grow sesame.** The value is in confectionery, not the crop. A
  candy maker would buy imported seed at $3/lb. Revisit only if the LLC already runs a kitchen.

## 6. Risks and disqualifiers
- **Raw margin below sorghum** at base ($35 vs $79/ac), with a three-year price decline.
- **Single buyer:** Sesaco is the only buyer; Equinom is out until about 2027.
- **Weeds:** no post-emergence broadleaf herbicide. Pigweed fields are disqualified.
- **Carryover:** atrazine and SU carryover from the core rotation. Field eligibility is narrow.
- **Season:** frost before maturity on late plantings; November harvest overlaps sorghum harvest.
- **Allergen:** sesame contaminates the sunflower oil facility plan.
- **Seed rights:** contract seed blocks the finished-product route.

## 7. Decision rules
1. Plant sesame only with a signed Sesaco contract whose delivered net price ≥ $0.37/lb at
   650 lb/ac, or ≥ $0.31/lb on a field with documented ≥ 800 lb/ac potential. Either one beats
   sorghum's ~$79/ac over direct.
2. Only on fields with no atrazine applied the prior season and clear wheat-herbicide plant-backs,
   and with low pigweed pressure.
3. First year, 20–40 acres maximum. Expand only if the yield is ≥ 650 lb and harvest finishes by
   Dec 1.
4. No sesame finished product until (a) Sesaco gives written permission to retain seed for LLC
   food use, and (b) an allergen plan for the shared facility is approved by the LLC's food-safety
   advisor.
