# Sweet sorghum (syrup)

> Research note (2026-11-23): no Kansas syrup yield trial, Kansas price list or labor
> study was found. Labor hours per gallon and equipment costs are estimates (**verify**
> with a working Kansas or Missouri syrup maker, e.g. the Heide family east of Wichita).

Benchmark: dryland grain sorghum ≈ **$79/ac** return over direct costs (`grain-sorghum.md`).

## 1. Fit for this farm
- **Climate and soil**: excellent. Sweet sorghum is the same species as grain sorghum;
  heat- and drought-tolerant; Kansas Orange was a Kansas Agricultural Experiment Station
  variety (1883) grown commercially in Kansas into the 1930s.
- **Rotation and herbicides**: fits where grain sorghum fits; same atrazine/S-metolachlor
  program with Concep-treated seed (verify labels for sweet sorghum); no new carryover.
- **Equipment**: the planter and sprayer work. **The grain combine is useless**: syrup
  needs whole stalks stripped of leaves, topped (seed heads off) and pressed within ~24–48
  hours of cutting (verify). Harvest is by hand (machete or corn knife) or a one-row corn
  binder; the farm owns neither a binder nor a mill. It lacks a roller mill (press),
  evaporator pan and firebox or steam setup, juice settling and filtering gear, and a
  bottling area.
- **Labor**: the binding constraint — a few weeks of intense hand work in
  September–October overlapping sorghum and soybean harvest and wheat planting. Even at
  1–2 acres it needs a crew of 3–6 on cooking days.
- **Trust**: growing cane is agriculture. Pressing and cooking is processing and goes to the
  LLC off trust land by default (haul stalks or juice). A stir-off with the public on trust
  land needs trustee consent and liability review.
- **Annual crop**; no perennial commitment.

**There is no raw market for sweet sorghum stalks** within reach of Sedgwick County. The raw
row in the CSV therefore transfers stalks to the LLC at field cost ($150/ac: seed,
fertilizer, herbicide, planting, fuel — estimate), so the farm's raw net is $0 and all
profit or loss appears in the products. Against grain sorghum on the same acre, sweet
sorghum gives up ≈ $79/ac in the base case; every product below must clear that.

## 4. Raw-crop market
- None for stalks or juice. The market is for syrup: Kansas syrup makers are few (Heide
  family east of Wichita; LocalHarvest lists Ross Farms and Circle's in McCune) — they are
  competitors, not buyers.
- Syrup price benchmarks (2025): quart $12 (Mazelin), $16 (Tipton-Haynes festival); pint
  $8–9; wholesale bulk $12–15/gal (NCAT, undated) to $40/gal (SARE, undated).
- Market size within reach: Wichita farmers markets, holiday markets, online; demand is
  heritage- and gift-driven. Assume 800–1,600 quarts/yr can sell in years 1–3 (≈ 2–4
  acres) — **verify** with a test season.

## 5. Finished products
Default: LLC buys cane at field cost and processes in leased or owned off-trust space with a
small mill and evaporator. Convention: uplift = LLC margin. Labor at FAMILY $11 (HIRED $18
shown).

**(a) Syrup in glass quarts and pints, direct to consumer.** 100 gal/ac = 400 quarts at
$14 (between Mazelin $12 and festival $16; pints at $8 are a similar $/oz). Variable
$2.25/quart (jar, lid, label $1.50; fuel to evaporate ~7 gal of water per gal syrup $0.75 —
verify); labor 100 hr/ac (≈1 hr per gallon field-to-jar, including hand harvest —
**verify**, NC State says "very labor intensive"); capital $15,000 (used roller mill,
evaporator pan with firebox, settling tanks, refractometer, thermometers, bottling — verify);
fixed $2,500/yr over 2 acres = $1,250/ac. LLC margin ≈ **$2,200/ac** (HIRED ≈ $1,500).
Annual at the market ceiling (2 ac) ≈ $4,400. Regulatory: direct-to-consumer
non-potentially-hazardous food needs no KDA license (verify with KDA/MF3138); label with
product name, maker, net contents, ingredients; Kansas sales tax. Verdict: **test** on
0.5–1 acre in year 1 with a borrowed or rented mill.

**(b) Bulk syrup in gallons to bakeries, distilleries and chefs.** 100 gal at $30/gal
(between the $12–15 and $40 benchmarks; verify with two Wichita bakeries and a distillery —
see `knowledge/verticals/spirits-and-beverages/`), $3/gal containers and fuel, 85 hr, capital
$25,000 (licensed kitchen fit-out share), fixed $3,000/yr over 3 acres. LLC margin ≈
**$615/ac** (HIRED ≈ $20). Needs a KDA food processing license and an inspected facility
(sales for resale), FSMA facility registration, product liability. Verdict: **test later**,
only once (a) proves yield and labor.

**(c) Stir-off harvest and boiling day (agritourism).** A fall event: visitors watch or help
strip cane, press and cook; tickets $10 (Wichita pumpkin-patch admissions $9–17), syrup sold
on site (counted in a). Per acre-equivalent: two events, 600 visitors, $2/visitor variable
(toilets, hand-wash, supplies), 80 hr, capital $5,000 (signage, parking, hand-wash
stations), fixed $2,000/yr (agritourism insurance rider, registration). Margin ≈
**$1,770/ac-equivalent** (HIRED ≈ $1,210), max ~1 acre. Regulatory: Kansas Agritourism
Promotion Act registration and warning signs; trustee consent if on trust land, or host at an
off-trust site; temporary food rules for samples. Competitor: the Heide family harvest
party east of Wichita. Verdict: **test** in year 2, combined with the pumpkin/fall-decor
agritourism option in `knowledge/crops/produce/pumpkins-and-winter-squash.md`.

**(d) Not costed — sorghum vinegar, sorghum candy and baked goods.** Vinegar needs
fermentation, acidity testing and a KDA license; candy and baked goods sell under the
cottage food rules direct to consumer but use little syrup per unit and add kitchen labor.
Verdict: **skip** for stage 1; revisit as uses for syrup that does not sell as jars.

## 6. Risks and disqualifiers
- **Labor at harvest** is the top disqualifier: the farm has no dedicated labor and
  harvest collides with the grain harvest and wheat planting.
- Processing skill: scorched or under-cooked syrup is unsaleable; learn from an experienced
  maker before buying equipment.
- Small market; competition from established Kansas, Missouri and Kentucky makers with
  online sales.
- Weather: lodging and frost before harvest; stalks must be pressed quickly.
- Food safety and liability move to the LLC; public events raise liability on whichever
  land hosts them.

## 7. Decision rules
1. Year 1: ≤ 1 acre, rented or borrowed mill and evaporator, record hours per gallon and
   gallons per acre.
2. Buy equipment only if year-1 hours are ≤ 1.2 per gallon and ≥ 70% of syrup sells by
   December 31 at ≥ $12/quart.
3. Never schedule cooking days in the same week as sorghum harvest or wheat planting on
   more than half the farm's acres.
4. Wholesale (b) only with a licensed kitchen already in use by another LLC product line.
