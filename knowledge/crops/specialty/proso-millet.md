# Proso millet

> Research note (2026-11-23): fetch blocked; figures from search summaries. Net per acre =
> gross revenue minus direct costs, excluding land and machinery ownership.

## 1. Fit for this farm
- **Climate.** Proso is a short-season (60–90 day), drought-tolerant warm-season grass
  grown mainly in northeast Colorado, western Nebraska, South Dakota and northwest Kansas.
  It will grow in Sedgwick County; its advantage there is the short season, not yield.
- **Rotation fit: double-crop after wheat.** The realistic slot is planted late June to
  early July into wheat stubble, harvested in September–October, competing with
  double-crop soybeans and sorghum (see `soybeans-double-crop.md`). As a full-season crop
  it cannot match sorghum.
- **Herbicide conflicts.** Very sensitive to atrazine carryover (do not follow sorghum or
  corn treated with atrazine; **verify** label intervals). Few in-crop grass herbicides;
  broadleaf control is 2,4-D/dicamba-type at specific stages. Volunteer proso and wild
  proso become weeds in following corn and sorghum.
- **Equipment.** Planter or drill works (small seed, shallow). The combine works with
  tightened seals and fine sieves; shattering favors swathing, which the farm cannot do
  without a swather (custom-hire, **verify**), or straight-combining at the right moisture.
  Bird damage and shatter are the harvest risks.
- **Trust and labor.** Growing is agriculture; bagging birdseed is LLC processing (it fits
  the birdseed vertical). Low labor.
- **Commitment.** Annual.

## 2. Agronomy summary
- Varieties: white proso (birdseed standard) such as Huntsman, Earlybird, Plateau (waxy)
  (**verify** current availability).
- Planting: soil above 65 °F; 15–20 lb/ac drilled, 0.5–1 in deep (seed $0.55/lb, UNL 2025).
- Fertility: 20–40 lb N/ac double-crop; residual P usually enough.
- Pests: few insects; head smut; birds.
- Harvest: swath at ~60% mature seed or straight-cut after desiccation; dry to 13%; clean
  to birdseed standard (bright, low dockage).

Price: Kansas proso $6.10–8.51/cwt in recent NASS years (year mapping **verify**); base
$7.00/cwt. Direct cost ~$123 (seed $8, N $17, herbicides $17, fuel and repairs $25,

**Comparison to dryland sorghum (~$100/ac base, `grain-sorghum.md`).** Proso does not
replace sorghum; as a double-crop it is compared with double-crop soybeans or a clean
fallow. At -$25/ac base, raw proso loses money unless the price is above ~$9/cwt or the
yield above ~1,800 lb/ac.

## 4. Raw-crop market
- Buyers: birdseed packers and proso processors in northeast Colorado, western Nebraska
  and northwest Kansas; AMS publishes millet bids in the national sunflower/millet report
  (AMS_2887). No buyer in Sedgwick County (**verify**).
- Terms: mostly spot; some contracts. Price swings are extreme ($2.50–22/cwt in one 5-year
  span, ARS): when birdseed and food markets saturate, proso falls to feed value.
- Volume ceiling for the farm: freight-limited, not demand-limited.

## 5. Finished products
Default structure: the LLC buys proso from the trust farm at $7.00/cwt ($98/ac base) and
processes off trust land. FAMILY $11/hr; HIRED $18/hr sensitivity.

**M1. Bagged white proso millet and blends through the birdseed vertical.**
Uses the cleaner, bagger and channels already planned in
`knowledge/verticals/sunflower-oil-and-birdseed/` (no new capital). 1,288 lb clean/ac at
$0.35/lb blended (feed-store retail $23–37 per 50 lb = $0.46–0.74/lb; LLC sells wholesale
and direct). Variable $0.035/lb (bags, delivery). Labor 4 hr. Fixed $500/yr over 20 acres.
Uplift **$239/ac**; whole-chain net $214/ac. Millet is the standard filler in mixed
wild-bird blends with black-oil sunflower, so it increases the vertical's product range
more than its margin. Ceiling ~40 acres (**verify** with Wichita feed and garden stores).
Regulatory: KDA commercial feed rules may cover bird food (**verify**); net-weight labels.
Verdict: **test** (5–10 acres) if and only if the birdseed vertical runs.

**M2. Hulled millet and gluten-free millet flour.** Dehuller and a dedicated
gluten-free room ~$25,000 (**verify**). ~966 lb hulled/ac at $2.50/lb (**verify**; no
retail price retrieved). Variable $0.50/lb including gluten testing. Labor 29 hr. Uplift
**$715/acre-eq**, ceiling ~3 acres. The farm's combine, bins and cleaner all handle wheat,
so a gluten-free claim (<20 ppm, 21 CFR 101.91) is very hard to support; the sorghum-flour
vertical already covers the gluten-free niche. Verdict: **skip**.

**M3. Cleaned bulk millet to birdseed packers.** $9/cwt for cleaned millet (**verify**)
vs $7 bin-run; cleaning and hauling ~$1/cwt. Uplift **-$6/ac**. Verdict: **skip** (only
as the raw outlet).

## 6. Risks and disqualifiers
- **Top disqualifier: negative raw net in the base case and a distant, boom-bust market.**
  Proso pays only through the birdseed vertical's bags.
- Atrazine carryover sensitivity and volunteer proso in later crops.
- Shatter and bird loss without a swather.
- Double-crop moisture draw lowers the next crop's yield.

## 7. Decision rules
1. Grow proso only as a double-crop after wheat, only on fields with no atrazine in the
   prior 12+ months, and only when the birdseed vertical has orders for the millet.
2. Choose proso over double-crop soybeans only if the bagged-millet margin plus raw net
   beats the soybean double-crop margin in that year's plan.
3. Cap at 10 acres until one year of yield and harvest-loss data.
