# Native grass and wildflower seed

> Research note (2026-11-23): web fetch was blocked for K-State, KCIA, NRCS and USDA
> hosts; numbers come from search-engine summaries of the cited documents. No current
> farm-gate (grower) price for any native species was found. Retail and NRCS foundation
> prices are what buyers pay, not what a grower receives. Every price below marked
> **verify** must be confirmed with a buyer quote before any acre is planted.

**Sorghum benchmark used in every specialty file in this group**: dryland grain sorghum,
75 bu/ac × $3.80/bu (Oct 2026 central Kansas cash) − $206 direct cost (K-State 2026 SC
budget) ≈ **$79/ac return over direct costs** (adverse 55 bu ≈ $3/ac; favorable 90 bu ≈
$136/ac). Fixed costs (land, machinery ownership) are excluded on both sides because the
land and the base machinery are owned. Source: `knowledge/crops/grain-sorghum.md`.

## 1. Fit for this farm
- **Climate and soil**: big bluestem, little bluestem, switchgrass, sideoats grama,
  Indiangrass and buffalograss are native to south-central Kansas; dryland production is
  normal. Deep upland silt loams give the best seed yields; shallow or eroded fields that
  lose money in sorghum are candidate seed fields (and match the playa/depression and
  steep-slope zones in the water file).
- **Perennial commitment**: establishment year with no seed income, then 5–10 harvest
  years (Tallgrass Prairie Center stand life 5–10 yr; Missouri G673: harvest begins one
  full season after the establishment year). Taking a field out of the wheat–sorghum
  rotation for 6–10 years is the main fit question. Perennial grass is agricultural use,
  so the trust risk is low; check any CRP or NRCS contract on the field first.
- **Rotation and herbicide carryover**: residual herbicides from the prior crop
  (atrazine and S-metolachlor after sorghum, ALS products after wheat) can kill native
  grass seedlings; plan a soybean or fallow year before seeding, check every label's
  plant-back interval, or run a soil bioassay (verify).
  Seed fields must be clean of the species' own off-types and of johnsongrass, cheat and
  bindweed, which make seed unsaleable under Kansas noxious-weed seed rules.
- **Equipment the farm has**: planter or drill (a native-seed drill with picker-wheel box
  is better for fluffy bluestem and Indiangrass — rent from the conservation district,
  verify availability), sprayer, tractors, and the grain combine. Switchgrass and sideoats
  thresh in a standard combine with setting changes; big bluestem and Indiangrass are
  chaffy, feed poorly and are often stripper-harvested.
- **Equipment it lacks**: seed stripper (Trac Vac ~$5,000; Flail-Vac type larger),
  a way to dry chaffy seed immediately (aerated wagon or floor; wet native seed heats in a
  pile within hours), a debearder and air-screen cleaner — or a custom conditioner.
  Sharp Bros (Healy, KS; now NativeSeed Group) is the Kansas conditioning specialist.
- **Labor**: low in season compared with produce (harvest is mechanical) but harvest
  timing is narrow (days) and lands in Oct–Nov, after sorghum harvest starts.
- **Wildflowers** (purple prairie clover, black-eyed Susan, Maximilian sunflower,
  coneflowers, milkweed): much higher price per lb, much lower yield, hand weeding and
  multiple harvest passes; treat as a later, small-plot step only.

Comparison with dryland sorghum on the same acre ($79 base, $3 adverse, $136 favorable):
native seed is ahead in base and favorable cases **in harvest years only**. Over a 6-year
stand (1 establishment year with ≈ −$250 and no revenue, 5 harvest years at $150), the
average is ≈ $83/ac/yr — roughly equal to sorghum, with a much wider spread and a buyer
dependence sorghum does not have. Seed price is the swing variable: at $5/PLS lb base net
is ≈ $50; at $10 it is ≈ $300.

## 4. Raw-crop market
- **Kansas seed companies**: NativeSeed Group (acquired Sharp Bros Seed Co., Healy, KS,
  ~Nov 2026; also owns Star Seed, Osborne, KS — 800-782-7311); Stock Seed Farms (Murdock,
  NE); Hamilton Native Outpost (MO); Turner Seed (TX); Bamert Seed (TX). The Healy
  acquisition concentrates the Kansas conditioning and buying market in one company —
  ask whether grower contracts continue and on what terms.
- **Program demand**: CRP general and continuous sign-ups (CP25, CP42 pollinator, CP2),
  NRCS EQIP/CSP plantings (Conservation Cover 327, Range Planting 550 — verify practice
  codes in the Kansas eFOTG), KDOT roadside seeding (KDOT Division 2100 specifications and
  LA850 mixes, bought through highway contractors' seed suppliers), county and city
  projects (Sedgwick County, City of Wichita), restoration contractors and landowners.
  Demand swings with CRP enrollment authority and farm bill status (see
  `knowledge/regulatory/policy-and-legislation-watch.md`).
- **Contract terms (to obtain)**: price per PLS lb by species and origin, PLS minimum,
  who pays conditioning and testing, payment timing (often after cleaning and testing,
  months after harvest), quantity cap.
- **Volume ceiling**: unknown; assume a seed company would take 20–40 acres of one
  species from a new grower (verify). CSV `max_acres_by_market` = 40.
- **Volatility**: high; prices swing with CRP sign-ups and drought (failed seed crops
  raise price). Little bluestem, sideoats and local-ecotype forbs command the highest
  prices.

## 5. Finished products
Default structure: a separate LLC buys cleaned seed from the trust farm at the documented
bulk price ($7.00/PLS lb base) and does mixing, bagging, labeling and selling off trust
land. Labor at FAMILY $11/hr; HIRED_SEASONAL $18/hr shown for the rule-3 check. Convention
for every file in this group: `net_per_acre_equiv` = raw net per acre + the LLC margin,
where the LLC margin = product revenue − variable cost − labor − raw crop bought at market
value − fixed-cost share; therefore uplift = the LLC margin.

**(a) Custom landowner and CRP native grass mixes.** Farm-grown grass plus purchased
companion species, mixed to the FSA/NRCS or landowner specification, bagged by the acre,
labeled. Equipment: platform scale, small paddle mixer, bag sealer, label printer
(stage-1 capital ≈ $5,000, verify). Per acre of seed field: 50 PLS lb sold inside mixes at
$12/PLS lb (2024 retail benchmarks, Turner and Stock Seed); variable $1.00/PLS lb (bags,
tags, test share); 6 hr labor ($66); fixed $1,500/yr (KDA registration, tests, insurance)
over 20 acres of seed = $75/ac. LLC margin ≈ **$59/ac** (HIRED: ≈ $17). Regulatory: KDA
agricultural seed registration (license year Sep–Aug); label each component (purity,
germination, hard seed, weed seed, origin); official lab test per lot. Competitors: every
seed company above, conservation districts, Pheasants Forever habitat store. Verdict:
**test** — thin margin; worth doing only as the outlet for the farm's own seed.

**(b) Pollinator (CP42-type) wildflower–grass mixes.** State-specific pollinator mixes for
CRP CP42, EQIP and small landowners, sold by the acre-pack. Farm grass supplies the grass
fraction; forbs are bought from other growers until the farm grows its own. Per acre of
grass seed field: ~15 acre-packs at $300 (2026 retail $250–450/ac in one guide,
$500–900/ac in another; Pheasants Forever 2026 listings ~$291–484/ac); purchased forbs
assumed at 50% of price ($150/pack, **verify** with forb wholesale quotes) plus $3
packaging; 10 hr labor; fixed $1,500/yr over 10 acres. LLC margin ≈ **$1,595/ac**
(HIRED ≈ $1,525) — but this is a retail margin on mostly bought-in seed and rests on two
unverified numbers (pack price and forb cost). Market: Sedgwick and adjoining counties,
perhaps 100–300 acres/yr of pollinator planting (verify with FSA/NRCS), i.e. ≈ 10 acre-
equivalents. Verdict: **test** — pursue only after one season of mix (a) sales and a
written quote list from forb suppliers.

**(c) Native grass plugs for restoration contractors, landscapers and the City of
Wichita.** Greenhouse-grown plugs in 50- or 72-cell trays. Seed use is negligible (a few
PLS lb), so the per-acre framing is not meaningful; modeled per 10,000-plug batch:
$1.25/plug wholesale (**verify**; no Kansas quote found), $0.45/plug trays and media,
250 hr labor (daily watering for 10–12 weeks), greenhouse off trust land ≈ $25,000,
fixed $4,000/yr. Margin ≈ $1,236 per batch at FAMILY rate and **−$514 at HIRED**. Needs a
KDA nursery certificate (verify). Verdict: **skip** for stage 1 — daily labor, capital and
a separate horticulture skill set.

**(d) Not costed — wild harvest of native hay meadows on shares.** Stripper-harvest
neighbors' native prairie meadows for local-ecotype mixed seed, split with the landowner.
Local-ecotype seed is the scarcest and highest-priced class. Needs the stripper and a
conditioner; buyer and share terms unknown. Listed as an open item.

## 6. Risks and disqualifiers
- **Buyer concentration**: the Kansas conditioner and largest buyer just changed hands;
  no contract, no planting.
- **Price opacity**: grower prices are not published; the plan has no verified farm-gate
  price.
- **Establishment failure**: native stands commonly take two seasons to establish;
  a failed year doubles establishment cost.
- **Harvest and drying**: chaffy seed heats in storage; a lost harvest window (rain,
  shatter) loses the year.
- **Demand cycle**: CRP enrollment authority, payment rates and farm bill timing drive
  demand; NRCS cost-share availability shifts year to year.
- **Weed contamination**: noxious-weed seed in a lot means a stop-sale under the Kansas
  seed law.
- **Long commitment**: 6–10 years on one field; field becomes harder to return to crop
  (sod, perennial weed seed bank).

## 7. Decision rules
1. No native seed field is planted without a written buyer contract or letter of intent
   (price per PLS lb, PLS minimum, conditioning terms) covering at least 60% of expected
   output (buyer-network rule 1).
2. First field ≤ 20 acres, on a field whose 5-year sorghum net is below the farm median
   (shallow, eroded, or a chronic-loss water zone).
3. Start with a combine-harvestable species (switchgrass or sideoats grama) unless the
   buyer pays at least $2/PLS lb more for big bluestem or Indiangrass to cover stripper
   and drying cost.
4. Mix sales (product a) start only after the farm has a cleaned, tested lot; pollinator
   mixes (product b) only after a supplier quote list for forbs exists.
5. Keep the field if harvest-year net (three-year rolling average) exceeds dryland
   sorghum's by $40/ac; otherwise terminate after the contract ends.
