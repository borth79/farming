# Specialty crops and finished products ranking (2026-11-23)

Source: `data/processed/finished_products.csv` (187 rows: 41 raw crops, 146 products),
merged from six research groups and normalized to one basis: uplift = margin earned by a
processing LLC after buying the crop from the trust farm at market value; whole-chain net
= raw net + uplift. Raw nets are over direct costs; dryland sorghum is about $79–100/acre
on the same basis. Labor at the FAMILY rate unless noted. Confidence is low throughout:
almost no farm-gate prices were found, and every figure carries "verify" in its file.
Honey is counted per colony and excluded from per-acre ranks.

## 3. Large on paper, rated skip
Own farm winery (~$58k/yr but $350k capital and 11-year payback), fresh masa and
tortillas (~$58k/yr but a full-time daily food job), canned beans via co-packer, granola,
elderberry gummies, aronia juice, tofu. Each fails on capital, daily labor, a collapsed
market, or a national competitor setting the price.

## 4. Readings
1. The highest uplifts per acre come from products whose market holds less than an acre.
   They are good side lines for a family member, not a reason to change the rotation.
2. The products that scale to 20–100 acres add $100–1,800 an acre, and they all run on
   two pieces of shared equipment: the planned flour mill (Turkey Red flour and berries,
   cornmeal) and one seed cleaning and bagging line (rye and cover-crop seed, certified
   wheat, deer corn, milo and wildlife feed, birdseed). Those two lines are the best
   capital in this whole analysis.
3. Perennials (blackberry, elderberry, grapes, lavender) earn the most per acre but tie up
   land for 5–25 years and need trustee approval, a long enough lease, and often public
   access. They belong in a separate decision with the stakeholders.
4. Contract crops (cowpeas, mung beans, hemp grain, sesame) hinge on one or two buyers
   whose prices were not found; one phone call each decides them.
