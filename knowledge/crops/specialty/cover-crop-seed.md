# Cover crop seed

> Research note (2026-11-23): sources are search summaries; no Kansas grower contract
> price for cover crop seed was found. Prices below are buyer-side listings, mostly
> undated. **verify** every price with a written quote.

Sorghum benchmark: dryland grain sorghum ≈ **$79/ac** return over direct costs base
($3 adverse, $136 favorable); wheat ≈ **$75/ac** (50 bu × $5.80 − $215 direct). See
`knowledge/crops/grain-sorghum.md` and `knowledge/crops/wheat-hard-red-winter.md`.

## 1. Fit for this farm
Species-by-species, because fit differs sharply:

| Species | Fit to grow for seed here | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Cereal rye | **good agronomically, bad for the wheat enterprise** | grown and combined exactly like wheat with the drill and grain combine; but volunteer rye is a serious contaminant of hard red winter wheat (dockage, grade, and a disqualifier in certified wheat seed fields) and persists for years in the seed bank |
| Triticale | good | same equipment as wheat; less volunteer persistence than rye (verify); smaller cover crop market |
| Winter (Austrian) peas | possible | drill and combine; Ascochyta and aphid risk; shatter at harvest; demand is in mixes only |
| Hairy vetch | **poor** | seed industry sits in OR, OK, TX, AR; hard seed volunteers for years and is a weed in wheat; vetch weevil and bruchid; pod shatter; 13.5% of fields not harvested in one study |
| Radish and turnip | **poor** | US brassica seed is grown mainly in the Pacific Northwest; isolation, shatter, flea beetles and aphids; no local conditioning |
| Sunn hemp | **poor** | short-day tropical legume; seed is produced in south Texas and Hawaii; unlikely to mature seed in Kansas (no source found confirming either way — verify) |

- Water: dryland rye and triticale fit the wheat water budget; no irrigation needed.
- Equipment: the farm has everything for rye/triticale except seed cleaning, a scale and
  bagging. Cleaning to seed standard needs an air-screen cleaner (or custom conditioner).
- Herbicide: rye and triticale share wheat's herbicide label constraints; no carryover
  conflict with the sorghum program beyond wheat's.
- Trust: seed production is agriculture; bagging and selling run through the LLC by
  default. Low trust risk.
- Labor: like wheat plus cleaning and bagging days in July–August, which is when
  cover crop seed sells (August–October seeding).

Comparison: ahead of sorghum ($79) and wheat ($75) on paper, **only if** a buyer pays
~$8/bu. At a commodity rye price (~$5/bu, verify) net drops to ≈ $20. The hidden cost is
volunteer rye contaminating later wheat on that field — budget one extra year of summer
crops on any rye seed field.

## 4. Raw-crop market
- Buyers: cover crop specialists (Green Cover Seed — Iola, KS location; Star Seed,
  Osborne, KS; regional seed dealers and co-ops). SARE 2026 survey: 40% of growers buy
  from specialist cover crop seed companies, 17% from commodity seed dealers.
- Demand driver: EQIP 340 pays $75.09/ac single species in Kansas FY2026 (verify), the
  second-largest Kansas EQIP practice by obligations 2017–2024 ($40.6 M). Demand moves with
  EQIP funding and drought (cover crop seed was tight in 2022, DTN).
- Volume ceiling for a new grower: unknown; assume ≤100 acres contracted (verify).
- Competitors: established seed growers in the KCIA directory; every co-op seed plant.

## 5. Finished products
Default: the LLC buys cleaned rye from the trust farm at $8.00/bu ($320/ac at base yield)
and cleans, blends, bags and sells from leased space off trust land. Convention: uplift =
LLC margin (revenue − variable − labor at FAMILY − raw crop at market − fixed share).

**(a) Bagged custom cover crop mixes for neighbors.** Rye base plus bought-in radish,
turnip, peas or vetch, blended to the neighbor's EQIP plan and bagged by the acre. One acre
of rye (2,240 lb) fills ~45 acre-packs at ~50 lb rye + purchased components. Price $35 per
acre-pack (verify against Green Cover and co-op quotes; EQIP 340 at $75/ac covers seed
and planting); variable $16/pack (purchased radish 2 lb ≈ $3–5, peas/vetch 10 lb ≈ $5–15,
bag, tag and test share); 7 hr labor; capital $15,000 (used air-screen cleaner, paddle
mixer, platform scale, bag sealer — verify); fixed $3,000/yr over 30 acres = $100/ac. LLC
margin ≈ **$358/ac** (HIRED ≈ $309). Market: Sedgwick County cover crop acres are not
known; 30 acres of rye supplies ~1,350 acre-packs, a large share of a county market
(verify with NRCS). Regulatory: KDA seed registration; label every component; germination
and purity test per lot; noxious-weed seed limits. Verdict: **test**.

**(b) 50-lb cereal rye food-plot bags to farm stores and hunters.** Wichita metro hunters
and small landowners buy rye for deer and turkey food plots in August–October. 44 bags per
acre at $28 to the store (retail 50-lb bags $45.95+ observed; wholesale assumed below
retail — verify with two farm stores); variable $2.50/bag; 6 hr; shared equipment. LLC
margin ≈ **$636/ac** (HIRED ≈ $594). Market ceiling small: perhaps 500–700 bags a season
through independent farm and feed stores (verify) ≈ 15 acres. Regulatory: as (a).
Verdict: **test** — best uplift in the group, simplest product, small ceiling.

**(c) Custom cleaning and blending service for neighbors' bin-run seed.** Fee per cwt
($1.50 assumed, verify) for cleaning rye, oats or wheat for the neighbor's own use. Per
acre-equivalent (22.4 cwt) revenue ≈ $34, margin ≈ **−$2**. Conditioners are exempt from
KDA registration, but cleaning protected wheat varieties for sale is a PVP violation risk;
refuse those jobs. Verdict: **skip** unless the cleaner is idle and already paid for.

**(d) Not costed — triticale and winter pea seed.** Same channels as (a); add once rye
mixes sell. Hairy vetch, radish, turnip and sunn hemp seed: **skip** (section 1).

## 6. Risks and disqualifiers
- **Volunteer rye in wheat** is the top disqualifier for a wheat-anchored farm; it also
  rules out certified wheat seed on the same fields.
- Price opacity: no verified farm-gate price.
- Demand tied to EQIP funding and drought.
- Ergot or weed seed makes a lot unsaleable; failed germination test.
- Mix business is a retail business: inventory risk on bought-in components after
  October.

## 7. Decision rules
1. Rye only on a field that will not grow wheat for certified seed, and only with a
   two-summer-crop break before wheat returns.
2. No bulk rye acres without a written price from a seed company covering 60% of output.
3. Start product (b) with ≤ 5 acres and two farm-store accounts; start (a) only after a
   cleaned, tested rye lot exists.
4. Drop the enterprise if rye net (three-year average) does not beat sorghum by $40/ac.
