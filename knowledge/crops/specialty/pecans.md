# Pecans (native and improved northern varieties)

## 1. Fit for this farm
**Verdict: poor fit for upland Sedgwick County fields; at most a small irrigated trial on
deep bottomland, and only if the trust can commit land for 40+ years.**

- **Climate.** Zone 6b–7a with ~200 frost-free days is inside the northern-pecan range,
  and native pecans reach Cowley County (adjacent to the south). Commercial Kansas
  production is concentrated in the southeast (Cherokee, Neosho, Labette and others),
  where rainfall is ~10 in higher. Late spring freezes and October freezes before shuck
  split are the main climate risks for northern varieties. No Sedgwick County production
  data was found.
- **Soil and water.** Pecans want deep, well-drained bottomland (Kansas Forest Service:
  "growth greatly restricted on upland droughty sites"). On the farm's upland silt loams
  and claypans, an improved orchard needs drip or micro-sprinkler irrigation. Irrigation
  status per field is undetermined (`farm-profile.md`), and irrigating a new orchard
  would need a water right and possibly a change-of-use approval from DWR.
- **Native groves.** If any field has native pecan trees on creek bottoms, improving
  them (thinning, weed and grass control, fertilizing, top-working to improved varieties)
  is the only cheap way in. Whether such trees exist is **unknown; ask Tony**.
- **Equipment.** The farm's tractors and sprayer help with orchard-floor mowing and
  herbicide strips, but a grain sprayer cannot reach a 60 ft canopy (air-blast sprayer
  needed). Pecans need a trunk shaker, sweeper or harvester, and a cleaner, or a custom
  harvester; none are owned. The grain combine is useless here.
- **Herbicide carryover and drift.** Pecans are less drift-sensitive than grapes, but
  2,4-D and dicamba on neighboring wheat and sorghum still injure young trees. Atrazine
  on adjacent sorghum is not a carryover problem for an orchard planted on a dedicated
  block.
- **Labor.** Harvest falls in October–December, overlapping sorghum and soybean
  harvest and wheat planting. The farm has no dedicated labor; custom harvest is the
  assumption.
- **Perennial and trust.** **An improved pecan orchard is a 40–80 year improvement on
  trust land.** Trees become part of the real estate and belong to the trust by default.
  It needs written trustee approval citing the trust power relied on, and a lease from
  the trust to the operating entity whose term covers payback (base case year ~22, see
  section 3) or a reimbursement clause on termination. It also changes the land's
  appraisal and its §2032A qualified-use profile, both to check with counsel
  (`family-trust-constraints.md`). Unknown trust terms are treated as prohibitions.

## 2. Agronomy summary
- **Varieties (northern, Kansas-tested).** Kanza (the most popular in Kansas; scab
  resistant, small nut, high kernel quality), Pawnee (early, large, scab-prone), Lakota,
  Faith, Greenriver, Peruque, Giles (native selection from Chetopa). Plant at least two
  pollination types (protandrous and protogynous). The Chetopa Pecan Experiment Field,
  the source of Kansas scion wood and trial data, has closed. Variety advice should now
  come from Oklahoma State or Missouri (verify).
- **Planting.** Bare-root or container seedlings planted in winter and grafted 1–2
  years later (OSU), or pre-grafted trees. 27–35 trees/ac (40 × 40 ft, or 35/ac thinned
  at year 14). Drip installed at planting. Keep a weed-free strip 6 ft or wider for the
  first 5 years.
- **Fertility.** Nitrogen ~100–150 lb/ac on bearing trees (verify against OSU).
  Zinc foliar sprays are essential on high-pH soils. Leaf analysis in July.
- **Pests.** Pecan scab (fungicide program in wet springs), pecan nut casebearer,
  hickory shuckworm, pecan weevil (the key pest in Kansas and Oklahoma), aphids,
  squirrels and crows.
- **Harvest.** Shake from October to December after shuck split. Sweep or harvest from
  a mown floor, then clean. Dry to ~4–5 % kernel moisture. Cold storage holds kernel
  quality.
- **Bearing.** First marketable crop around year 8. Commercial production in years
  7–10, peak 3–5 years later (OSU). Strongly alternate-bearing.

## 4. Raw-crop market
- **Buyers.** Accumulators, shellers and buying stations in southeast Kansas,
  Oklahoma and Texas buy in-shell by the pound with grading for kernel percentage and
  quality. No named buyer within an hour of Wichita was found (**verify**; call Oklahoma
  pecan buyers and the Kansas pecan growers around Chetopa and Baxter Springs). A
  formal Kansas pecan growers' association no longer appears to exist.
- **Prices.** US average $2.10/lb in 2025 and $1.77/lb in 2024 (NASS via Pecan South).
  Improved ~$3.00/lb and native ~$2.92/lb Jan–Oct 2025 versus ~$3.49/lb improved in 2024
  (USDA via Mississippi State; the two series conflict, so **verify**). Prices peak in
  November–December. The volatility is high: alternate bearing, Mexican supply (now the
  largest producer) and China and India export demand.
- **Federal marketing order.** Kansas is in Federal Marketing Order 986 (American
  Pecan Council), which collects handler assessments. Membership and handling rules
  changed in a 2026-11-09 Federal Register notice (**read before selling**).
- **Volume ceiling.** The commodity market absorbs any volume this farm could produce.
  The ceiling is on the price, not the quantity.

## 6. Risks and disqualifiers
1. **Trust and time horizon (top disqualifier).** 40–80 year trees on trust land,
   payback around year 22, 11 years of negative cash. Needs trustee approval and a lease
   longer than payback. Trees belong to the trust by default.
2. **Site.** Upland soils and no confirmed water right. Without irrigation, the
   adverse case is likely in drought years.
3. **No equipment or labor** for shaking, sweeping, cleaning or air-blast spraying, and
   custom pecan harvesters are scarce in south-central Kansas (**verify**).
4. Pecan weevil, scab, squirrels and crows, and alternate bearing. Freeze before shuck
   split in northern varieties.
5. Price: imports from Mexico and export demand swings. 2024–2025 prices were below
   2023.
6. Drift from neighbors' and the farm's own 2,4-D and dicamba on young trees.

## 7. Decision rules
1. Do not plant improved pecans on upland fields.
2. Consider at most a 2–5 acre irrigated block only if all of these hold: (a) a
   bottomland field with deep soil is identified; (b) a water right covers it; (c) the
   trustee approves in writing and the lease covers 25+ years or has a reimbursement
   schedule; (d) a custom harvester and an in-shell buyer are named.
3. If native pecans exist on creek bottoms, run an OSU-style native improvement
   (thin, control weeds, fertilize, control weevil) before any new planting. It is low
   capital and reversible.
4. Sell in-shell and custom-shelled through the LLC. Skip oil.
