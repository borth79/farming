# Certified seed wheat and sorghum

> Research note (2026-11-23): no published Kansas price for certified seed wheat sold by
> a grower, no Kansas Wheat Alliance royalty rate, and no KCIA per-acre fee schedule was
> retrieved. The economics below use placeholders flagged **verify**; the first action is
> three phone calls (KCIA, Kansas Wheat Alliance, a custom seed conditioner).

Benchmarks: dryland wheat ≈ **$75/ac** return over direct costs (50 bu × $5.80 − $215);
dryland sorghum ≈ **$79/ac** (`knowledge/crops/grain-sorghum.md`).

## 1. Fit for this farm
- **Wheat — strong agronomic fit.** The farm already grows hard red winter wheat with its
  own drill and combine. Growing registered or certified seed of public (K-State) or
  licensed private varieties adds: foundation or registered seed purchase, a field with
  the right history, roguing off-types and rye, KCIA inspection after heading, a scrupulously
  cleaned combine, truck and bin, and conditioning by a KCIA-approved conditioner.
- **What it lacks**: a seed conditioning plant (cleaner, gravity table, treater, bagger).
  Building one is a $250,000+ decision (verify) and out of scope for stage 1; use a
  custom approved conditioner within hauling distance (identify from the KCIA directory).
- **Field history**: wheat seed fields must not have grown a different wheat variety or
  rye recently (KCIA standard; verify the exact years). This **conflicts with cereal rye
  seed** (see `cover-crop-seed.md`) and with double-crop soybeans only through volunteer
  wheat management.
- **Sorghum — poor fit.** Nearly all grain sorghum planted in Kansas is hybrid seed
  produced by seed companies under contract (A-line × R-line, isolation, usually
  irrigated in the Texas High Plains and southwest Kansas). Public open-pollinated grain
  sorghum varieties have little farmer demand. Possible niches: certified open-pollinated
  forage sorghum, sudangrass or a food-grade heritage variety — small and unverified.
  Contract hybrid production for a seed company would need irrigation, which is
  undetermined on this farm. Not costed.
- **Trust and labor**: seed production is agriculture; seed sales can stay with the farm
  or run through the LLC (default). Labor adds roguing (June) and sales (Aug–Oct), the
  latter overlapping fall planting.

## 3. Yield and raw-crop economics
Raw sale = the same wheat sold as commodity grain.

| Case | Yield bu/ac | Cash $/bu | Net over direct ($215) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Adverse | 35 | 5.30 | ≈ −30 |
| Base | 50 | 5.80 | ≈ 75 |
| Favorable | 60 | 6.40 | ≈ 170 |

Cash price basis: AMS Kansas bids $5.26–5.96 (2026-11-19); wheat file Oct 2026 terminal
$6.10–6.80 less country basis. Seed wheat itself, base: 50 bu harvested → ~43 bu clean
seed after ~14% screenings (verify) → sold at $14/bu bulk (verify; national certified HRW
$16–20 bagged per a secondary source; 2019 Kansas $9–11 at $4 grain).

## 4. Raw-crop market
- Buyers of certified seed are neighbors within ~30–50 miles, co-op seed departments, and
  seed dealers. Most Kansas growers save some seed; certified seed demand rises after a
  poor seed year or when a new variety is released (Kansas Wheat 2022 shortage notice).
- Competitors: the established certified growers in the KCIA 2026 Seed Directory around
  Sedgwick, Sumner, Harvey, Reno and Kingman counties (names to pull); co-op seed plants.
- Volume ceiling: one seed acre supplies ~35–45 acres of neighbor planting at 60–75 lb/ac
  (1.0–1.25 bu). 100 acres of seed (~4,300 bu) seeds ~3,500–4,300 acres — achievable only
  with an established customer list. Start at 40–60 acres.
- Price behavior: certified seed premium over grain is sticky (seed $13–20 when grain is
  $4–8); seed revenue is less volatile than grain but unsold seed after mid-November is
  worth grain price.

## 5. Finished products
Default: the LLC (or the farm, if counsel prefers to keep seed sales in the farm entity)
buys the seed-field wheat at the commodity price ($5.80/bu × 50 bu = $290/ac) and pays for
conditioning, testing, royalty and tags. Convention: uplift = LLC margin.

**(a) Bulk certified seed wheat to neighbors.** Clean, tested, tagged seed in bulk
(customer's truck or totes). Per acre: 43 bu × $14.00 = $602, plus screenings 7 bu as feed
(~$4/bu, $28) credited against variable cost; variable $3.75/bu gross before the screenings
credit (royalty, KCIA inspection share, lab tests, tags and custom conditioning — all
**verify**) → $3.10/bu net; labor 4 hr (roguing, handling, sales); capital $2,000 (bin
clean-out kit, probes, sample bags); fixed $3,000/yr (seed registration, insurance,
advertising) over 100 acres = $30/ac. LLC margin ≈ **$105/ac** (HIRED ≈ $77). Regulatory:
KCIA application, inspection and Form B fees; Kansas Wheat Alliance license and royalty for
KSU varieties; PVP Title V (protected varieties only as certified); KDA seed registration;
truthful labeling. Verdict: **test** on 40–60 acres once two to three neighbors commit.

**(b) Bagged certified seed (50-lb bags via the conditioner's bagging line).** Same seed,
bagged and palletized for smaller buyers and co-op resale. $19/bu (verify), extra $1.50/bu
bags and bagging, 6 hr labor, fixed $4,000/yr over 50 acres. LLC margin ≈ **$183/ac** (HIRED
≈ $141). Market narrower: most farm buyers take bulk; bagged suits small farms, co-op
shelves and food-plot buyers. Verdict: **test** alongside (a), ≤ 50 acres equivalent.

**(c) Seed-treating add-on.** Fungicide or fungicide–insecticide treatment on order at
+$3/bu (verify), chemical ~$2/bu (verify), treater ≈ $15,000 (verify). LLC margin ≈ $107/ac,
only ~$2/ac better than untreated bulk, and needs a pesticide applicator certification and
treated-seed labeling. Verdict: **skip**; buy treatment from the custom conditioner if
customers ask.

**(d) Not costed — certified sorghum seed.** Hybrid grain sorghum: skip (section 1).
Open-pollinated forage sorghum, sudangrass or food-grade heritage grain sorghum for
niche buyers: open item.

## 6. Risks and disqualifiers
- **Unsold inventory**: seed not sold by planting season reverts to grain price after
  carrying conditioning and testing costs.
- **Failed inspection or germination** (rye, off-types, weed seed, low germ after a wet
  harvest) drops the lot to grain.
- **Customer concentration and relationship sales**: neighbors buy from neighbors they
  trust; a new seller needs 2–3 committed customers first.
- **Conditioner access**: no conditioning plant on the farm; the nearest approved
  conditioner's capacity and timing in July–September is the binding constraint.
- **Legal exposure**: selling bin-run seed of a PVP-protected variety ("brown bagging")
  is illegal; only tagged certified seed is sold.
- **Rotation conflict** with rye seed and with any field that had volunteer issues.

## 7. Decision rules
1. Plant a seed field only with written commitments for ≥ 60% of expected clean seed.
2. Book conditioning capacity and get the conditioner's per-bushel rate in writing before
   buying foundation or registered seed.
3. Seed fields: best wheat fields with clean history; no rye on those fields ever.
4. Price bulk seed at commodity cash + a fixed premium published by August 1; unsold seed
   after November 15 goes to the elevator.
5. Exit if the three-year average uplift is below $40/ac over commodity wheat.
