# Cowpeas (black-eyed peas and southern peas)

## 1. Fit for this farm
- **Climate: good.**
  - Cowpea (Vigna unguiculata) is the most heat- and drought-tolerant pulse on this list.
  - Oklahoma's leading vegetable crop is the blackeye pea.
  - Cowpeas yield well in hot, dry conditions (Oklahoma AITC).
  - Warm nights at bloom can still reduce pod set (UC Davis blackeye work, cited
    secondhand; verify). Cowpea tolerates this far better than Phaseolus beans.
- **Season**: plant late May to early July into warm soil. Dry harvest falls in September
  or October. A double-crop after wheat is feasible for short-season bush types (70–90 days
  to dry, verify by variety).
- **Soil**: tolerant of low fertility and sandy soils. Intolerant of waterlogging.
- **Rotation**:
  - Fits after wheat.
  - Atrazine plant-back: assume cowpea cannot follow an atrazine year (verify the label).
  - Root-knot nematode host. Do not follow with susceptible crops on sandy fields.
- **Equipment**:
  - Dry harvest: bush or semi-upright blackeye types can be direct-combined with a flex or
    draper header after desiccation.
  - Vining types (many cover-crop and heirloom southern peas) are a poor fit for the
    combine.
  - Fresh-shelled product: needs hand picking or a mechanical pea picker. The farm has
    neither.
  - Shelling: needs a pea sheller.
- **Trust and labor**: dry cowpeas are low labor. Fresh peas are labor-heavy and belong in
  the produce plots, with sales through the LLC.
- **Commitment**: annual.

## 2. Agronomy summary
- **Varieties**:
  - Blackeye: California Blackeye No. 5 and similar.
  - Southern peas for fresh market: Pinkeye Purple Hull, Mississippi Purple, cream types.
  - Cover-crop types: Iron & Clay, Red Ripper, Whippoorwill.
  - Apache Seed contracts five varieties (names not found).
- **Planting**:
  - Soil at 65 °F or above.
  - About 50 lb/ac for grain types (Purdue); higher for cover crops.
  - Rows of 15–30 in.
  - Inoculate with cowpea-group Bradyrhizobium.
- **Fertility**: little N. P by soil test.
- **Weeds**: PRE options are pendimethalin and S-metolachlor (label verify). Few POST broadleaf
  options. Palmer amaranth is the risk.
- **Insects**: cowpea curculio (southern; range in Kansas unclear), stink bugs, aphids, pod
  borers.
- **Disease**: cowpea mosaic viruses, Fusarium wilt, root-knot nematode, charcoal rot.
- **Harvest**: desiccate for dry harvest. Combine gently, because blackeye seed coats crack.
  Clean and grade for food or seed.

## 4. Raw-crop market

  has its own processing facility (HPJ 2025). Terms, price and Kansas eligibility are not
  known.
- **Other outlets**:
  - Canning and dry-pack processors in Texas, Arkansas and California (not identified).
  - Cover-crop seed companies buying cowpea seed. Green Cover Seed (Nebraska) and similar

  - Local cattle and wildlife food-plot buyers.
- **Volume ceiling**: contract acreage. Unknown; 50–300 acres is plausible for a new grower
  (estimate).
- **Price history**: $14–25/bu historically (Purdue). Thin, opaque market.

## 5. Finished products
The default LLC structure applies. Raw crop is charged at 1,000 lb × $0.30 = $300 per acre-
equivalent. Labor is at FAMILY $11/hr; HIRED $18/hr is shown where it changes the verdict.
Uplift follows the schema (LLC net minus raw net of $158).

### 5a. Fresh-shelled southern peas (purple hull, pinkeye, cream) at markets and stand
- **Process**:
  - Grow 0.5–1.5 acres in the produce plots.
  - Hand-pick green-mature pods over 3–4 weeks in July and August.
  - Machine-shell with a bench pea sheller, rinse, cool and bag (10 lb "shelled bushels"
    and 1–2 lb bags).
- **Capital**: about $8,000 (pea sheller, verify price; cooler shared with produce).
- **Labor**: about 0.15 hr/lb (picking 0.10, shelling and washing 0.02, selling 0.03) ×
  1,500 lb = 225 hr = $2,475 at FAMILY rate ($4,050 at HIRED).
- **Raw needed**: about 1,500 lb of shelled peas per acre (estimate, verify).
- **Price**: $4.50/lb blended.
  - Shelled bushel of about 10 lb sells for $44–60 at farm stands (Arkansas and online
    listings).
  - Extension guide (~2020) wholesale: $2–3/lb.
- **Margin**: $6,750 − $900 (bags, ice, market fees) − $2,475 − $300 − $667 ($1,000 ÷
  1.5 ac) = **$2,408 at FAMILY rate**. Schema uplift $2,250. At HIRED rate, about $833.
- **Regulatory**:
  - FSMA Produce Safety Rule: likely qualified exempt at this scale (verify).
  - Whether shelled, bagged peas count as "processed" under KDA rules: verify.
- **Competitors**: few in Wichita. Southern peas are a cultural product with a loyal but
  modest Wichita demand. Oklahoma and Texas growers supply grocery chains in season.
- **Market**: about 1.5 acres (estimate).
- **Verdict: test** on 0.5 acre in the produce plots. Highest margin per acre on this list,
  but labor-bound.

### 5b. Dry bagged blackeye and heirloom cowpeas, 1–2 lb
- **Process**: same cleaner and bagging line as dry beans.
- **Capital**: about $8,000, shared with beans.
- **Labor**: 0.05 hr/lb × 900 lb = 45 hr = $495.
- **Price**: $4.00/lb blended (estimate, verify). Heirloom southern peas command premiums
  nationally; commodity blackeye retail is much lower.
- **Margin**: $3,600 − $765 − $495 − $300 − $667 = **$1,373**. Schema uplift $1,215.
- **Regulatory**: KDA license for store sales; FSMA qualified facility.
- **Market**: about 3 acres. Strong seasonal spike at New Year's (good-luck black-eyed
  peas), when holiday-market and gift-box demand peaks.
- **Verdict: test** with the bean line.

### 5c. Frozen shelled field peas
- **Process**: blanch, cool, IQF or tray freeze, then bag. Licensed kitchen and freezer
  storage.
- **Capital**: about $15,000.
- **Labor**: 261 hr per acre-equivalent.
- **Price**: $5.00/lb (estimate).
- **Margin**: $7,250 − $1,740 − $2,871 − $300 − $2,000 = **$339**. Negative at HIRED rate
  (-$1,488).
- **Regulatory**: KDA license; frozen storage.
- **Verdict: skip.** Labor-bound and only marginally better than fresh.

### 5d. Cover-crop seed: cleaned, germination-tested, tagged 50 lb bags
- **Process**:
  - Grow a public, non-PVP variety (e.g., Iron & Clay type; verify protection status).
  - Clean, run a germination and purity test, and tag under the Kansas Seed Law.
  - Sell to neighbors, cover-crop mixers and EQIP cost-share participants.
- **Capital**: about $8,000, shared cleaner.
- **Labor**: 14 hr.
- **Price**: $1.00/lb wholesale (estimate, verify). Retail cover-crop cowpea and mung seed
  lists at about $1.36/lb and up (Green Cover).
- **Margin**: $900 − $72 − $154 − $300 − $75 = **$299**. Schema uplift $141.
- **Regulatory**:
  - KDA seed labeler registration and tested labels.
  - Federal Seed Act if sold across state lines.
  - No PVP-protected varieties.
- **Market**: about 20 acres (estimate), growing with cover-crop adoption.
- **Verdict: test.** Cheap to add and uses the existing combine.

## 6. Risks and disqualifiers
- **Top disqualifier: no confirmed buyer for raw dry cowpeas at a known price.** Apache Seed
  terms are unknown and the plant is about 210 miles away. Without a contract, raw cowpeas
  are unmarketable beyond the cover-crop seed niche.
- **No RMA coverage.**
- **Atrazine plant-back.**
- **Few herbicides.**
- **Fresh product labor**: 225 hr/ac is not available on a farm with no dedicated labor.
- **Combine**: vining types do not suit it.
- **Seed coats**: blackeye seed coat damage at harvest lowers grade.

## 7. Decision rules
1. Grow raw dry cowpeas only under a written contract (Apache Seed or another buyer) that
   nets at least $0.28/lb after freight. Grow on post-wheat, atrazine-free fields.
2. Cover-crop seed acres are capped at the volume pre-sold to named buyers. Use public
   varieties only.
3. Fresh southern peas: at most 0.5 acre in year 1, and only if family pickers are
   committed for July–August.
