# Other oilseeds and industrial crops: guar and castor

**Both are not viable for this farm.** No full template file is warranted. This note records why,
so the question is not re-researched without a trigger.

## Guar
- **Market:** one contractor, Guar Resources in Brownfield TX (about 450 road miles from Wichita,
  **verify**). It contracts in TX, OK (southwest) and NM. The latest published grower price was
  2023: $0.175/lb delivered Brownfield, $0.160 at other receiving points, described as "the same
  as 2020". Demand follows oilfield fracking (guar gum) and food gum imports from India.
- **Fit:** a long, hot, dry-season legume. South-central Kansas is north of the production belt,
  and frost risk at harvest is high (estimate).
- **Rough economics (ESTIMATE):** 1,000 lb/ac × $0.13 (after freight) = $130, against ~$120 of
  direct cost, or about **$10/ac** over direct. Dryland sorghum nets ~$79/ac (`grain-sorghum.md`).
- **Finished products:** none plausible. Guar gum milling is an industrial process.
- **Verdict: skip.**
- **Trigger to revisit:** a receiving point within 150 miles and a price ≥ $0.25/lb.

## Castor
- **Market:** no U.S. grower contract market was found for 2025–2026. World supply is Indian
  (NCDEX futures). U.S. castor oil is imported.
- **Fit and hazards:**
  - The seed contains ricin, so a residue in grain handling is a serious food and feed
    contamination liability. It would be incompatible with the farm's food-grade wheat, sorghum
    and sunflower lines and the LLC's food products.
  - The dust is allergenic.
  - Plants are indeterminate and shatter-prone, and volunteers persist.
- **Economics:** no buyer means revenue is $0, a loss equal to direct cost (~$110/ac estimate).
- **Verdict: skip.** Do not grow even as a trial on a farm that handles food grain.
- **Trigger to revisit:** only if a contracted, low-ricin variety program with a dedicated
  harvest-and-haul service appears in the region.
