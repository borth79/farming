# Black walnut (timber and nuts)

## 1. Fit for this farm
**Verdict: not a cash crop. At best a riparian, windbreak or odd-corner timber planting
that is a legacy asset for the remainder beneficiaries, and only with cost-share.**

- **Climate and soil.** Black walnut is native across eastern and central Kansas
  riparian corridors. It grows well only on deep, well-drained, moist bottomland. On
  upland claypans or droughty soils growth and log quality are poor. Sedgwick County
  creek bottoms (Arkansas River tributaries) may suit it; upland crop fields do not.
- **Rotation conflict.** Juglone from roots and leaves is toxic to many plants (alfalfa,
  tomatoes, potatoes and others; **verify** list for the farm's crops). Keep plantings
  away from produce plots and alfalfa. Walnut shade and roots also cut yields on
  adjoining crop rows.
- **Equipment.** Tractors and the sprayer handle planting-row weed control and mowing.
  Nut harvest is by hand, with rolling nut gatherers, or a small harvester. The grain
  combine has no role. Hulling is done at the Hammons station.
- **Labor.** Low after establishment: pruning and thinning about 2–4 hr/ac/yr
  (estimate). Nut pickup in October is hand work that competes with fall harvest.
- **Perennial and trust.** **A timber planting is a 40–80 year improvement on trust
  land, the longest commitment in this group.** The trees are part of the real estate
  and belong to the trust; the timber value accrues to whoever holds the land at harvest,
  which is usually the remainder beneficiaries rather than the current operator. It
  needs written trustee approval, impartiality analysis (current versus remainder
  beneficiaries) and a check against §2032A qualified use (timber may or may not count
  as the qualified farm use; counsel to confirm). It may also affect agricultural-use
  property valuation if acres are reclassified. A timber sale contract would be signed
  by the trustee.

## 2. Agronomy summary
- **Planting.** 1-0 bare-root seedlings, ~300–450/ac at 10–12 × 10–12 ft (**verify**
  Kansas Forest Service recommendations and seedling price). Direct-seeding is possible.
  Grafted nut cultivars (e.g., Sparrow, Kwik-Krop, Thomas, Emma K; **verify** suitability)
  at 30–40/ac for a nut orchard.
- **Establishment.** 3–4 years of strict weed control in a 4–6 ft strip
  (pre-emergent herbicide plus mowing). Tree shelters where deer pressure is high.
  Corrective pruning to a single straight 9–17 ft butt log.
- **Management.** Thin to 30–35 crop trees/ac by about year 30–40. Prune lower limbs
  in years 3–15. Pests: walnut anthracnose, thousand cankers disease (quarantine issue;
  **verify** Kansas status), walnut curculio and husk fly.
- **Nuts.** Seedling trees bear sporadically from year ~10–15, usually alternate years.
  Hulled at a Hammons station, which pays on the weight of hulled nuts.
- **Timber.** Veneer- and sawlog-quality trees at 40–80 years, depending on site.

## 3. Yield and raw-crop economics
Modeled: a dual-purpose seedling plantation, 1 acre. Establishment $900 in year 1 and
$150/yr in years 2–4 (seedlings, planting, weed control, shelters; **estimate, no Kansas
source retrieved**). Maintenance $40/yr. Nuts: 300 lb/ac hulled in years 15–29 and 800
lb/ac from year 30 (estimate, below the Missouri 1,000–1,500 lb figure for
nut-managed plantations). Nut price $0.16/lb (Hammons 2025 closing). Pickup and haul
$0.20/lb (hand pickup at ~100 lb/hr × $18 HIRED_SEASONAL, **estimate**). Timber is sold
as stumpage at year 50.

**Cumulative cash, base case.** About −$1,350 by year 4. Nuts and maintenance roughly
cancel, so the position drifts to about −$4,400 by year 50 (undiscounted). **Payback
comes only at the year-50 timber sale.** At a 5 % discount rate the base case NPV is
about $0; at 6 % it is −$521/ac.

**Nuts alone do not pay.** At $0.16/lb, hand pickup costs more than the station pays,
which matches the USDA note that harvest can take half or more of nut income at the
older, higher prices. Nut pickup is worthwhile only with family or volunteer labor, or as
recreation.

**Versus dryland sorghum.** Sorghum nets ~$80/ac over direct costs at county-trend yield
and price, and $295/ac at the K-State base (`grain-sorghum.md`). A walnut acre's
annualized −$33 to +$36 forgoes that every year for 50 years. **Never plant walnut on a
productive crop acre.** Use only riparian or odd acres where sorghum nets less than
zero, and only with cost-share.

## 4. Raw-crop market
- **Hammons Products Co.** (Stockton, MO) is effectively the only commercial buyer of
  wild black walnuts. It runs seven hulling stations in Kansas (KSNT 2025), mostly in
  eastern Kansas. The nearest station to Sedgwick County is **unknown; verify** at
  black-walnuts.com in September. 2025 prices were $13 opening, then $14, then $16 per 100
  lb hulled, with the season running Oct 1 to Nov 30. The price is set annually by
  Hammons. There is no contract, and volume is limited only by station hours.
- **Timber buyers.** Veneer and sawlog buyers from Missouri and the eastern Kansas
  sawmills buy standing timber by sealed bid. The Kansas Forest Service district
  forester can mark and appraise trees (**verify** service and fees).
- **Volatility.** The nut price is low and stable (roughly $13–20/cwt over recent
  years; **verify** history). Veneer prices swing with export demand.

## 6. Risks and disqualifiers
1. **Trust horizon (top disqualifier).** Value arrives in 40–80 years, to the remainder
   beneficiaries. Trustee approval and an impartiality analysis are needed, and the
   trustee signs any timber sale.
2. Opportunity cost: 50 years of forgone crop net on any productive acre.
3. Site: upland soils produce poor logs.
4. Single nut buyer, with a price below the cost of harvest labor.
5. Thousand cankers disease, anthracnose, deer, fire, storm and wind damage over a long
   rotation. Timber theft (walnut poaching).
6. Juglone injury to adjacent alfalfa and produce.

## 7. Decision rules
1. Plant black walnut only on riparian or odd acres with no crop value (sorghum net
   below $0/ac), and only with NRCS or Kansas Forest Service cost-share that covers at
   least half of establishment (**verify** programs).
2. Do not plant for nuts. Pick up nuts only if family labor wants it.
3. Pursue no nutmeat product.
4. If mature walnut trees already exist on trust land, get a Kansas Forest Service
   appraisal before any clearing. They may be worth more than the crop ground they shade.
