# Dry field peas (yellow and green)

## 1. Fit for this farm
- **Climate: poor to marginal.**
  - Field pea (Pisum sativum) is cool-season. K-State's pea work centers on northwest
    Kansas, where peas replace summer fallow in wheat systems.
  - K-State abandoned its Garden City site because heat stress severely limited yields
    (SRP1142). An older K-State bulletin calls peas "not well adapted" to western Kansas.
  - South-central Kansas is wetter but hotter in May–June, when spring peas flower and
    fill. Expect heat-shortened fill and disease (Ascochyta/Mycosphaerella, powdery mildew).
- **Winter (fall-seeded) peas**: Austrian-type forage peas are winterhardy here as a cover
  crop. Grain-type winter peas exist, but no south-central Kansas grain yields were found
  (verify).
- **Season**: spring pea planted late February to March, harvest late June to early July.
  That is **the same window as wheat harvest**, on the farm's single combine.
- **Rotation**:
  - Peas fit before sorghum or double-crop soybeans, or as a fallow replacement.
  - This farm is not in a fallow system, so the northwest-Kansas "peas instead of fallow"
    economics do not transfer.
  - Peas use about 3.5 in more water than fallow. Wheat after peas yields 4–12 bu/ac less
    than wheat after fallow (K-State).
  - Atrazine plant-back applies.
- **Equipment**:
  - Drill or planter: fine.
  - Harvest: pods are low and the crop lodges, so a flex or draper header is needed.
  - Handling: peas crack in augers.
- **Trust and labor**: no issue. Low labor.

## 2. Agronomy summary
- **Varieties**: yellow (e.g., Agassiz-type), green, and Austrian winter forage peas.
  Northwest-Kansas trial varieties are in SRP1142 and the WKREC reports (not read).
- **Planting**:
  - Seeding rate about 160–200 lb/ac (estimate, verify): high, because the seed is large.
  - Inoculate with pea/vetch Rhizobium.
- **Fertility**: P by soil test. Little N.
- **Weeds**: PRE pendimethalin or sulfentrazone, as labeled. Weak POST options.
- **Disease**: Ascochyta complex, powdery mildew, root rots (Aphanomyces, Fusarium).
- **Harvest**: desiccate if uneven. Harvest at 13–16% moisture. Low pods, so cut close.

  depends on early pea harvest.

## 4. Raw-crop market
- No pea processor or pulse buyer within reach of Sedgwick County was found. Pea protein
  and pulse-splitting plants, and the export trade, sit in the northern Plains and Canada
  (verify any Kansas or Nebraska buyer).
- Local options: livestock feed (rations, mills) at feed value; cover-crop seed companies.
- US 2026 production: 20.9 million cwt on 1.08 million ac. Prices are driven by Indian
  import policy (CMB News 2026) and Chinese and Canadian supply.

## 5. Finished products
The default LLC structure applies. Raw crop is charged at 1,200 lb × $0.083 = $100 per acre-
equivalent. Labor is at FAMILY $11/hr. Uplift follows the schema (LLC net minus raw net of
-$29).

### 5a. Pea protein contract (assessment)
- **Process**: sell yellow peas to a fractionation plant (protein isolate and starch).
  Plants are in Canada and the northern US.
- **Price**: at best northern bids ($0.10/lb used here) less long freight ($0.04/lb,
  estimate).
- **Margin**: $120 − $48 − $11 − $100 = **-$39**. Schema uplift -$10.
- **Verdict: skip.** No plant within reach, and a commodity price with no premium. Building
  protein fractionation is far beyond stage 1.

### 5b. Green or yellow split peas, 1–2 lb bags
- **Process**: dehull and split (pulse splitter), then clean, polish and bag. Out-turn about
  80–85%.
- **Capital**: about $25,000 (small dehuller and splitter, cleaner; not priced, verify).
- **Labor**: 50 hr per acre-equivalent.
- **Price**: $3.50/lb blended (estimate). Grocery split peas are likely lower; not priced,
  verify.
- **Margin**: $3,500 − $700 − $550 − $100 − $1,167 ($3,500 ÷ 3 ac) = **$983**. Schema uplift
  $1,012.
- **Regulatory**: KDA license; FSMA qualified facility.
- **Market**: about 3 acres. Split pea soup is a niche, winter-seasonal product.
- **Verdict: skip.** The raw crop does not fit, and buying peas to split locally adds
  capital to a tiny market.

### 5c. Cover-crop seed (forage or Austrian winter pea), cleaned and tagged
- **Process**: grow winter peas (fall-seeded; winterhardy here as a cover). Clean,
  germination-test and tag under the Kansas Seed Law.
- **Capital**: about $8,000, shared cleaner.
- **Labor**: 12 hr.
- **Price**: $0.45/lb wholesale (estimate, verify against cover-crop seed price lists).
- **Margin**: $495 − $66 − $132 − $100 − $75 = **$122**. Schema uplift $151.
- **Regulatory**: Kansas Seed Law labeler registration; germination test; no PVP-protected
  varieties.
- **Market**: about 20 acres.
- **Verdict: test** as the only plausible pea enterprise. The winter pea is cut in June,
  which also clashes with wheat harvest.

## 6. Risks and disqualifiers
- **Top disqualifiers**:
  - Heat stress during fill. K-State abandoned its pea trials in southwest Kansas for this
    reason.
  - No buyer within reach.
  - Harvest coincides with wheat harvest on the farm's one combine.
- **Water**: wheat yield penalty from pea water use.
- **Harvest losses**: lodging and low pods.

## 7. Decision rules
1. Do not plant grain peas for sale. Revisit only if K-State publishes south-central Kansas
   yields of 30+ bu/ac and a buyer within 200 miles appears.
2. Winter pea seed production only against pre-sold cover-crop seed orders, and only if
   wheat harvest logistics allow (custom harvest or a staggered harvest).
