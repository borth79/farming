# Aronia, haskap and thornless blackberries (hardy berries)

## 1. Fit for this farm
| Crop | Fit verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Thornless blackberry** (Arkansas floricane and primocane types) | **best fit of the berries; test 1 acre** | Bred in Arkansas for southern heat; Oklahoma State reports strong yields; short-lived trellis (8–12 yr); Wichita metro retail and u-pick demand; fast payback |
| **Aronia** (*Aronia melanocarpa*, 'Viking', 'Nero') | skip | Very hardy and tolerant of heat and soils, but the market has collapsed; astringent fruit sells only as juice or powder; growers are leaving the market |
| **Haskap / honeyberry** | skip | Heat-limited above zone 7 (Sedgwick County is 6b–7a with 100 °F+ summers); Kansas growers report failures from sun and heat; no trial data |
| Other (currants, gooseberries, raspberries) | skip | Raspberries and currants suffer in Kansas summer heat; not researched further |

- **Soil and water.** Blackberries need well-drained soil (Phytophthora root rot on wet
  clay) and drip irrigation; raised beds on heavy soils. Irrigation status is unconfirmed.
- **Equipment.** The tractor handles row middles. A small airblast or backpack sprayer
  is needed for SWD and cane diseases; the grain sprayer is too large. Hand harvest. The
  combine has no role. Mechanical blackberry harvesters exist but suit processing fruit
  at scale only.
- **Herbicide drift.** Blackberries and aronia are broadleaf. 2,4-D and dicamba drift
  from wheat, sorghum and pasture is a real risk.
- **Labor (the binding constraint).** Blackberry harvest is every 2–3 days from mid-June
  to late July. At ~15 lb/hr picked and packed (estimate), 7,000 lb takes ~470 hr/ac.
  With no dedicated labor this needs hired pickers, or u-pick customers doing the work.
- **Perennial and trust.** **Blackberries are an 8–12 year planting with a trellis and
  drip, and aronia a 15–20 year planting.** Both are improvements on trust land and need
  written trustee approval and a lease term covering payback plus the planting life
  (blackberry base payback ~year 3–4; aronia ~year 12). U-pick brings the public onto
  trust land. Operate it through the LLC with liability insurance, Kansas agritourism
  registration and trustee consent. It may be restricted by trust terms (non-farm or
  retail use) or by county zoning.

## 2. Agronomy summary
- **Blackberry varieties.** Floricane thornless: Natchez, Ouachita, Ponca, Caddo,
  Osage, Navaho (low establishment from root cuttings). Primocane thornless: Prime-Ark
  Freedom and Prime-Ark Traveler, for a second fall crop (fall heat limits this).
  Plant in spring, 3 ft in-row, 10–12 ft rows (~1,200–1,450 plants/ac), on a T-trellis
  or rotatable-cross-arm trellis.
- **Blackberry management.** Summer-tip primocanes. Remove spent floricanes after
  harvest. Pests: SWD (spray program at ripening), rednecked cane borer, orange rust
  (rogue infected plants), rosette or double blossom, and Japanese beetle. Shade cloth
  is an option against sunscald.
- **Blackberry bearing.** Light crop in year 2, full from year 3. Productive 8–12 years.
- **Aronia.** Plant at 3–4 ft × 10–12 ft. First crop in year 3, full in year 5.
  Mechanical harvest with a blueberry or currant harvester. Few pests. Mature yields are
  often quoted at 8,000–12,000 lb/ac (**unsourced in this pass; verify**).
- **Haskap.** Blooms in very early spring, so frost risk. Fruit ripens in May–June.
  Japanese types are better for mild climates. Would need afternoon shade in Kansas.

## 3. Yield and raw-crop economics
Basis: 1 acre, 2026 dollars, hand labor at HIRED_SEASONAL $18/hr.
- **Blackberry.** Establishment $6,500/ac (plants ~1,210 at ~$2.50, T-trellis ~$1,800,
  drip $1,200, prep and mulch $500; estimate). Yield 4,200 lb in year 2, 7,000 lb/ac
  mature (UTK 5,000 qt × ~1.4 lb/qt, **verify** conversion; well below the OSU 20,000 lb
  maximum). Cultural costs $2,500/ac/yr (pruning, training, sprays, irrigation, cooler,
  haul). Pick and pack $1.45/lb (picking $1.20 plus clamshells $0.25). Raw sale:
  wholesale flats to grocers, restaurants or a distributor at $2.50/lb (**estimate;
  verify** with buyers). Amortized at 6 % over 10 years ($883/ac/yr).
- **Aronia.** Establishment $5,100 over 2 years (estimate). Yield 8,000 lb mature
  (unsourced). Price $0.60/lb (Wisconsin range $0.50–1.25). Cultural $900/yr. Harvest
  and haul $0.40/lb. Amortized at 6 % over 18 years ($471).
- **Haskap.** Not modeled: no yield data for Kansas.

**Versus dryland sorghum.** Sorghum nets ~$80/ac over direct costs (county trend) and

times higher, but it is a produce business needing ~500 hours and a sales channel per
acre. Aronia base ($229) is about the same as sorghum, with far more risk.

## 4. Raw-crop market
- **Blackberries.** Wichita metro (~650k) grocers with local programs (Dillons local,
  Natural Grocers, GreenAcres; see `knowledge/markets/specialty-buyer-network.md`),
  restaurants, farmers' markets (Old Town, Kansas Grown), and roadside stands. Shipped
  California and Mexican berries set grocery prices year-round. A local premium depends
  on freshness. Competitor: Elderslie Farm (Kechi) grows blackberries for its own
  restaurant and market. A wholesale ceiling of ~2–3 acres is an estimate.
- **Aronia.** No buyer within reach of Sedgwick County was found. The Midwest Aronia
  Association region (Iowa, Nebraska) has reported payment delays and exits. The market
  is effectively closed to a new grower.
- **Haskap.** No market or supply in Kansas.

## 6. Risks and disqualifiers
1. **Labor (top disqualifier).** ~470 hr/ac for picked fruit. U-pick shifts the work to
   customers but brings public liability.
2. **Trust.** Trellis and drip improvements, and u-pick public access, on trust land.
   Both need trustee approval. Retail and agritourism must run in the LLC.
3. SWD, late spring freezes on floricane blooms, 100 °F+ sunscald, hail.
4. Phytophthora on heavy wet soils. Orange rust and rosette.
5. Drift from 2,4-D and dicamba.
6. Aronia: no market. Haskap: no climate fit.

## 7. Decision rules
1. Blackberry only. Start with ≤ 1 acre on drip near the farmstead, with a road, parking
   and a buffer from sprayed fields.
2. Only proceed with u-pick after: trustee approval for public access, Sedgwick County
   zoning confirmation, Kansas agritourism registration, and an LLC liability policy.
3. Price u-pick and pints from an on-site survey in June 2027 (Elderslie, farmers'
   markets, grocery) before a second acre.
4. Do not plant aronia unless a buyer contracts ≥ $1.00/lb for five years. Do not plant
   haskap.
