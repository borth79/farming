# Mung beans (mungbean)

## 1. Fit for this farm
- **Climate: good.** Mung (Vigna radiata) is a warm-season, heat- and drought-tolerant
  legume of about 75–90 days. Oklahoma has been the US center of production since the
  1940s. JLee Co. now contracts growers "from Fort Cobb north to the Kansas border", about
  60–100 miles south of Sedgwick County. The crop is adapted to south-central Kansas heat.
  Frost ends the season, which limits late double-crop planting.
- **Season**: full-season planting late May to June. Double-crop after wheat in late June
  to early July (JLee: harvest in time to plant wheat or rye after). The double-crop window
  is like double-crop soybeans (`knowledge/crops/soybeans-double-crop.md`).
- **Soil**: well-drained. Moderate pH. Not tolerant of waterlogging.
- **Rotation**:
  - Atrazine carryover: assume no mung the year after atrazine (Vigna is
    triazine-sensitive; verify the label rotational crop list). Mung fits after wheat, not
    after sorghum.
  - It breaks soybean cyst nematode cycles less than sorghum does. Mung is a host of some
    soybean pests (stink bugs, pod feeders).
- **Equipment**:
  - Planting: the existing row planter (with a soybean plate or small-bean plate) or a
    drill.
  - Harvest: direct-combine with a flex or draper header after desiccation. Pods are near
    canopy top. Uneven maturity and shatter are the harvest risks.
  - Small seed: needs a tight combine and clean bins.
- **Trust and labor**: growing is agriculture under the contract. Little extra labor.
- **Commitment**: annual. Can be tested on 20–80 acres if a contract is available.

## 2. Agronomy summary
- **Varieties**: Kimlee is the variety under JLee contract; the seed is supplied by the
  contractor. OSU-bred OK2000 and older public lines exist. Variety comes with the
  contract.
- **Planting**:
  - Soil at 65 °F or above.
  - Seeding rate 15–25 lb/ac (estimate; verify with the contractor).
  - Rows of 15–30 in.
  - Inoculate with cowpea-group Bradyrhizobium.
- **Fertility**: low N need. P by soil test.
- **Weeds**: few labeled herbicides. PRE options are pendimethalin and S-metolachlor (label
  verify), with a POST grass herbicide. Palmer amaranth is the main threat.
- **Insects**: stink bugs, bean leaf beetle, pod worms. Scout from pod set.
- **Disease**: Cercospora, powdery mildew, charcoal rot in drought.
- **Harvest**:
  - Desiccate when 60–90% of pods are mature (contractor protocol).
  - Combine at low cylinder speed. Food grade needs minimal splits and stained seed.
  - Deliver to the contractor's plant. Haul is about 130 miles to Hennessey or about 210
    miles to Apache (estimate, verify).

## 4. Raw-crop market
- **Verified**:
  - JLee Co. (Hennessey, OK) contracts growers for food-grade Kimlee mung beans and is
    expanding its plant for food manufacturers with higher food-safety standards.
  - It exports to South Korea (1.5 million lb reported; year to verify).
  - Apache Seed & Supply (Apache, OK) also contracts mung beans and has its own
    processing.
- **Not verified**: the contract price, acreage caps, whether Kansas growers outside the
  stated region are accepted, delivery terms, quality discounts, and payment timing.
- **Contract form**: acreage contracts at a set price, paid per pound, with a non-GMO
  premium (HPJ 2023).
- **Volume ceiling**: the contractor's demand. "If we had ten million pounds we could sell
  them" (undated quote), which is roughly 10,000 acres at 1,000 lb/ac. A new out-of-region
  grower would likely start with 50–300 acres (estimate).
- **Competitors**: Oklahoma contract growers; imports, which supply most US consumption
  (market-report estimate ~90%; low reliability).
- **Price history**: not available.

## 5. Finished products
The default LLC structure applies. Raw crop is charged at 900 lb × $0.29 = $261 per acre-
equivalent. Labor is at FAMILY $11/hr. Uplift follows the schema (LLC net minus raw net of
$129).

### 5a. Sprouting seed for commercial sprout growers
- **Process**:
  - Grow under seed-for-sprouting practices: no manure, field history records, animal
    intrusion controls, clean harvest and storage (FDA 2023 draft guidance for seed growers).
  - Clean, size, gravity-separate and color-sort.
  - Test germination and pathogens (Salmonella, E. coli O157) per lot.
  - Bag in 50 lb lots with traceability.
- **Capital**: about $25,000.
  - Air-screen cleaner: $0.5–6k used.
  - Gravity table: price not found, verify.
  - Color sorter: $2–15k.
  - Bins and bagging.
- **Labor**: 12 hr per acre-equivalent.
- **Price**: wholesale to commercial sprouters $1.25/lb (estimate, verify).
  - Retail sprouting seed runs $3.80–11.95/lb (True Leaf, Johnny's). Most is organic, which
    this farm is not.
- **Margin**: $1,000 − $168 (testing, bags, freight) − $132 − $261 − $150 ($6,000 ÷ 40 ac) =
  **$289**. Schema uplift $160.
- **Regulatory**:
  - Sprout operations must treat seed or rely on supplier treatment under 21 CFR 112
    Subpart M.
  - Seed growers are expected to follow FDA 2023 guidance.
  - Sprout outbreaks have been traced to seed lots, so product liability and recall
    insurance are essential.
- **Competitors**: established sprouting-seed suppliers (organic, Australian, Chinese and
  Myanmar imports).
- **Market**: few commercial sprouters regionally. Estimated 40 acres if one or two buyers
  sign.
- **Verdict: test.** Call commercial sprouters to learn spec and price. Liability is the
  limiting factor. Do it only with a written spec and insurance.

### 5b. Fresh mung bean sprouts (LLC grows sprouts for Wichita grocers and restaurants)
- **Process**: seed disinfection, controlled-environment sprouting drums, and per-batch
  testing of spent irrigation water. Cold pack and refrigerated delivery.
- **Capital**: about $40,000 (cold room, drums, water treatment, lab setup).
- **Labor**: 112 hr per acre-equivalent.
- **Output**: about 7 lb sprouts per lb seed (estimate), so 5,600 lb per acre-equivalent.
- **Price**: $1.40/lb wholesale (estimate).
- **Margin**: $7,840 − $1,960 − $1,232 − $261 − $7,500 ($15,000 ÷ 2 ac) = **-$3,113**.
- **Regulatory**: the highest food-safety burden of any product in this group (Subpart M;
  environmental Listeria monitoring).
- **Market**: Wichita Asian grocers. Imported sprouts from regional sprouters are cheap.
- **Verdict: skip.**

### 5c. Dehulled split mung (moong dal) to Indian grocers
- **Process**: dehull and split (dal mill), then clean and bag. Out-turn about 75%.
- **Capital**: about $20,000 (verify).
- **Labor**: 20 hr.
- **Price**: $1.75/lb wholesale (estimate, verify against Wichita Indian-grocery shelf
  prices).
- **Margin**: $1,181 − $135 − $220 − $261 − $400 = **$165**. Schema uplift $36.
- **Competitors**: imported dal at low prices.
- **Verdict: skip.**

### 5d. Cleaned food-grade whole mung direct to importers or distributors
- **Process**: the farm or LLC cleans and bags in 25 kg or 50 lb bags and sells around the
  contractor.
- **Price**: $0.50/lb (export unit value benchmark).
- **Margin**: $410 − $49 − $66 − $261 − $75 = **-$41**. Schema uplift -$170.
- **Verdict: skip.** The contractor already does this. Selling around the contractor needs
  food-safety certification and export logistics. Also, the Kimlee variety is exclusive to
  JLee.

## 6. Risks and disqualifiers
- **Top disqualifier: the contract price and eligibility are unknown.** The economics swing
  from about $40/ac to about $330/ac on that one number.
- **Single-buyer dependence**: JLee holds exclusive Kimlee rights. The only alternative is
  Apache.
- **No crop insurance** (verify).
- **Atrazine plant-back.**
- **Few herbicides; Palmer amaranth risk.**
- **Harvest**: shatter and uneven maturity; small seed lost from a loose combine.
- **Freight**: 130–210 miles to the plant.
- **Harvest timing**: full-season mung harvest in late August to September overlaps sorghum
  and soybean harvest on one combine.

## 7. Decision rules
1. Plant mung only under a signed contract stating the price per lb, seed supply, acreage,
   delivery point, quality discounts and payment date.
2. Grow full-season mung on post-wheat fields free of atrazine. Grow double-crop mung only
   where its expected return beats double-crop soybeans at that year's prices.
3. Start with 40–80 acres for 2 seasons. Expand only if the realized net beats sorghum by
   at least $40/ac in both years.
4. Sprouting-seed sales only after an LLC food-safety plan, insurance and a written buyer
   spec.
