# Honey and pollination

> Unit basis: bees use no dedicated acres. In this file and in the CSV the
> "acre-equivalent" is **one colony per year** (`max_acres_by_market` = colonies).
> Honey adds to the farm's sunflower, alfalfa, clover and cover crop acres; it does not
> replace sorghum, so the sorghum comparison ($79/ac) does not apply directly.

## 1. Fit for this farm
- **Forage**: sunflower (July–August), alfalfa (if grown and allowed to bloom), sweet
  clover and native forbs on field edges and waterways, cover crops with legumes or
  brassicas, plus Wichita-area urban forage. Summer dearth after wheat harvest and drought
  years cut yields; Kansas 2023 averaged 52 lb/colony.
- **Pesticide conflict**: the farm sprays sunflowers (head moth, stem weevil), sorghum
  (sugarcane aphid) and alfalfa (weevil). Hives on the farm require dusk spraying,
  bee-safer products and advance notice (`sunflower.md`). Neighbors' spraying is the
  bigger uncontrolled risk.
- **Equipment**: none of the grain equipment helps; needs hives, protective gear, an
  extractor and bottling setup, a truck for moving hives.
- **Labor**: ~10 hr/colony/yr at small scale (estimate, verify) spread April–October with
  peaks at swarm season (Apr–May) and extraction (Jul–Sep). Skill matters more than hours:
  varroa management decides survival.
- **Trust**: keeping bees is agricultural use; low risk. HB 2158 requires honey to be
  packaged "on the harvesting beekeeper's property" for the resale exemption — if the LLC
  is the beekeeper, package on LLC-held (leased, off-trust) premises; confirm with counsel
  whether trust land can serve without breaching the default structure.

## 3. Yield and raw-product economics (per colony)
| Case | lb honey/colony | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Adverse | 20 | drought, dearth |
| Base | 50 | Kansas 2023 52 lb; US 2025 48 lb |
| Favorable | 80 | good sunflower flow |

Raw sale = bulk honey in drums or pails to a packer at $2.45/lb (2025 co-op/private, verify).
Annual colony cost ≈ $150 (varroa treatment ~$15, feed ~$20, replacing ~40% losses at ~$200
per nuc ≈ $80, equipment wear ~$20, fuel and supplies ~$15 — all estimates) plus 10 hr labor
($110). **Raw net ≈ −$138 per colony** (adverse ≈ −$211, favorable ≈ −$64). Bulk honey does
not pay at this scale; only retail products do.

## 4. Raw-product market
- Bulk packers and larger beekeepers buy drums at ~$2.45–3.05/lb (2025), unlimited volume
  but uneconomic for a small apiary.
- Retail market: Wichita has many hobby and sideline beekeepers and local brands ((316)
  Honey at HomeGrown, farmers market vendors); local raw honey is a crowded category with a
  price ceiling near $10–14/lb.
- Pollination: Kansas Region 1 rates not retrieved; Kansas sunflower placements $30–55/hive
  (vendor, unverified); local produce growers rent at $60–100 (pumpkin file). Almonds pay
  $209/colony (2025) but need migratory trucking — not a fit.
- Nucs: strong spring demand from new beekeepers; Kansas asking prices $180–275.

## 5. Finished products
Default: the LLC owns the colonies and the honey house off trust land, places hives on the
trust farm under a written apiary-site agreement (rent or honey share). Convention: uplift =
LLC margin per colony; labor at FAMILY $11 (HIRED $18 shown). Raw honey enters each product
at $2.45/lb × 50 lb = $122.50 per colony.

**(a) Raw honey in 1-lb jars.** 50 jars/colony at $11 (below the $12.35 2026 survey average
to win shelf space in a crowded Wichita market — verify); jars and labels $1.00; 7.5 hr
bottling and selling; capital $2,500 (extractor, uncapping tank, bottling bucket, share of a
small honey room); fixed $800/yr over 20 colonies. Margin ≈ **$255/colony** (HIRED ≈ $202);
whole-chain ≈ $118/colony. Market ≈ 3,000 jars/yr ≈ 60 colonies (verify). Regulatory: direct
to consumer, no KDA license; retail resale under the HB 2158 exemption (≤ $35,000 honey
sales/yr, KDA sanitary standards, label disclaimer, Kansas hives, packed on the beekeeper's
property) or else a KDA food processing license; Kansas sales tax. Verdict: **test** with
4–10 colonies; the base product.

**(b) Cut comb honey.** ~30 lb comb/colony (comb supers yield less than extracted — verify)
at $16 (2026 survey $16.95; NebGuide $15–30); $1.50 packaging; 6 hr; capital $1,000 (comb
cassettes or cut-comb supers). Margin ≈ **$232/colony** (HIRED ≈ $190); market ≈ 20 colonies'
worth. HB 2158 covers honeycomb. Verdict: **test** — less equipment than extraction.

**(c) Creamed honey.** Controlled crystallization of liquid honey with seed crystals, 50 lb
at $14 (survey $14.70); $1.20 jar; 10 hr; capital $1,500 (cool room or temperature control).
Margin ≈ **$378/colony** (HIRED ≈ $308); market ≈ 10 colonies' worth (a line extension of a).
Creaming may count as "altering" honey under HB 2158 — if so, resale needs a KDA license
(verify). Verdict: **test** after (a) sells.

**(d) Beeswax candles.** ~1 lb cappings wax/colony (verify) → 4 × 4-oz candles at $10; wax
valued at $7/lb bulk. Margin ≈ **$11/colony**. Verdict: **skip** as a business; make a few for
the market table.

**(e) Local pollination placements.** Rent colonies to Wichita-area produce growers (pumpkins,
melons, cucurbits) and possibly hybrid sunflower or alfalfa seed growers: 2 placements ×
$60 (pumpkin file range $60–100; Kansas sunflower $30–55 unverified), $10 moving cost, 2 hr.
Margin ≈ **$78/colony** on top of the honey crop (pesticide exposure and moving stress may
reduce honey — not costed). Written contract with spray-notice clause. Verdict: **test** with
the farm's own pumpkin plots first.

**(f) 5-frame nucleus colonies.** Split strong colonies in spring: 1.5 nucs per parent at
$200 (Kansas $180–230; Salina seller $225); $80/nuc for mated queen and nuc box; 6 hr; half
the parent's honey forgone ($61). Margin ≈ **$43/colony** (≈ $1 at HIRED). Verdict: **test**
only after the apiary survives two winters with < 30% loss; doubles as loss replacement.

pumpkins, melons and sunflowers.

## 6. Risks and disqualifiers
- **Colony losses** (varroa, viruses, drought, pesticides) — the top risk; a 50% loss year
  wipes out the margin.
- Pesticide exposure from the farm's and neighbors' spraying of sunflower, sorghum and
  alfalfa.
- Crowded Wichita local-honey market; price competition from hobbyists.
- HB 2158 cap ($35,000) and conditions; creamed or infused honey may fall outside it.
- Stings and public liability at markets and pollination sites; family members with
  allergies.

## 7. Decision rules
1. Start with 4–10 colonies under one family member who completes a beekeeping course;
   no expansion until two winters with < 30% loss.
2. Spray program: sunflower and sorghum insecticide applications at dusk with 48-hour notice
   to the beekeeper; register hives on any pesticide-sensitive crop registry available in
   Kansas (verify; e.g. FieldWatch/BeeCheck).
3. Sell retail only (jars, comb, creamed); never sell bulk at packer prices except to clear
   a surplus.
4. Grow toward 20–40 colonies only if jar sales exceed 1,500/yr at ≥ $10.
