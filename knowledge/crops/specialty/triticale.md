# Triticale

> Research note (2026-11-23): fetch blocked; figures from search summaries. Net per acre =
> gross revenue minus direct costs, excluding land and machinery ownership.

## 1. Fit for this farm
- **Climate.** Winter triticale (wheat x rye) is hardy and drought-tolerant and grows well
  in Kansas. Its Kansas role is forage: fall and spring grazing, hay, silage (MF2227).
- **Rotation.** Same slot as wheat. Shares wheat diseases; volunteer triticale in later
  wheat is a contaminant (like rye, a grading problem).
- **Equipment.** Drill, sprayer and combine fit. For hay the farm lacks a swather, rake
  and baler; custom haying is required. Thick stems wilt slowly (Kansas Forage Task Force),
  raising rain-damage risk.
- **Competition on the farm.** Wheat grazing and dual-purpose wheat
  (`wheat-grazing-and-dual-purpose.md`) already fill the winter-forage role with the
  farm's anchor crop; triticale must beat that, not sorghum.
- **Trust and labor.** All agricultural; low labor.
- **Commitment.** Annual.

## 2. Agronomy summary
- Varieties: forage types (e.g., Thundercale, NE-bred forage triticales) and grain types;
  many are PVP-protected (**verify** names and status against K-State 2025 forage tests).
- Planting: mid-September to mid-October (earlier for fall grazing); 90–120 lb/ac.
- Fertility: 60–90 lb N/ac for forage; less for grain.
- Pests: rust, barley yellow dwarf; ergot occasionally (rye parent).
- Harvest: hay at boot to early heading for quality; grain in June (test weight 50 lb/bu).

## 4. Raw-crop market
- Grain: local cattle feeders and feed mills at a discount to wheat; no elevator bids found.
- Hay: Kansas small-grain hay (oat hay $155–165 per large square ton-basis, HPJ
  2026-11-19; **verify** unit) sells to cow-calf and stocker operators; triticale hay
  quality is lower than oat or wheat hay.
- Seed: forage and cover-crop seed dealers.

## 5. Finished products
Default structure: products are farm sales except seed labeling (registered entity) and
any distilling sale. Raw value $4.00/bu x 50 = $200/ac. FAMILY $11/hr; HIRED $18/hr.

**T1. Triticale hay, large round bales, custom-baled.** No capital (custom swath, rake,
bale ~$75/ac net of grain harvest costs saved, **verify** against K-State custom rates
2026). 2.5 t/ac at $120/ton (**verify**; between bluestem $95 and oat hay $155–165).
Labor 1 hr. Uplift **$14/ac** over selling grain; ~$64 at $140/ton. Ceiling ~50 acres.
Verdict: **test** only with a named hay buyer, and compare against grazing the same acre

**T2. Triticale grain to distilleries.** $1.50/bu premium over $4.00 (**verify**; no

## 6. Risks and disqualifiers
- **Top disqualifier: no grain market, and wheat grazing already fills the forage role.**
- Slow hay drying; lower forage quality than wheat or oats.
- Volunteer triticale in later wheat; PVP limits on seed sales.

## 7. Decision rules
1. Grow triticale only as forage or seed against a named buyer; never for grain on spec.
2. Choose triticale over dual-purpose wheat for grazing only if K-State forage trials show
   at least 20% more fall-plus-spring forage at equal quality in this region.
3. Seed sales only from public or licensed varieties with germination tests.
