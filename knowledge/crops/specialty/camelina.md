# Camelina

## 1. Fit for this farm
- **Season:** winter camelina is seeded mid-September to mid-October. It is very winter-hardy and
  is harvested in late May to June in Kansas (earlier than wheat; **verify** local maturity). That
  leaves time to double-crop soybeans or sorghum the same summer. The real comparison is therefore
  "camelina plus a double crop" against "wheat plus a double crop" or "fallow then full-season
  sorghum", not camelina against sorghum alone.
- **Water:** shallow-rooted and fast. It uses less water than wheat in a wheat–fallow system (Hays
  work found wheat and sorghum yields were unaffected), and it fits the dryland, drought-exposed
  side of the farm.
- **Equipment:** drill (owned; very small seed, 3–6 lb/ac, shallow placement); combine (owned; seal
  for a tiny seed, fine sieves, low fan); sprayer (owned). No new equipment for the raw crop.
- **Herbicides:** limited labeled options. Camelina is sensitive to many residuals used in wheat and
  sorghum: SU and imidazolinone carryover, atrazine (**verify** each plant-back). Brassica weeds
  and volunteer wheat are the main competition.
- **Trust and labor:** raw crop under contract is ordinary agriculture, with a grain-crop workload.
- **Gating problem:** no confirmed camelina contract serves south-central Kansas in 2026. Known
  programs are in western Kansas and eastern Colorado (Sustainable Oils, older), the Upper Midwest
  (Cargill) and the Northern Plains (Bayer–bp). Without a buyer there is no crop: camelina "can't be
  sold on the open market" (Producer.com).

## 2. Agronomy summary
- **Varieties:** winter types (e.g. Joelle and newer contract lines from Sustainable Oils or Syngenta,
  Cargill and Bayer; ARS EF-9 early-maturing). Contract programs supply proprietary seed.
  Non-contract winter camelina is sold as cover-crop seed (Millborn, Albert Lea, Welter).
- **Planting:** mid-September to mid-October, 3–6 lb/ac, ≤0.25 in deep, firm seedbed or no-till into
  soybean or sorghum stubble.
- **Fertility:** 40–60 lb N in early spring. The Obour 2022 double-crop study roughly doubled yield
  with N (556 → 1,157 kg/ha).
- **Pests and diseases:** few. Downy mildew in wet springs; flea beetle on fall stands (verify).
- **Harvest:** direct combine at <10% moisture. Pods resist shatter better than canola, but timing
  still matters. Clean and store dry (≤8%).

- **Yield basis:** Hays 754 lb/ac (wheat–camelina); double-crop winter camelina ~500–1,030 lb/ac
  (Obour 2022); commercial farms 800–1,000 lb/ac; pilots 400–1,800.
- **Price basis:** Sustainable Oils western Kansas ~$0.25–0.30/lb (2024). The Cargill formula (July
  soybean futures + $1.00–1.50/bu, 50-lb bushel) gives ~$0.26–0.27/lb at $12 soybeans. Freight to
  a distant crusher or receiving point is the unknown. If the nearest delivery point is western
  Kansas or Colorado, subtract an estimated $0.02–0.04/lb (**verify**).
- **Direct cost (ESTIMATE):**
  - seed: $20–30, or $0 if the contractor supplies it
  - N: $25–30
  - burndown and herbicide: $15
  - planting and spraying fuel and repairs: $30
  - harvest fuel and repairs: $30
  - hauling: $20–40
  - insurance: none assumed (RMA camelina coverage outside the Northern Plains **verify**)
  - interest: $5
- **Versus dryland sorghum:** sorghum nets ~**$79/ac** over direct (`grain-sorghum.md`). Base
  camelina ($66) is close, and camelina occupies the field only October to early June. With a
  double-crop soybean or sorghum crop netting even $30–60/ac in an average year (estimate; see
  the soybean double-crop file), the system can beat a single full-season sorghum crop. The fair
  comparison is against wheat–double-crop. Wheat's net was not computed here; the orchestrator
  should run it.
- **45Z and SAF angle:** the §45Z credit is claimed by the fuel producer, not the farmer. Growers see
  it only as a contract premium or a carbon-intensity bonus. Since 2026, 45Z requires
  North-American-grown feedstock, which favors domestic camelina over imported used cooking oil
  and tallow. Camelina's low land-use carbon intensity (as an intermediate crop) is its selling
  point. Final 45Z rules (expected about Nov 2026) and the CI score for camelina oil set how much
  premium the crusher can pass through. Treat any premium as upside, not base.

## 4. Raw-crop market
- **Contractors** (none confirmed for Sedgwick County, 2026–2027):
  - Sustainable Oils, with Syngenta seed (western Kansas and eastern Colorado; southern variety
    work)
  - Cargill (Upper Midwest, 12,000 ac)
  - Bayer–bp (Northern Plains, 2026)
- **Contract terms:** typically Act-of-God, 100% of production, seed supplied or sold under contract,
  price formula off soybeans, and delivery to the buyer's point.
- **Volume ceiling:** whatever a contractor offers. **Zero** until one does.
- **Price history:** tied to soy complex and renewable-diesel/SAF policy (RFS volumes, 45Z, state
  LCFS). The policy dependence is high.

## 5. Finished products
Default structure: an LLC off trust land buys seed from the farm at the contract-equivalent price.
Labor is FAMILY $11/hr, with HIRED $18/hr shown. **Schema note:** `net_per_acre_equiv` already
charges raw seed at market value, so the LLC's real added margin over selling raw equals the net.
The schema uplift subtracts raw net ($66) again.

**Seed-rights caveat:** contract camelina must be delivered 100% to the contractor. Any LLC product
needs non-contract (cover-crop) seed grown on separate, documented acres.

### 5a. Oil and meal under contract (the raw sale) versus own crush for bulk oil
- **Option:** the LLC crushes on the sunflower-plan press and sells crude oil to a biodiesel or
  renewable-diesel buyer plus meal to feeders.
- **Economics:** 760 lb clean seed → 30% oil (228 lb at ~$0.50/lb, soy-oil-like value, **verify**)
  plus 70% meal (530 lb at ~$0.08/lb) = ~$0.21 per lb seed crushed. That is **below** the $0.27
  contract price for the seed itself. Net -$168/ac-equiv (schema -$234).
- Small-scale pressing destroys value on a commodity oil.
- **Verdict: skip.** Sell seed under contract.

### 5b. Cold-pressed culinary camelina oil
- **Product:** a 500 ml bottle positioned as a high-omega-3, high-smoke-point finishing and cooking
  oil (smoke point claims need testing).
- **Process:** the sunflower press line, with a smaller nozzle and screens for tiny seed; settle,
  filter, bottle.
- **Capital:** ~$8,000 incremental.
- **Labor:** 22 hr ($242).
- **Yield:** 760 lb → ~228 lb oil → ~220 bottles.
- **Price:** $6.00 wholesale (estimate). Retail listings run from $9.79 per 4 oz to $34.99 per 36 oz
  (Walmart unrefined organic), and Three Farmers (Canada) lists 12 × 500 ml at $45.49 (currency
  unclear). Consumer awareness in Wichita is low.
- **Margin:** net $221/ac-equiv, including a meal credit (schema $155; $67 at the hired rate).
- **Regulatory:** KDA food license; FDA (camelina oil GRAS notice exists, **verify** the number and
  scope).
- **Market:** perhaps 8 acres (~1,800 bottles).
- **Verdict: test** as a third oil SKU on an operating sunflower line, using non-contract seed.

### 5c. Camelina press cake as feed
- **Product:** bagged or bulk cake for local laying-hen flocks ("omega-3 eggs") or feedlot rations.
- **Rules:** AAFCO and CDFA allow ≤10% inclusion for broilers, layers and feedlot cattle (check
  whether the definition covers mechanically pressed cake). Pressed meal trades at a 15–60%
  discount to soybean meal (MBOLD/AURI 2026).
- **Economics:** 530 lb at $0.12/lb against a feed license, analysis and handling gives net
  -$45/ac-equiv (schema -$111).
- **Verdict: skip as a standalone product.** It is a byproduct of 5b, sold under the sunflower meal
  feed license.

## 6. Risks and disqualifiers
- **Top disqualifier: no buyer within reach** of Sedgwick County for 2026–2027.
- **Policy dependence:** 45Z final rules, RFS volumes and SAF incentives drive crusher demand.
- **Stand establishment:** tiny seed in dry fall soils, and herbicide carryover from wheat and
  sorghum residuals.
- **Harvest timing:** a late-May/June harvest overlaps wheat harvest, so the combine conflicts.
- **Weeds:** limited herbicide options; brassica weed seed contamination.

## 7. Decision rules
1. Grow camelina only under a written contract with a delivered net price ≥ $0.27/lb, or ≥ $0.23/lb
   if seed is supplied free. Delivery must be ≤150 miles or freight paid.
2. Only on fields with no SU, IMI or atrazine residual conflict, and where a double crop follows.
3. Compare the camelina-plus-double-crop margin to the wheat-plus-double-crop margin field by field
   before committing.
4. Pilot 40–80 acres. Expand only if yield is ≥ 700 lb and harvest finishes before wheat harvest.
5. No culinary oil SKU until the sunflower press line is running. Use non-contract seed on separate
   acres.
