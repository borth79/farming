# Broomcorn

> Research note (2026-11-23): there is no current US wholesale broomcorn price series and
> no Kansas budget. Prices are small-lot craft prices; the raw price used here ($4/lb for
> craft grade) is a judgment between Mexican craft supply (≤ $6/lb delivered to makers) and
> what a new grower with unproven quality can ask. **verify** with three broom makers.

Benchmark: dryland grain sorghum ≈ **$79/ac** return over direct costs.

## 1. Fit for this farm
- **Climate**: broomcorn is a sorghum and grows well here; Kansas was a leading broomcorn
  state in the early 1900s (first commercial crop 1870 in McPherson County; later southwest
  Kansas). US commercial production effectively moved to Mexico after NAFTA (SARE FNC26-1498).
- **Quality is the problem**, not growth: broom makers want long, fine, straight, green
  hurl. Wind, hail, rain on cut brush, sun bleaching and late harvest all downgrade it.
  Harvest at or just after bloom (before seed matures) for green fiber (verify by variety).
- **Equipment**: planter and sprayer work; the combine does not. Harvest is by hand
  (tabling: bending stalks and cutting heads, the historical method), followed by shade or
  shed drying on racks, scraping seed off (a seed scraper/thresher is a simple machine, not
  owned), sorting by length and grade, baling or bundling.
- **Labor**: hand harvest and sorting ≈ 40 hr/ac (estimate, verify) in late summer; no
  dedicated labor on the farm makes anything beyond 1–2 acres unrealistic.
- **Rotation and herbicides**: same as grain sorghum; no new conflict. Shattercane-like
  volunteers from dropped seed are a minor risk.
- **Trust**: growing is agriculture; broom making and craft sales go to the LLC.

## 3. Yield and raw-crop economics
| Case | Brush lb/ac | Craft-grade share |
|---|---|---|
| Adverse | 200 | 40% |
| Base | 350 | 60% |
| Favorable | 500 | 70% |

Base revenue: 210 lb craft grade × $4.00 + 140 lb low grade × $1.00 (verify) = $980/ac
(average $2.80/lb). Costs: WSU production cost $460/ac at 450 lb (treated here as non-labor
cost, since the labor content of the WSU figure is not known) plus hand harvest and sorting
40 hr × $11 = $440. **Raw net ≈ $80/ac at FAMILY rate, ≈ −$200 at HIRED** — about equal to
grain sorghum ($79), with far more labor and market risk. If the WSU $460 already includes
harvest labor, raw net is ≈ $520; verify.

## 4. Raw-crop market
- Buyers: US craft and production broom makers (several hundred, mostly small; a few
  factories), basket and craft makers, florists. Most buy Mexican brush through one major
  distributor (SARE). A domestic, story-bearing supply sells at a premium ($12/lb in 5-lb
  bundles for one 2025 US grower) but in very small lots.
- Volume ceiling: a single acre produces enough for 100–175 brooms; the reachable buyer
  list (Kansas, Missouri, Oklahoma craft makers plus online) is unlikely to take more than
  1–3 acres of brush a year from a new grower (CSV = 3; verify).
- Volatility: supply shortages by material type are common; no price series.

## 5. Finished products
Default: LLC buys brush at $2.80/lb average ($980/ac) and makes and sells products off
trust land. Convention: uplift = LLC margin.

**(a) Handmade brooms.** Kitchen, hearth and whisk brooms from craft-grade hurl plus
low-grade inner fill. ~2.1 lb brush per broom (1 ton → 80–100 dozen) → ~100 brooms/ac
(210 craft lb, low grade used inside). Price $50 (verify; craft brooms at fairs and online
commonly $30–90); variable $6 (handle, wire, twine); 1.5 hr/broom → 150 hr; capital $2,500
(broom winder or kick-winder, vise, stitching vise — verify used prices); fixed $800/yr.
LLC margin ≈ **$970/ac** (HIRED ≈ −$80). Market: craft fairs, Wichita holiday markets, Old
Town market, online; perhaps 100–150 brooms a year (≈ 1–1.5 ac). Verdict: **test** only if a
family member wants to learn the craft; it is a labor product, not a land product.

**(b) Natural and dyed craft and decor bundles.** Bundled brush (~0.5 lb) for florists,
craft stores and fall decor, sold with the gourds and ornamental corn line. 700 bundles/ac
at $6 (verify), $0.50 variable, 70 hr, fixed $300/yr. LLC margin ≈ **$1,800/ac** (HIRED ≈
$1,310). Market: Wichita garden centers, florists and fall decor buyers; ≈ 1 acre. Verdict:
**test** with the fall decor line — lowest skill, uses low-grade brush too.

**(c) Broom-making workshops.** Half-day classes ($90/seat, verify), each seat uses ~2 lb
brush and a handle. Per acre-equivalent (105 seats ≈ 3 seasons) margin ≈ $4,265 but the
market is ~32 seats a year (≈ 0.3 acre), i.e. ≈ $1,280/yr. Needs class liability coverage
and an off-trust venue or trustee consent. Verdict: **test** after (a) produces a
competent instructor.

## 6. Risks and disqualifiers
- **Hand labor** at harvest and in broom making — top disqualifier for a farm with no
  dedicated labor.
- Fiber quality in Kansas wind, hail and heat; one storm downgrades the year.
- Tiny, relationship-driven market dominated by cheap Mexican supply.
- No price discovery; buyers judge samples.

## 7. Decision rules
1. Plant broomcorn only as a ≤ 0.5-acre trial attached to the fall decor line, with three
   broom makers having seen samples or a prior-year grower's brush.
2. Expand to 1–2 acres only if the trial yields ≥ 50% craft grade and sells ≥ 80% by the
   following spring at ≥ $3.50/lb or as products.
3. Broom making stays a family-craft option; never hire labor for it.
