# Oats and barley

> Research note (2026-11-23): fetch blocked; figures from search summaries. This file
> covers two crops; the CSV carries a `raw` row for each. Net per acre = gross revenue
> minus direct costs, excluding land and machinery ownership.

## 1. Fit for this farm
**Honest assessment: neither crop fits south-central Kansas well as a food or malting
grain. Both grow; neither reliably makes the quality that earns a premium.**

- **Oats.** Spring oats must be planted late February to mid-March (K-State) and fill grain
  in late May–June heat. K-State states that typical grain-fill conditions give low test
  weights that make Kansas oats "unsuitable for food use", and that few local markets
  exist. Kansas planted 160,000 acres in 2025 but harvested only 35,000 for grain; most
  oats are grown for forage. Food millers (Grain Millers, Quaker/PepsiCo, General Mills)
  source from the northern Plains and Canada. Equipment fits (drill, combine), and oats
  are a useful forage or cover crop.
- **Barley.** Winter barley is less winter-hardy than wheat, matures earlier and is caught
  by heat at fill; K-State has long said Kansas suits feed barley better than malting
  barley. Kansas produced only ~204,000 bu in 2025. The Plains' dry climate does lower
  scab (DON) risk, and breeders and Limagrain now offer winter malting varieties for the
  central Plains, so a small contracted malting trial is plausible, not proven.
- **Rotation.** Both slot like wheat (barley) or as a spring crop before double-crop
  soybeans or sorghum (oats). Both share wheat diseases. Barley carryover limits: same
  as wheat after atrazine.
- **Trust and labor.** Grain and feed are agriculture; food processing goes to the LLC.
- **Commitment.** Annual.

## 2. Agronomy summary
- **Oats.** Seed 2–3 bu/ac (64–96 lb) late Feb–mid March; 40–60 lb N; crown rust and
  barley yellow dwarf are the main diseases; harvest June; low test weight (K-State's
  standard is 32 lb/bu; food mills want ~38+ lb, **verify** spec).
- **Barley (winter).** Plant late September–early October (earlier than wheat for winter
  survival, **verify** K-State SRP686); 60–90 lb N for feed, lower N for malting (protein
  must stay ~9.5–12.5%, **verify** maltster spec); harvest late May–early June; avoid
  pre-harvest sprout (germination must exceed ~95%, **verify**). Two-row winter malting
  varieties from Limagrain; feed six-row varieties are more winter-hardy.
- Both: combine with wheat settings; dry to 12–13%; clean and keep identity-preserved for
  any premium sale.

## 4. Raw-crop market
- Oats: feed buyers and horse owners locally; no food-oat mill within reach (nearest food
  oat buyers are in the northern Plains, **verify**). Price follows Minneapolis/CBOT oats.
- Feed barley: feedlots and feed mills; few Kansas elevators post barley bids
  (**verify**).
- Malting barley: Kansas Malt Company (Wilsey, Morris County) and Great Plains Custom
  Grain (Ottawa) are the only Kansas maltsters found; both are small and whether they are
  buying is **verify** (spirits plan open item). Large maltsters contract in Idaho,
  Montana, North Dakota and Colorado. U.S. malting barley averaged $5.86/bu in Aug 2026,
  ~$2.00 over feed; craft maltsters have paid $8/bu (Indiana, ~2020) to $12–14/bu
  equivalent ($0.25–0.30/lb; spirits plan).

## 5. Finished products
Default structure: the LLC buys from the trust farm at oats $2.85/bu ($157/ac) or barley
$3.90/bu ($214/ac) and processes off trust land. FAMILY $11/hr; HIRED $18/hr sensitivity.

**O1. Cleaned horse oats, 50-lb bag.** Clean and bag on the shared birdseed/seed line (no
new capital). 31 bags/ac at $15 ($0.30/lb; **verify** Wichita feed-store prices). Variable
$1.90/bag; labor 3.1 hr; fixed $500/yr over 10 acres. Uplift **$165/ac**. Ceiling ~30
acres (horse owners and boarding stables; Sedgwick County horse numbers **verify**).
Horse buyers also want heavy, bright oats, so Kansas test weight is still a handicap.
Regulatory: KDA commercial feed registration and label (**verify**). Verdict: **test**.

**O2. Rolled oats and groats.** Hulling, kiln/steam stabilization and flaking: ~$40,000
(**verify**; SARE found used equipment for ~$5,000 and ~$0.10/lb processing; full small
lines run to $135,000). ~1,000 lb groats/ac at $2.75/lb retail-blend. Variable
$0.35/lb; labor 30 hr; fixed $7,500/yr over 10 acres. Uplift **$1,163/acre-eq** on paper,
but K-State's test-weight warning means the raw oats may not mill to food quality at all,
and gluten-free claims are impossible with shared wheat equipment. Verdict: **skip**.

**O3. Granola.** ~2,000 lb granola per acre of oats at $6.00/lb blended (bulk competitors
$2.78–4.40/lb). Ingredients and packaging dominate ($2.50/lb plus rolling). Uplift
**$3,463/acre-eq** with a ceiling of ~5 acres. The farm's oats contribute almost nothing
that purchased rolled oats (~$1/lb) would not; this is a food business independent of
the crop. Verdict: **skip** as a crop decision.

**B1. Malting barley under contract to a craft maltster.** No capital. 55 bu at an
expected $6.95/bu (50% chance of making malting grade at $10/bu, 50% falling to feed at
$3.90; **verify** both). Handling $0.80/bu (testing, cleaning, IP storage, hauling ~120
mi to Wilsey). Uplift **$91/ac**. Ceiling ~30 acres (one or two craft maltsters).
Verdict: **test** 10–20 acres only with a signed contract and a winter malting variety.

**B2. Pearled barley, retail bag.** Pearler ~$20,000 (**verify**). ~1,545 lb pearled/ac at
$2.50/lb (**verify**). Uplift **$1,446/acre-eq** but ceiling ~2 acres; no buyer
identified. Verdict: **skip**.

**B3. Toll-malted craft malt sold to Wichita brewers.** No owned malthouse (spirits plan
rejects one). Toll malting ~$0.50/lb (MSU cost $793–1,209/ton; toll price **verify**);

Ceiling ~15 acres (10+ Wichita breweries using small specialty-malt volumes). Requires a
toll maltster willing to run small lots (**verify**). Verdict: **test** only after B1

## 6. Risks and disqualifiers
- **Top disqualifier: heat at grain fill.** Low oat test weight and high, shriveled
  barley protein are the normal Kansas outcome, which removes the food and malting
  premiums that would justify these crops.
- Winterkill (barley); sprout damage ruins malting germination.
- No food-oat or large malting buyer within reach; craft maltsters are small and may not
  be buying.
- Raw net per acre is below sorghum and wheat in all but favorable years.

## 7. Decision rules
1. Do not grow oats for grain except as a cover or forage crop with a hay buyer, or for a
   named horse-oat demand covering 60% of output.
2. Grow malting barley only on a signed contract that states the feed fallback price, with
   a winter malting variety, on no more than 20 acres until two years of malting-grade
   results.
3. No oat-processing or pearling equipment.
