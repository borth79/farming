# Chickpeas (garbanzo)

## 1. Fit for this farm
- **Climate: poor.** Chickpea is a cool-season, long-day crop grown in Montana, the Dakotas,
  the Palouse and Saskatchewan. In Sedgwick County, a March–April planting flowers in late
  May to June. Daytime highs then are often above 90 °F, which reduces pod set (heat-stress
  thresholds are near 95 °F at flowering; verify for chickpea specifically). The same window
  brings Kansas's wettest, most humid weeks (May–June). Long humid spells drive Ascochyta
  blight, the disease that wiped out northern-Plains crops in 2000–2001 (MSU). The farm would
  get heat at bloom and disease pressure at once.
- **Fall-seeded (winter) chickpea**: not winter-hardy at Wichita latitude with available
  kabuli varieties (verify; no Kansas data found).
- **Soil**: well-drained, neutral to alkaline. Intolerant of waterlogging.
- **Rotation**:
  - Chickpea fits after wheat, ahead of sorghum.
  - It will not tolerate atrazine carryover (assume the same restriction as dry beans; check
    each label).
  - It needs 4 years between chickpea crops (USA Pulses).
- **Equipment**:
  - Planting: a planter or drill works with large seed plates (kabuli seed is large; about
    100–150 lb/ac). The sprayers work.
  - Harvest: chickpea pods sit higher than lentil pods, so a grain platform or flex header
    can harvest them. A flex draper is better.
  - Seed handling: large seed cracks in augers.
- **Trust and labor**: agriculture stays with the farm. Products go through the LLC.
- **Verdict on fit: do not grow at field scale.** The products in section 5 are better
  made from bought chickpeas than farm-grown ones.

## 2. Agronomy summary
- **Varieties**: kabuli (large, cream) types: Sierra, Billy Bean, CDC Orion, and others.
  Desi types are smaller. No variety tested in Kansas was found.
- **Planting**:
  - Plant early spring (March to early April) into 45 °F soil, 1.5–2.5 in deep.
  - Inoculate with chickpea-specific Mesorhizobium.
  - Use a fungicide seed treatment for Ascochyta and Pythium.
- **Fertility**: little N if nodulated. P by soil test.
- **Weeds**: few POST options. Rely on PRE (pendimethalin, sulfentrazone, as labeled) and
  clean fields.
- **Disease**: Ascochyta blight needs 2–4 protectant or systemic fungicide passes in humid
  years (northern-Plains practice). Fusarium wilt and root rots are also risks.
- **Harvest**: desiccate. Harvest at 13–14% moisture, July in Kansas, which collides with
  wheat harvest on the single combine. Clean and size for food grade.

## 4. Raw-crop market
- There is no chickpea buyer within reach of Sedgwick County. US processors and handlers
  are in Montana, North Dakota, Washington, Idaho and Nebraska (2026 harvested area
  416,700 ac, down 20%).
- Contracts specify size (e.g., 9 mm and larger), color and damage. Prices fell in 2025 and
  2025/26 (ERS).
- A Wichita LLC can buy food-grade kabuli chickpeas from northern handlers at about
  $0.30–0.40/lb delivered (estimate, verify). That is the realistic raw-material source for
  any chickpea product.

## 5. Finished products
The default LLC structure applies. Economics are per acre-equivalent of 650–700 lb of
cleaned chickpeas. Raw crop is charged at farm market value, 700 lb × $0.22 = $154. If the
LLC buys northern chickpeas instead, the raw charge rises to about $230–280 and every product
margin falls by about $100. Labor is at FAMILY $11/hr. Uplift follows the schema (LLC net
minus the raw net of -$73).

### 5a. Bagged dry chickpeas, 1–2 lb (local retail)
- **Process**: clean, size, bag and label. **Capital**: about $8,000, shared with the bean
  line.
- **Labor**: 0.05 hr/lb × 650 lb = 32.5 hr = $358.
- **Price**: $4.00/lb blended (estimate, verify against grocery bagged garbanzos; commodity
  shelf price is lower).
- **Margin**: $2,600 − $552 − $358 − $154 − $667 ($2,000 ÷ 3 ac) = **$869**. Schema uplift
  $942.
- **Regulatory**: KDA license for store sales; FSMA qualified facility.
- **Market**: about 3 acres.
- **Verdict: skip.** Same channel as bagged beans, with no local story or quality edge.
  Add it to the bean line only if customers ask.

### 5b. Roasted seasoned chickpea snack, 5 oz bag
- **Process**: soak, cook, dry, roast with oil and seasoning, cool, and pack in nitrogen-
  flushed bags in a licensed commercial kitchen. A rented commissary kitchen is the cost
  model.
- **Capital**: about $6,000 (small equipment, nutrition analysis, shelf-life test, label
  design).
- **Labor**: 0.04 hr/bag × 2,080 bags = 83 hr = $913 ($1,494 at HIRED).
- **Raw needed**: about 1 lb dry per 1 lb roasted (estimate, verify); 0.31 lb per bag.
- **Price**: $3.50 blended (retail ~$4.50, wholesale ~$2.75; estimate). National brands
  (Biena and similar) were not priced in this review; verify.
- **Margin**: $7,280 − $2,912 ($1.40/bag including kitchen rent) − $913 − $154 − $1,333
  ($4,000 ÷ 3 ac) = **$1,968**. Schema uplift $2,041. At HIRED rate, $1,387.
- **Regulatory**:
  - KDA food processing license.
  - FSMA: qualified facility (preventive controls rule) if sales are small; verify.
  - Allergens: peanut, tree nut and sesame cross-contact is a risk in a shared kitchen.
  - Nutrition Facts label, unless the small-business exemption is filed.
- **Competitors**: Biena, The Good Bean, store brands. Local and regional snack shelves.
- **Market**: about 3 acres-equivalent (6,000 bags/yr, estimate).
- **Verdict: test, but with purchased chickpeas.** This is the only chickpea product with a
  meaningful margin, and none of that margin depends on growing chickpeas on this farm.

### 5c. Hummus, 10 oz tub via a refrigerated co-packer
- **Process**: a co-packer cooks and blends with tahini, then cold-fills or uses HPP.
  Refrigerated distribution.
- **Capital**: about $8,000 (formulation, labels, process validation). MOQs are
  1,200–15,000 units per SKU.
- **Labor**: 15 hr.
- **Raw needed**: about 0.15 lb dry chickpea per tub (estimate).
- **Price**: wholesale $3.25/tub (estimate) against an all-in co-pack cost of about $2.40.
- **Margin**: $13,975 − $10,320 − $165 − $154 − $3,333 = **$3**. Schema uplift $76.
- **Regulatory**: co-packer FDA registration and validation. Sesame is a major allergen.
  Cold chain.
- **Competitors**: Sabra, Cedar's, store brands.
- **Verdict: skip.** The chickpea is a trivial share of hummus value, cold chain and spoilage
  risk are high, and the category is dominated by national brands.

### 5d. Chickpea (garbanzo) flour
- **Process**: clean, then stone or hammer mill and sift. Could share the wheat-flour
  vertical's mill.
- **Capital**: about $12,000 if a dedicated small mill is needed (verify).
- **Labor**: 25 hr per acre-equivalent.
- **Price**: $4.00/lb blended.
  - Bob's Red Mill foodservice price was $1.78/lb for 25 lb (old listing).
  - Retail runs $4–18/lb.
- **Margin**: $2,480 − $496 − $275 − $154 − $667 = **$888**. Schema uplift $961.
- **Regulatory**: KDA license. A gluten-free claim requires <20 ppm (21 CFR 101.91), which a
  shared wheat mill makes hard to meet. Most buyers of chickpea flour want gluten-free.
- **Verdict: skip** unless the flour vertical runs a dedicated gluten-free line. Even then,
  buy the chickpeas.

## 6. Risks and disqualifiers
- **Top disqualifier**: climate. Heat at flowering plus May–June humidity (Ascochyta). No
  local buyer, no insurance, and no Kansas trial data.
- **Harvest timing**: harvest overlaps wheat harvest on the farm's one combine.
- **Herbicides**: narrow weed-control options; atrazine plant-back.

## 7. Decision rules
1. Do not plant chickpeas at field scale on this farm.
2. Revisit only if K-State or Oklahoma State publishes a southern-Plains chickpea trial
   showing 1,200+ lb/ac in 3 of 5 years, and a buyer within 200 miles offers a contract.
3. Any chickpea snack in the LLC sources food-grade chickpeas from a northern handler under
   a supplier specification.
