# Cereal rye (grain and seed)

> Research note (2026-11-23): no Kansas rye yield, acreage or price series was retrieved
> (NASS fetch blocked); figures are national or out-of-state and marked **verify**. Net per
> acre = gross revenue minus direct costs, excluding land and machinery ownership.

## 1. Fit for this farm
- **Climate.** Rye is the most winter-hardy and drought-tolerant small grain, matures
  ~1–2 weeks before wheat, and handles poor or sandy soils. It suits south-central Kansas.
- **Rotation.** Fits the wheat slot (Oct planting, June harvest) and leaves an earlier
  double-crop window than wheat. **Conflict:** volunteer rye is a serious weed in wheat.
  Rye in wheat is graded as a contaminant (dockage/foreign material) and few herbicides
  separate rye from wheat in-crop. Never plant rye grain fields that will go back to wheat
  within 2–3 years without a summer crop and a clean fallow between (**verify** control
  options with K-State).
- **Equipment.** Drill, sprayer and combine are all fine (wheat settings; tall straw needs a
  reel adjustment). Lacks: a seed cleaner (shared with the birdseed vertical if built) and,
  for straw, a small square baler.
- **Herbicide carryover.** Rye after sorghum with late atrazine carries the same risk as
  wheat (see sorghum file).
- **Trust and labor.** Grain and bulk seed are agricultural. Labeled seed sales need a KDA
  seed registration (entity to decide with counsel). Flour goes to the flour LLC.
  Labor is low except for seed cleaning and bagging.
- **Commitment.** Annual.

## 2. Agronomy summary
- Varieties: Elbon (forage, early), Maton, Bates, Wheeler, Brasetto/hybrid rye for grain
  (**verify** seed availability and PVP status; hybrid rye cannot be saved).
- Planting: Oct 1–Nov 1; 55–60 lb/ac drilled for cover (K-State); 60–90 lb/ac for grain
  (**verify**).
- Fertility: 40–60 lb N/ac for grain; excess N causes lodging.
- Pests and disease: ergot is the rye-specific risk (toxic sclerotia, grain rejection);
  leaf rust; few insect problems.
- Harvest: early-to-mid June; tall straw (1.5–2+ t/ac). Dry to 13%.
- Seed handling: germination test on every lot; clean to remove weed seed (cheat, rye
  ergot bodies).

Price: base $6.00/bu blended (bin-run to a seed conditioner or feed buyer; **verify**).
The U.S. marketing-year price was $4.75/bu for 2025 (YCharts/USDA); Kansas elevators
generally do not bid rye (**verify**), so $4.75 is a ceiling for feed sale and gives
only $26/ac. Direct cost ~$140 (seed 60 lb x $0.25 $15; N $28; herbicide $10; insurance
$8; fuel and repairs $30; hauling $10; labor $20; interest $5; misc $14; **verify**).

**Comparison to dryland sorghum (~$100/ac base, `grain-sorghum.md`).** Raw rye at $70/ac
is below sorghum and modern wheat (~$107/ac). Rye earns its place only as seed or a
contract grain, or as the early-harvest small grain that opens a double-crop window.

## 4. Raw-crop market
- Cover-crop seed: Kansas NRCS cost-share programs drive cereal rye demand; local seed
  dealers and co-ops buy cleaned or bin-run rye from growers (**verify** prices).
- Distilleries: Kansas craft distilleries (Boot Hill, Union Horse, Wheat State, Bluff
  Creek, Smoky Valley and others in the spirits plan) buy rye for mash bills in small lots;
  Iowa distiller contracts paid $12.50/bu in 2022.
- MGP Ingredients (Atchison KS) is a large rye-whiskey distiller; whether it contracts
  local rye is **verify** (most commodity rye is imported from Canada and Europe).
- Feed: limited; rye is less palatable and ergot risk deters feeders.
- Volatility: thin market; prices swing with cover-crop program funding.

## 5. Finished products
Default structure: the LLC (or farm, for bulk seed) buys rye from the trust farm at
$6.00/bu ($210/ac base). FAMILY $11/hr; HIRED $18/hr sensitivity. Uplift = revenue -
variable - labor - raw at market value - fixed share, per acre processed.

**R1. Cleaned, labeled cover-crop seed sold to farmers.**
Process: air-screen clean, germination test, label, sell in bulk totes or 50-lb bags.
Capital ~$8,600 (used Clipper-class cleaner ~$5,000 and gross-weigh bagger $3,600, as in
the birdseed vertical; shared if that vertical runs). 1,800 lb clean seed/ac at $0.30/lb
(bulk market $0.235–0.42/lb; ~$0.25/lb Aug 2026 snapshot). Variable ~$0.036/lb (test
fees, totes/bags, delivery). Labor 4.2 hr/ac. Fixed $1,715/yr (registration, depreciation)
over 40 acres. Uplift **$177/ac** ($147 at HIRED); whole-chain net ~$247/ac, about 2.5x
the sorghum acre. Ceiling ~60 acres (~108,000 lb; Sedgwick and neighboring counties'
cover-crop demand, **verify** NRCS acres). Regulatory: KDA seed registration ($185/yr
wholesale+retail before the SB 425 increase), germination test within 9 months, named
variety (no VNS). Competitors: local co-ops, Kansas seed companies, online marketplaces.
Verdict: **pursue** (test 20–40 acres): uses owned equipment, low labor, real demand.

**R2. Rye grain to Kansas distilleries (rye whiskey).**
No capital beyond a dedicated bin. 35 bu at $9.00/bu delivered (**verify**; Iowa paid
$12.50 in 2022, Kansas buyers likely lower). Handling $0.65/bu (spirits plan Route A).
Labor 2 hr. Uplift **$60/ac**. Ceiling ~100 acres (3–6 buyers at ~1,000–1,500 bu each).
Regulatory: none for the farm; buyers test DON and ergot. Verdict: **test** through
spirits Route A outreach; small but zero-capital.

**R3. Stone-ground rye flour (through the wheat-flour vertical).**
Incremental capital ~$5,000 on the planned mill. ~1,860 lb whole rye flour/ac at $1.80/lb
(**verify**; no rye flour benchmark retrieved). Variable $0.20/lb; labor 19 hr; fixed
$1,000/yr over 10 acres. Uplift **$2,465/acre-eq**, but Wichita rye flour demand is
small: ~4 acres (~7,000 lb/yr to 2–5 bakeries making rye or pumpernickel; a "rye bread
partnership" with one bakery is the channel). Regulatory: flour LLC KDA license. Verdict:
**test** as a flour-vertical SKU only.

**R4. Rye straw small squares (mushroom substrate, garden mulch, fall decor).**
Capital ~$14,000 (used small square baler and rake; farm owns neither, **verify**).
~67 bales/ac (1.5 t at ~45 lb/bale, **verify**) at $6.00/bale (AMS small-square wheat

(mushroom vertical 150–250 bales/yr plus Wichita fall decor and garden demand). Verdict:
**test** only if the mushroom vertical runs or a baler is borrowed or custom-hired.

## 6. Risks and disqualifiers
- **Top disqualifier: volunteer rye contaminating later wheat crops** (grade and dockage
  losses on the farm's anchor crop). Confine rye to fields that rotate to summer crops for
  2+ years.
- Ergot (food, feed and seed rejection).
- No Kansas elevator market; every bushel needs a named buyer.
- Seed liability: germination or weed-seed claims; keep the test records.
- Cover-crop demand depends on NRCS and state program funding.

## 7. Decision rules
1. Grow rye only on fields scheduled for two or more summer crops before the next wheat.
2. Plant seed rye only against seed orders or a dealer letter for 60% of output at
   $0.25/lb or better.
3. Every lot: germination test and ergot inspection before sale.
4. Distillery rye only with a written price of at least $1.50/bu over the seed-market
   alternative after handling.
