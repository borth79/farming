# Industrial hemp

## 1. Fit for this farm
**Bottom line:** grain hemp under contract is the only hemp branch that fits a farm with no
dedicated labor. Fiber is possible only as a dual-purpose bonus sold to a processor. CBD/floral
does not fit: it is labor-intensive, the price has collapsed, and the legal status is shrinking.

- **Climate and water:** grain varieties do well near Wichita in some years (K-State Pair Center,
  2022 average 1,057 lb/ac) and poorly in others (Manhattan 2022, most varieties ≤200 lb/ac).
  Heat and drought at flowering, and photoperiod-driven early flowering of northern (Canadian or
  European) grain varieties, drive the variance.
- **Soil:** needs well-drained, fertile, non-crusting soils. Hemp hates wet feet and is weak on
  crusted surfaces.
- **Rotation:** May planting, harvest August to September. It works after wheat in the following
  year, and the early harvest leaves time for wheat seeding. It is a broadleaf break.
- **Herbicides:** very few conventional herbicides are labeled for hemp (**verify** current EPA and
  Kansas labels). Plan on a clean seedbed and dense stands. Carryover sensitivity to atrazine,
  sulfonylureas and other residuals is a real risk (**verify**).
- **Equipment:** the grain combine works with a draper or flex head, wrap guards and frequent
  cleaning. Stalks wrap on rotating parts. Grain comes off at 15–25% moisture and must be dried to
  ~9% within hours (it heats fast). **Ask:** does the farm have a dryer or aerated bin with heat? A
  dual-purpose fiber harvest needs a swather or mower-conditioner and a large baler, which are not
  owned (custom or rental).
- **Trust fit:** hemp is a federally legal agricultural crop. The trustee may still view a crop with
  THC-testing and "destroy if hot" exposure as an unusual risk. Get explicit trustee sign-off.
  The producer license should be held by the operating entity, not the trust (**verify** with
  counsel).
- **Labor:** grain hemp is close to a grain-crop workload plus compliance paperwork. CBD would need
  roughly 150 hours per acre.
- **Commitment:** annual.

## 2. Agronomy summary
- **Varieties:** grain or dual-purpose types from the K-State top group (CFX-1, Henola, NWG-2463,
  X-59; NWG2730 led Wichita in 2022). Seed must be certified low-THC, and viable seed above 0.3%
  total THC is excluded from "hemp" under P.L. 119-37.
- **Planting:** May (soil ≥ 50–55 F), drilled 7.5 in rows at 25–35 lb/ac (estimate, verify with the
  processor).
- **Fertility:** ~80–100 lb N, P and S to soil test (estimate).
- **Pests:** birds at maturity, corn earworm/headworm, and white mold in wet years.
- **Harvest and storage:** direct combine at 60–70% seed maturity (August to early September). Dry
  immediately, clean, and store cool at ≤9% moisture.
- **Compliance:** see §5. A USDA license, an FSA acreage report, sampling within 30 days before
  harvest by an authorized sampler, and a DEA-registered lab total-THC test are standard under the
  USDA rule (**verify** current fees and windows on the USDA HeMP site).

## 4. Raw-crop market
- **Midwest Hemp Technology, Augusta KS** (Butler County, ~25 miles from Wichita) is a
  producer-owned processor. It contracts grain and fiber growers in KS, MO, NE and OK and makes
  hearts, oil, protein, meal and long-strand fiber. It is the only grain buyer confirmed within
  trucking distance. Contract price and terms are on request. It is also the main competitor to any
  LLC hemp food product.
- **Fiber:** South Bend Industrial Hemp (decortication, opened 2021; current status **verify**),
  Prairie Band Potawatomi Nation (building products, insulation), and a Culbertson NE bedding plant
  (~4,000 ac, 2026).
- **Market size:** the U.S. grew only ~7,500 acres of grain hemp in 2025. This is a tiny, thin
  market, and a single processor's appetite sets the ceiling. Working cap: 80 acres until the
  processor commits in writing.
- **History (be candid):** the 2019 boom (~500,000 licensed acres nationally, mostly CBD) collapsed
  within a year. CBD biomass went from $35–50/lb to under $1–5/lb (secondary, citing Hemp
  Benchmarks). Many growers were left with unsold crops and unpaid contracts. Fiber and grain
  prices have been low but stable, and grain value rose in 2025 (+112% in production).
  Counterparty risk is the main risk. Require payment terms, a quality spec, and a delivery window
  in writing.

## 5. Finished products
Default structure: an LLC off trust land buys grain from the farm at the documented contract
price. Labor is FAMILY $11/hr, with HIRED $18/hr shown. **Schema note:** `net_per_acre_equiv`
already charges raw seed at market value, so the real LLC margin over selling raw is the net. The
schema uplift subtracts raw net ($220) again.

### Legal status of products (as of 2026-11-23)
- **Grain foods (hearts, oil, protein):** inside the statutory "industrial hemp" category (whole
  grain, oil, cake, nut, hull). They remain lawful federally subject to the total-THC limits.
  Seed-derived foods have FDA GRAS "no questions" letters (2018, hulled seed, protein, oil;
  **verify**).
  - **Open question:** the 0.4 mg total THC per container cap applies to "final hemp-derived
    cannabinoid products". Confirm with counsel that hemp seed oil and hearts are outside it. Test
    every lot regardless.
- **Kansas:** K.S.A. 2-3908 bans several consumable hemp forms unless the seller is a registered
  processor (teas, smokables, vaping liquids and others). Processors register with the State Fire
  Marshal (**verify**).
- **CBD and floral:** from 2026-12-11 (P.L. 119-103, tied to the CR and could slip again), a
  full-spectrum CBD oil or gummy with more than 0.4 mg total THC per container is not hemp. THCA
  flower above 0.3% total THC is marijuana. Synthesized cannabinoids lost hemp status 2026-11-12.
  The CBD market is legally shrinking on top of a price collapse.

### 5a. Hulled hemp hearts
- **Process:** clean → size → impact dehuller → aspirate and sieve → (optional) pasteurize → pack.
- **Capital:** ~$100,000 for a small line (**verify** with vendor quotes).
- **Labor:** 15 hr/ac-equiv ($165).
- **Yield:** 800 lb → ~250 lb hearts (≈31%, verify).
- **Price:** $6.50/lb wholesale blend. Canadian hearts wholesale at ~$9.76/lb in 8-lb packs (Faire),
  and Manitoba Harvest retails ~$12/lb.
- **Margin:** net $50/ac-equiv (schema -$170; -$275 at the hired rate).
- **Competitors:** Manitoba Harvest and other Canadian scale producers, plus the Augusta processor
  25 miles away.
- **Verdict: skip.** Toll-dehull at Augusta if a hearts SKU is ever wanted.

### 5b. Cold-pressed hemp seed oil
- **Process:** the sunflower-plan screw press (clean between oils; hemp is not a major allergen),
  cold settle, filter, dark glass under nitrogen, refrigerated distribution.
- **Capital:** ~$15,000 incremental (cold storage, dedicated tanks). About $60,000 if standalone.
- **Labor:** 30 hr/ac-equiv ($330).
- **Yield:** 800 lb → ~190 lb oil (≈25%) → ~370 × 250 ml.
- **Price:** $6.00 wholesale (retail $10–11). Manitoba Harvest is $22.19 per 32 fl oz retail
  (~$5.80 per 250 ml equivalent), so price headroom is limited.
- **Margin:** net $582/ac-equiv (schema $362; $152 at the hired rate).
- **Regulatory:** KDA food license; FDA; lot THC testing; Kansas processor registration.
- **Limits:** short shelf life (high omega-3; ~6–12 months refrigerated, verify) and a market of
  perhaps 15 acres.
- **Verdict: test** as a cold-chain SKU on the sunflower line, with seed bought from the farm
  under the Augusta contract terms or a carve-out.

### 5c. Hemp protein powder (from 5b press cake)
- **Process:** mill and sift the cake. A pin mill and sifter cost ~$25,000.
- **Labor:** 10 hr ($110).
- **Yield:** ~570 lb cake → ~250 lb powder.
- **Price:** $5.00/lb wholesale (estimate; retail $10–15/lb).
- **Margin:** net $500/ac-equiv with the cake charged at a nominal $57/ac. That nominal charge is
  generous: hemp seed meal's approval as livestock feed is limited or pending (**verify** AAFCO and
  KDA), so its feed value may be near zero.
- **Verdict: test**, only alongside 5b.

### 5d. Fiber and hurd: animal bedding or hempcrete
- **Process:** decortication separates bast fiber from hurd; dust-extract, bag or bale.
- **Capital:** ≥ $750,000 even for a small line (estimate). Fixed costs are ~$110,000/yr.
- **Labor:** 6 hr/ac (baling stalks included).
- **Economics:** ~1,800 lb hurd/ac → 45 bales of 40 lb at $14 (estimate, no sourced 2026 bedding
  price) = $630 revenue against $330 of stalk value. Net -$121/ac-equiv (schema -$341).
- **Hempcrete:** no 2026 material price found. The binder (lime) carries most of the cost, and
  demand is project-by-project.
- **Verdict: skip.** Sell baled stalks to an existing decorticator if the processor contract allows
  dual-purpose harvest.

### 5e. CBD or floral hemp
- **Process:** transplanted female clones or feminized seed (~1,500–1,800 plants/ac), plastic
  mulch, drip irrigation, hand harvest, barn drying, and sale as biomass to an extractor.
- **Capital:** ~$60,000 (drying barn, transplanter, mulch layer).
- **Labor:** ~150 hr/ac.
- **Economics:** 1,200 lb at $2.00/lb against ~$3.00/lb in variable cost gives a net of about
  -$3,850/ac. Even at the NASS 2025 floral average of $19.30/lb, which is inflated by THCA
  smokable flower that is now becoming illegal, the labor model does not fit this farm.
- **Regulatory:** total-THC compliance and "hot" crop destruction; the per-container cap kills most
  full-spectrum products from 2026-12-11; Kansas consumable-product restrictions.
- **Verdict: skip.** Do not revisit unless a federal regulated CBD framework passes and biomass is
  above $15/lb under contract.

## 6. Risks and disqualifiers
- **Counterparty and thin market:** one buyer within reach and ~7,500 U.S. grain acres. The contract
  price is unknown.
- **Yield variance:** 200 to 2,380 lb/ac in K-State trials. Heat and photoperiod hit northern
  genetics.
- **Weed control:** almost no labeled herbicides.
- **Harvest and drying:** wrapping and fire risk on the combine; immediate drying required.
- **Compliance:** a test above 0.3% total THC means destruction, and THCA now counts. Federal
  felony-conviction bars apply to license holders. License and sampling rules are in transition
  (USDA program; Kansas fee statute).
- **Policy volatility:** the definition change could be delayed again by each CR. It could also
  tighten further or be replaced by a federal framework.
- **Reputation and trust:** the trustee, lender or insurer may decline. Get confirmation in
  writing.

## 7. Decision rules
1. Grow grain hemp only under a written Midwest Hemp Technology (or equivalent) contract with
   (a) a price ≥ $0.55/lb clean, dry, delivered (break-even with sorghum at 800 lb is $0.52);
   (b) a stated quality spec and payment within 30 days of delivery; and (c) a processor-supplied
   certified seed variety from the K-State top group.
2. First year 20–40 acres on a weed-clean, residual-free field near the drying setup.
3. A USDA producer license held by the operating entity, with trustee approval on file, before seed
   is ordered.
4. Never grow CBD or floral hemp on this farm under the current law.
5. Start the hemp oil and protein LLC SKU only after the sunflower press line is operating and the
   0.4 mg cap question is answered in writing by counsel.
