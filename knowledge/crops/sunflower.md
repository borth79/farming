## Fit for this operation

**Summary verdict:** Sunflower is a legitimate but *niche* option for a Sedgwick
County dryland rotation. It fits best as (a) a full-season broadleaf break between
wheat crops on fields with a deep profile and no recent Sclerotinia history, or (b) a
double crop after wheat in years when the profile is wet at wheat harvest and a
high-oleic (HO) Act-of-God contract is in hand. It is **not** a default crop: the
market is thin in south-central Kansas, head-moth pressure is highest in the state's
southern tier, and sunflower leaves a drier profile for the following wheat than
sorghum or soybeans do.

Fit factors:
- **Soil / climate.** Sunflower tolerates heat and short-term drought better than
  corn or soybeans because of a 6-10 ft taproot (see Water). It performs on medium-
  to fine-textured upland soils common in Sedgwick County and does not need the
  irrigation that corn does. It is hail-sensitive at bud through bloom and prone to
  lodging on wet soils late. South-central Kansas (SC KS) was historically the
  lowest-production sunflower region in the state (Kansas crop profile, 2001); the
  acreage base is in the northwest (Sherman, Cheyenne, Thomas counties).
- **Rotation.** Broadleaf break crop that follows wheat well and precedes wheat
  acceptably (with a soil-water penalty). Needs a 4-year interval between sunflower
  crops and ideally 3-4 years from other Sclerotinia hosts (soybean, canola, dry
  bean). Herbicide plant-back restrictions after common wheat herbicides are
  22-24 months (K-State eUpdate 1105, 2026), so sunflower after wheat requires
  checking the wheat herbicide record.
- **Equipment.** Standard row-crop planter (30-in rows) and sprayer are fine. Harvest
  needs either a row-crop/corn head fitted with sunflower pans, an all-crop header
  with pans, or a dedicated sunflower header; see Harvest. Combine fire risk is
  real and requires management.
- **Storage.** Sunflower is light (oil-type test weight ~28-32 lb/bu; bushel of
  sunflower at 25 lb is the trade convention when converting, **verify** per
  buyer). Bins fill fast by volume for little weight; fines and trash heat. Oil-type
  stored over summer must be at 8% moisture or lower.
- **Market access (the main disqualifier risk).** No crush plant operates within
  normal trucking distance of Wichita. The former ADM/Northern Sun sunflower crush
  plant at Goodland KS was bought by Scoular (2021) and recommissioned in October
  2024 as a **soybean/canola** crush plant, not sunflower (Scoular, trade press). The
  nearest HO crush delivery points quoted by NSA are Lamar CO (~330 road mi from
  Wichita, **verify**), Enderlin ND and West Fargo ND (both >600 mi). Bird-food and
  confection buyers exist in Kansas and Nebraska/Texas, but the specific current
  buyer list for SC KS must be built by phone. **Decision rule: do not plant
  sunflower without a signed contract that names the delivery point and freight.**

Disqualifiers for a given field: Sclerotinia or Phomopsis in sunflower, soybean or
canola within the last 3 years; heavy kochia or ALS/PPO-resistant Palmer amaranth
pressure with no PRE-residual plan; wheat herbicide in the prior 22-24 months with a
sunflower plant-back restriction; adjacency to cattail marshes or large blackbird
roosts; no contract.

### Hybrid selection (Kansas)
- **Market type first.** Pick the hybrid to match the contract: high-oleic (HO) oil
  for crush contracts (NuSun contracts are no longer being offered by crushers for
  2026-27; NuSun demand is now mainly bird food), conventional/NuSun for bird-food
  contracts, confection (large striped seed) only under a confection contract with a
  named buyer.
- **Maturity.** Full season: medium (approx. 100-110 days) planted late May to
  mid-June in SC KS. Double crop: early/medium-early hybrids only; must reach R9 by
  mid-October.
- **Herbicide tolerance.** Clearfield / Clearfield Plus (imazamox: Beyond /
  Beyond Xtra) or ExpressSun (tribenuron: Express). Never cross-apply; Beyond on a
  non-Clearfield hybrid causes extensive injury. Choose the trait based on the
  field's weed spectrum: neither system controls kochia or ALS-resistant pigweeds,
  so the trait is a supplement to a PRE residual, not a replacement. Most SC KS
  Palmer amaranth should be assumed ALS-resistant.
- **Disease.** Require downy mildew resistance ("advanced DMR" claims from Nuseed
  and equivalents) and rust resistance; most current U.S. hybrids carry both
  (NDSU). Ask seed suppliers for Phomopsis and Sclerotinia tolerance ratings; they
  are partial, not full.
- **Birds and lodging.** Prefer hybrids with heads that turn down at maturity and
  good stalk strength (stem weevil lodging). Confection hybrids are more upright and
  bird-exposed.
- Suppliers active in Kansas include Nuseed, Croplan (WinField), Pioneer/Corteva,
  Syngenta and regional dealers (Polansky Seed in Belleville KS carries Nuseed).
  K-State no longer runs annual sunflower performance tests statewide; use the
  NDSU/SDSU trials and Kansas dealer strip trials. **verify** current K-State test
  availability.

### Fertility
- **Nitrogen (K-State MF2384 / MF2586):**
  `N rec (lb/ac) = yield goal (lb/ac) x 0.065 - profile nitrate-N (lb/ac, 24 in) - other credits`,
  minimum 30 lb N/ac. Examples: 1,500 lb/ac goal = 98 lb N less soil N; 1,200 lb
  goal (double crop) = 78 lb N less soil N. Over-application reduces oil percentage
  and increases lodging; Dakota research shows little response above ~100 lb N/ac.
  Sunflower's deep root recovers nitrate below the wheat root zone; after a
  wheat crop with residual N, a 24-in profile nitrate test is worth the cost.
- **Phosphorus:** apply when Bray-1 P < 20 ppm; K-State minimum 15 lb P2O5/ac, build
  rates per MF2586. Starter P (2x2) is useful on cool soils; do not place in-furrow
  with the seed at more than low rates.
- **Potassium:** apply when exchangeable K < 130 ppm; minimum 15 lb K2O/ac. Most
  Sedgwick County soils test adequate, **verify** by field.
- **Removal per 100 lb seed:** approx. 5 lb N, 2.5 lb P2O5, 3.5 lb K2O (SDSU). A
  1,500 lb crop removes ~75 lb N, 38 lb P2O5, 53 lb K2O.
- **Sulfur / micronutrients:** rarely limiting in Kansas; sunflower is sensitive to
  boron deficiency on sandy soils (general literature).

### Weed control
Sunflower has few herbicide options; the program is PRE-heavy. Palmer amaranth and
kochia are the two decisive weeds in SC KS. ALS-resistant Palmer is widespread; the
K-State "KCTR" population is six-way resistant (2,4-D, ALS, PSII, EPSPS, PPO, HPPD)
but was still fully controlled by PRE-applied sulfentrazone at label rate, so PRE
Spartan remains the backbone.

| Timing | Options (trade names for clarity; confirm labels) | Target | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Burndown (no-till) | Glyphosate + 2,4-D ester or glyphosate + Sharpen (saflufenacil). Observe Sharpen plant-back to sunflower (label). Paraquat alternative. | Emerged weeds, volunteer wheat | Required in double crop; volunteer wheat is the #1 double-crop weed |
| PRE | **Spartan 4F / Spartan Charge (sulfentrazone)** - the "heavy lifter" for pigweeds, kochia, broadleaves; rate is soil-texture and pH dependent (lower on coarse/low-OM soils, injury risk). Plus a grass residual: **Prowl H2O (pendimethalin)**, **Dual Magnum (S-metolachlor)**, or trifluralin (PPI). Spartan + Dual is a standard tank mix. | Palmer, kochia, grasses | Pyroxasulfone (Zidua) effective in K-State trials but **not labeled** on sunflower as of 2022 webinar; **verify** current label |
| POST broadleaf | Clearfield only: Beyond / Beyond Xtra (imazamox) + adjuvant; ExpressSun only: Express (tribenuron). | ALS-susceptible broadleaves | Weak/no activity on kochia and ALS-resistant Palmer; apply small weeds (< 3-4 in) |
| POST grass | Select/Clethodim, Poast (sethoxydim), Assure II (quizalofop) | Grasses, volunteer wheat/sorghum | Compatible with all hybrids |
| Layby / row cultivation | Cultivation in 30-in rows is an option where residue permits. | Escapes | Few SC KS no-till operations do this |
| Desiccant | Glyphosate (not for seed quality), paraquat (Gramoxone), sodium chlorate, diquat per label | Harvest aid | See Harvest |

Resistance management: rotate PRE sites of action with the rest of the rotation
(sunflower years are PPO + Group 15; sorghum/corn years carry atrazine/HPPD; wheat
years carry Group 2/4). Do not rely on Beyond or Express alone for Palmer.

| Pest | Damage | Scouting | Threshold | Control |
|---|---|---|---|---|

Pollinator note: sunflower is bee-attractive; spray at dusk, notify local
beekeepers, prefer products with shorter bee-hazard windows.

### Diseases
| Disease | Conditions | Management |
|---|---|---|
| **Phomopsis stem canker** | Wet July-Aug, dense canopies, high N; lodging and premature death | Hybrid tolerance; 4-yr rotation; avoid excess N and populations >20,000; foliar fungicide at R1 (NDSU) only under high pressure; residue burial/rotation |
| **Sclerotinia** (head rot, stalk/basal rot, wilt) | Cool wet bloom (head rot); wilt from soil sclerotia | **Rotation is the main tool: 3-5 years from sunflower and 3-4 years from soybean/canola/dry bean**; fungicides (Endura/boscalid, Priaxor) give suppression only and inconsistent return; harvest early where head rot present (desiccation can slow spread) |
| **Rust** | Humid, mid-season | Resistant hybrids; fungicide threshold **1% severity on upper four fully expanded leaves at or before R5**; Priaxor, Headline, azoxystrobin labeled |
| **Downy mildew** | Saturated soils 1-2 weeks after planting | Resistant hybrids (near-universal), seed treatment (metalaxyl/mefenoxam), avoid wet spots; no rescue |
| Rhizopus head rot | Follows head-moth and bird injury | Control head moth; timely harvest |
| Charcoal rot, Verticillium, Alternaria | Drought stress; mild | Rotation, hybrid |

**Rotation interval rule for this farm: sunflower no more than once in 4 years on any
field, and never within 3 years of soybeans on a Sclerotinia-history field.**

### Birds
Blackbird (red-winged, grackle, cowbird) damage starts as petals wilt (R6) and
continues to harvest, ~6 weeks of exposure. Northern Plains losses averaged
~1.7% of crop value (1999) but single fields near roosts can lose 20-50%.
Kansas pressure is lower than the Dakotas but Sedgwick County has urban grackle
populations and the Arkansas/Ninnescah river corridors and Cheney Reservoir
marshes are roost habitat; **avoid fields within ~2 miles of large cattail
marshes or known roosts** (USDA APHIS guidance, general). Mitigation: hybrids with
down-turned heads and thick hulls; plant with the neighborhood (very early or very
late fields take the damage); desiccate and harvest as early as moisture allows;
propane cannons/pyrotechnics with frequent relocation; decoy ("Wildlife
Conservation Sunflower") plots are effective in some cases but cost-inefficient;
Avipel (anthraquinone) foliar use on sunflower is still research-stage under NSA
funding - not a labeled option as of 2025, **verify**. Budget 2-5% yield loss for
birds on confection and open-field sites.

### Double-crop HO oil sunflower after wheat (deltas from the table above)
Seed 15,000 seeds (-$4); N 40 lb (-$13); no POST broadleaf if PRE holds (-$18);
burndown mandatory incl. volunteer wheat; insecticide probability lower (late
bloom); **direct costs approx. $240-300/ac, planning point $265/ac**. Wheat
residue and no land charge beyond the wheat enterprise; attribute machinery
ownership at half.

### Confection sunflower (deltas)
Seed +$15-25/ac (confection seed is priced higher and sometimes buyer-supplied at
a deduction from settlement), insecticide for seed weevil nearly certain (+$8),
higher bird/harvest care, seed-size discounts: budget **direct costs approx.
$320-400/ac**. Only grow under contract with the buyer's seed and spec sheet.

this is a real constraint on a 1,000-acre operation with one combine.

  Act-of-God (Sept 2024); 2027 crop ~$25.50/cwt cash, $25.00 AOG (Oct 2026,

### Contract structures
- **Cash forward (fixed price, fixed pounds):** full delivery obligation; shortfall
  bought back at market. Avoid on dryland unless covering <= 50% of expected yield.
- **Act-of-God (AOG) production contract:** price typically $0.50/cwt under cash;
  grower delivers only what the contracted acres produce (drought, hail, insects,
  disease excused). **Default structure for this farm.** Usually opens
  Sept-Oct for the following year and fills quickly; some crushers offer AOG only
  on HO.
- **Minimum-price / basis contracts, oil premium schedules, freight allowances
  (buyer-paid freight from distant points)**: ask; freight allowance can be worth
  $1-2/cwt from SC KS.
- Act in **October-November** for next-year acres when crushers roll out
  contracts (contracts for the 2027 crop were being offered Oct 2026).

## Rotation effects

- **Position:** wheat -> sunflower -> (sorghum or fallow) -> wheat, or wheat ->
  DC sunflower -> sorghum/soybean -> wheat. Sunflower is a strong broadleaf break
  for wheat diseases and grass weeds and lets Group 14/15 PRE chemistry rotate in.
- **Soil water:** sunflower dries the profile to 8-10 ft. Wheat after sunflower
  yielded about **3 bu/ac less** than wheat after other crops at Akron CO (MF2384
  3-yr study), and the deficit "is not always refilled" by fall rain. In SC KS
  (~30-33 in precipitation) the recharge is better than at Akron but expect a
  wheat penalty in dry falls; sorghum after sunflower carries a similar risk.
- **N credit:** none. Sunflower removes ~5 lb N per 100 lb seed; residue is low in
  N and decomposes slowly; no credit to the following crop.
- **Residue:** stalks are brittle and leave moderate cover but the stand (16-18k
  plants) gives less erosion protection than sorghum stubble; wind erosion risk on
  sandy fields in winter.
- **Volunteer sunflower:** shattered seed emerges in the next wheat and in the
  following spring crop. In wheat, 9 plants/m2 season-long reduced wheat yield
  ~19% (Canadian study); control before wheat flag leaf with 2,4-D/MCPA/dicamba
  (easy, non-ALS). In following Clearfield/ExpressSun-tolerant volunteers in a
  spring crop, do not rely on Beyond/Express chemistry; use glyphosate, 2,4-D,
  dicamba or Group 27 as the crop allows.
- **Herbicide carryover INTO sunflower:** many wheat herbicides (e.g., Group 2
  sulfonylureas such as chlorsulfuron/Finesse, Glean; Olympus/propoxycarbazone;
  PowerFlex; Ally) carry **22-24 month** plant-back restrictions for sunflower
  (eUpdate 1105). Atrazine from a prior sorghum/corn year is a plant-back issue
  for sunflower (check label, often 2nd year). Record every wheat herbicide at
  field level in `data/reference/`.
- **Herbicide carryover OUT of sunflower:** Spartan (sulfentrazone) has 4-12 month
  rotation intervals for wheat (10 months at higher rates, label-dependent) and
  longer for some crops; Beyond has 9-month+ restrictions to non-Clearfield corn
  and others. Check labels before drilling wheat after sunflower.
- **Disease carryover:** Sclerotinia sclerotia persist 3+ years and affect
  soybean, canola (excluded), sunflower; Dectes stem borer shares soybean.

### Comparison rules (replace with linked budgets when `grain-sorghum.md` and
`soybeans.md` are finished)
- **Full-season sunflower vs grain sorghum (same field, same year).** Sorghum at a
  SC KS trend of ~75 bu/ac dryland and $4.00/bu (verify) grosses ~$300 on direct
  costs of ~$240-260 (verify) -> ~$45-55 margin over direct, with a local market,
  insurance, and lower fire/bird/moth risk. Sunflower only beats sorghum when
  **(a)** an AOG HO contract nets >= $25/cwt at the farm, **(b)** the field's trend
  sunflower yield is >= 1,500 lb/ac (deep profile, no Sclerotinia history), and
  **(c)** sorghum price is <= $4.00/bu or the field has a sorghum-specific problem
  (sugarcane aphid history, grass-weed resistance, or a need for a broadleaf
  break). If any of the three fails, grow sorghum.
- **Double-crop sunflower vs double-crop soybeans.** Double-crop soybeans are the
  K-State default in central Kansas and have a local market at every elevator.
  Choose DC sunflower only when: wheat harvest finishes by **~June 30 with
  seed-zone moisture**, the field has no soybean (Sclerotinia) within 3 years,
  the wheat herbicide record clears sunflower, an AOG contract is in hand, and the
  forecast is for a hot, dry August (sunflower's deep root beats soybean in a dry
  August; soybeans win in a wet August). Expected DC soybean of 20-25 bu at $10
  (= $200-250 gross, direct cost ~$150-180, verify) sets the bar. If wheat harvest
  slips past July 5, plant soybeans or nothing; sunflower after July 10 in SC KS
  rarely finishes.
- **Sunflower vs fallow/cover before wheat:** sunflower's profile drying penalizes
  the following wheat by ~3 bu/ac; at $6 wheat that is ~$18/ac to charge against
  the sunflower enterprise.

### Decision rules for this farm (orchestrator-planner)
1. **No contract, no sunflower.** Require a signed HO AOG (or confection) contract
   with a named delivery point and freight terms before seed is ordered. Cover
   60-80% of expected AOG production on the planned acres.
2. **Cap acres.** Limit sunflower to <= 10-15% of cropland (100-150 ac) in any year
   given one combine, thin markets, and the 4-year rotation cap; treat as a
   rotation tool, not a core enterprise.
3. **Field screen.** Deep silt loam or better; no sunflower/soybean/canola within
   3 years (4 for sunflower); wheat herbicide plant-back clear; >= 2 miles from
   major blackbird roost habitat; not a sandy field prone to winter wind erosion.
4. **Full season planting window:** May 25 - June 20, targeting bloom after
   August 10 to dodge peak head-moth flight; 16,000-18,000 seeds/ac; 1.5-2.5 in
   into moisture; 30-in rows.
5. **Double crop go/no-go on June 30:** plant only if wheat is off, seed-zone
   moisture is present, and a planting rain or stored moisture supports
   emergence; 14,000-16,000 seeds/ac; early hybrid; hard stop July 10.
6. **Herbicides:** PRE Spartan + Group 15 grass residual on every acre; trait
   herbicide (Beyond/Express) is a supplement; grass POST as needed; record
   sunflower herbicides for wheat plant-back.
7. **Fertility:** 24-in profile nitrate test; N = 0.065 x yield goal minus
   credits, minimum 30 lb; cap at 90 lb N dryland; P per soil test.
8. **Scouting commitments:** cutworm at emergence; head moth and seed weevil
   daily/48-h from first bloom through pollen shed (dusk counts); rust 1% rule;
   lodging check in September.
9. **Harvest:** desiccate at 35% seed moisture if birds, Sclerotinia or schedule
   demand; otherwise harvest at 12-15% and aerate to 10% (8% if stored past
   March); pans or sunflower header, 3-4.5 mph; combine cleaned daily, two
   extinguishers, water tank in field, no harvest in >25 mph winds with low
   humidity (fire rule, verify against `machinery/` safety file).
10. **Review trigger:** revisit this crop's inclusion each October when crushers
    release next-year contracts; if no buyer offers an AOG contract deliverable
    from SC KS at >= direct-cost breakeven + $3/cwt, drop sunflower from the plan
    that year.

## Harvest, drying and storage (added section)

- **Maturity:** physiological maturity (R9) when the back of the head is yellow to
  brown and bracts are brown; seed ~35% moisture. Seed dries ~1-1.5 points/day in
  good weather.
- **Desiccation:** labels allow application at **<= 35% seed moisture** (backs of
  heads yellow, bracts turning brown); earlier application cuts yield and oil.
  Paraquat is the common choice (5-7 days to harvest), sodium chlorate and diquat
  are alternatives, glyphosate is slow and not for seed. Benefits: earlier harvest
  (bird and Sclerotinia escape, timeliness before wheat drilling), uniform dry-down,
  less green stalk material (fire fuel) in the combine.
- **Harvest moisture:** combine at 12-15% and dry/aerate, or 9-10% straight to bin.
  Harvesting above ~10% reduces shatter and fire risk (grower survey, NSA).
- **Combine settings:** cylinder/rotor slow (300-500 rpm), concave open, fan low -
  sunflower is light and blows over the sieve; ground speed 3-4.5 mph with pans.
  Check header loss: ~2 seeds per sq ft is ~1% loss at 1,500 lb/ac (**verify**
  conversion).
- **Fire:** sunflower trash and oily dust accumulate on hot surfaces (turbo,
  manifold, hydraulics) and static builds at speed in dry stalks. Rules: blow off
  the engine compartment and axle areas at least twice daily (every 2-3 hours in
  dry wind), wash oil film from the engine bay before the season, carry a Class
  ABC extinguisher plus a water/pressure extinguisher, keep a tractor and disk or
  water tank in the field, avoid harvesting in afternoons with low humidity and
  high wind, and shut down immediately at the first sign of smoke. Dryers also
  catch fire on sunflower fines - clean plenum and screens between batches.
- **Drying:** maximum plenum temperature up to ~160-200 F in continuous dryers
  (NDSU lists 200 F max) but lower (110-120 F) for confection/seed quality,
  **verify**; natural-air drying needs 0.75-1.0 cfm/bu; hulls dry faster than
  kernels so meter readings coming out of the dryer read 1-2 points low - let
  samples equilibrate before reading.
- **Storage:** oil-type **10% for winter, 7-8% to hold into summer**; confection
  ~10% over summer (Hellevang, NDSU 2022). Cool to 20-30 F for winter. Clean
  before binning (stalks/heads/fines heat); core the bin; monitor temperature
  weekly. Sunflower stores well when dry but insect (Indian meal moth) and heating
  risks rise fast above 10%.
