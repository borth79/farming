# Sweet corn (fresh market)

> Research note: K-State (bookstore.ksre.ksu.edu, hnr.k-state.edu, www.k-state.edu), USDA AMS,
> Missouri Extension and UK CCD hosts failed to resolve in this session; the figures below come
> from search summaries of those sources and are marked **verify** where the summary did not
> show the number directly. No Wichita sweet corn price was found online; the market price used
> is inferred from Kentucky, Virginia and national farm-stand series and must be replaced by a
> count at the Kansas Grown and Old Town markets in July 2027.

## 1. Fit for this farm
- **Equipment fit — best of any produce crop.** The farm already grows corn: the row planter
  (set to a 30-in row and 18–22k seeds/acre with a small-seed plate or vacuum setting), the
  sprayer (with a clean tank or a separate small sprayer) and tractors plant, cultivate and
  spray sweet corn with no new purchase. Harvest is by hand below ~5 acres; a one-row picker is
  not justified at stage 1 (section 6).
- **Soil and water.** Any loam or sandy loam with good drainage; sweet corn tolerates the
  farm's soils better than melons. It needs 1–1.5 in/week from tassel through fill (late June
  to August here), so a planting without irrigation is a yield lottery in Sedgwick County's July
  (corn-dryland.md: July average high ~93 °F, first 100 °F day on average July 1). Plan on
  irrigation (overhead or drip) for every planting; water-right status per field is an open item
  (farm-profile.md) and must be resolved before the plot is chosen.
- **Season and grain-calendar conflict.** A mid-April planting silks in mid-June and is picked
  from about July 1, which collides with wheat harvest (mid-June to early July) and the family's
  labor. The business plan already calls for sweet corn "for late July onward." Rule: the first
  planting goes in **about May 15–25** (harvest ~July 25–Aug 5), with two more successions at
  ~10–14 day intervals (harvest into early–mid September). This sacrifices the early-July price
  premium but keeps June free for wheat. A late-June planting for Labor Day is possible but
  faces the heaviest earworm and spider-mite pressure of the season.
- **Perishable, not storable.** Ears hold 5–7 days (su/se) to 8–12 days (sh2) only under a
  32 °F cold chain; without a cooler, sell within 24 hours. A hydrocool tub (ice + water) and the
  shared CoolBot room in business-plan.md are required before the first harvest.
- **Isolation from the farm's own field corn.** sh2 (supersweet) kernels become starchy if
  pollinated by field corn or by su/se sweet corn. Keep sh2 plantings **700–1,000 ft** from field
  corn or separate silking by **10–14 days** (Rupp; Missouri cites 250 ft between sweet corn
  types — use the larger figure against field corn). On this farm the practical rule is: put
  the produce plot on a field that is not in corn that year, or plant all-sh2 (or all-synergistic)
  types so the problem is only field-corn drift and timing.
- **Trust fit.** No structure, lease, loan or water change is implied at 1–2 acres; a new
  irrigation well or a change in use of an existing right would trip the family-trust checklist
  (family-trust-constraints.md). Market sales are a new business line only in the sense already
  covered by the produce business plan.
- **Labor fit.** ~90–120 hours/acre, of which ~55 are harvest and pack concentrated in 2–3
  weeks per planting; successions spread it. One person can hand-pick 1 acre per planting
  (about 1,000 dozen over 10–12 picking days at ~25 dozen/hour) alongside market days.

## 2. Varieties and types
| Genetics | Trait | Use here | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| su (standard) | low sugar, converts to starch fast | no | obsolete for market sales |
| se / se+ (sugary enhanced) | tender, creamy, good flavor; holds 2–4 days | market, chef | less isolation fuss than sh2; many bicolors (e.g., Bodacious-type yellow, Delectable, Ambrosia-type bicolor) |
| sh2 (supersweet) | highest sugar, 8–12 day shelf life, tough pericarp | wholesale, chef, market | needs isolation from field corn and se/su; needs 65 °F soil and shallow seeding; favored by grocers |
| synergistic / augmented (se × sh2 blends, e.g., "Triplesweet") | se texture with sh2 holding | market, chef | most new fresh-market releases; isolate from field corn like sh2 |
| **Bt (Attribute®/Attribute II®, Performance Series)** | Cry1Ab (Attribute I) or Vip3A + Cry1Ab (Attribute II) for earworm, corn borer, fall armyworm | **yes — every planting after June 1** | Attribute II gives the highest earworm protection; cuts foliar sprays from 3–4 to 0–1; late plantings can still take tip damage (Kentucky). Label the ears as GMO where buyers care (Natural Grocers, some chefs) — se/sh2 non-Bt for those accounts |

- **Recommended set for Kansas (verify with K-State variety trials and seed reps):** one early
  se bicolor (70–75 day) for the first planting; one Attribute II bicolor (78–82 day) for the
  June successions; one sh2 or synergistic white or bicolor for wholesale lots. Colors:
  bicolor dominates Plains markets; some white demand from chefs.
- **Seed cost:** sh2/Bt fresh-market seed ~$12–14 per 1,000 (Rupp; Attribute II typically
  higher — **verify**), 18,000–22,000 seeds/acre ⇒ **~$230–300/acre** (Attribute II may reach
  $350). Non-Bt se ~$150–220/acre.
- Seed sources: Rupp, Siegers, Harris, Johnny's; order in January.

## 9. Profitability verdict
- **Best channel per acre:** chef-direct ($3,978 net/acre at FAMILY, $52/hour) but it absorbs
  at most ~0.3–0.5 acre of ears. **Best scalable channel:** farmers market ($3,068 net/acre,
  $36/hour, family income ~$4,400/acre including wages). Wholesale is a volume outlet for
  surplus only.
- **Stage-1 recommendation: 1.5 acres** in three ~0.5-acre successions planted May 15–June 25,
  all Bt (Attribute II) except a non-Bt se block for GMO-sensitive buyers, irrigated, fenced;
  sell top grade to 3–5 chef accounts, the balance at both Wichita markets, and seconds or
  overflow to one independent grocer. Expected family income at FAMILY rate ≈ $6,000–7,000
  for ~160 hours of work, in a July–September window that does not collide with wheat harvest.
- **What changes the verdict:** (up) a counted Wichita market price ≥$8/dozen or a CSA that
  pre-sells 40+ dozen/week moves the market channel above $4,000/acre; a one-row picker
  becomes defensible only with a grocery or Dillons-store program above ~5 acres. (down) No
  irrigation water right on the plot, a market price below $5/dozen, or earworm damage above
  ~25% of ears (non-Bt) pushes the market channel under the $25/hour floor; at that point drop
  to a single late-May planting of 0.5 acre or stop.

## 10. Risks and disqualifiers
| Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation / disqualifier |
|---|---|---|---|
| No legal irrigation on the plot | unknown (farm-profile open item) | high — dryland sweet corn in July is a coin flip | **Disqualifier** until water status is confirmed per field |
| Earworm damage on non-Bt or late plantings | high | medium–high | Bt hybrids; silk sprays on schedule; label GMO ears honestly |
| Raccoon destruction of a planting at milk stage | high without fence | high | electric fence 2 weeks before milk stage, every planting |
| Pollen contamination from the farm's field corn (sh2 starchiness) | medium | medium | 700–1,000 ft or 10–14 day separation; choose the plot field accordingly |
| Harvest/market collision with wheat harvest (June) | high for April plantings | high | no planting before ~May 15 |
| Unsold ears (perishable) | medium | medium | cooler, pre-orders, chef accounts, seconds outlet; CSA later |
| Herbicide drift or residual (atrazine carryover is fine for corn; others not) | low for this crop | low | sweet corn is the one produce crop tolerant of the grain program's atrazine |
| Spider mites / heat blank tips in August | medium | medium | irrigation, avoid pyrethroids |
| Burnout: picking at dawn plus market Saturdays | medium | high | cap at 1.5 acres until a second person is committed (mechanization rule 2) |
