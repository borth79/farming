# Potatoes (specialty, fingerling, red, yellow; new potatoes)

## 1. Fit for this farm
- **Soil and water.** Potatoes want deep, loose, well-drained sandy loam with pH 5.5–6.5 (scab is
  worse above 6.5; many Sedgwick County soils run 6.5–7.5, so pick the sandiest, lowest-pH field
  and favor scab-tolerant varieties). Irrigation is required for a marketable crop in Kansas: 1–1.5
  in/week during tuber bulking (May–June), drip or overhead. Dryland potatoes are not a plan.
- **Season.** Plant mid-March to early April (K-State), harvest new potatoes mid-June, main dig
  late June–July before soil heat (>75°F soil) degrades quality. The crop is in and out before the
  worst heat but **harvest lands on wheat harvest** (mid-June to early July). The one-row dig plus
  hand pickup must be scheduled around the combine; new-potato sales to chefs in June are the
  alternative use of that window.
- **Equipment.** Existing tractors plant (modified row planter or furrow and hand drop), hill
  (cultivator sweeps or disc hillers) and dig (used one-row chain digger, $2,000–6,000). Pickup
  behind a one-row digger is by hand; a two-row digger with conveyor ($8,000–25,000) removes the
  pickup labor at grocery volumes. Storage uses the shared CoolBot room.
- **Storable.** Cured and held at 40–45°F, 90–95% RH, dark: 4–7 months for reds and yellows, so a
  July dig sells Oct–Apr in the winter window when no Wichita farm offers local potatoes.
  Fingerlings and new potatoes store less well (2–4 months) and are sold first.
- **Trust and labor.** No structure, lease or water change is involved below ~2 acres on existing
  irrigated ground; a new well or water-right change for a produce plot passes
  `knowledge/operations/family-trust-constraints.md` first. Labor is the constraint: ~210–320
  hr/ac depending on channel (section 5).
- **Mechanizable, storable, fits the chef-direct winter route**: this is the lead crop of the
  mechanized-storables set with garlic.

## 3. Agronomy
- **Planting.** Mid-March to early April when soil at 4 in is ≥45°F (K-State). Cut seed to
  1.5–2 oz pieces with at least one eye, suberize 2–3 days at 50–60°F and high humidity, plant
  2–4 in deep (shallower on heavier soil), 8–12 in in-row (K-State), 34–36 in rows to fit existing
  cultivation equipment. Hill twice (emergence +2 weeks and +4 weeks) to 6–8 in to prevent greening
  and heat damage.
- **Fertility.** Soil test first. Typical irrigated fresh-market rate 120–160 lb N/ac (NDSU gives
  160 ± 10 for early reds and Yukon Gold; too much N delays tuber set and makes vine kill hard),
  P and K to soil test (potatoes are K-hungry; 150–250 lb K2O/ac is common on sandy soils).
  Split N: two-thirds at planting, the rest at hilling. Budget $350/ac.
- **Irrigation.** 1–1.5 in/week from tuber initiation (flowering) through bulking; keep soil
  moisture even to avoid knobs, growth cracks and hollow heart. Drip tape (one line per row,
  ~$400–500/ac installed) or the farm's overhead if a field is under a pivot; let soil dry to
  60–65% available water at vine kill (Rutgers 2026).
- **Weeds.** Hilling and cultivation do most of the work; pre-emergence metribuzin or
  s-metolachlor labelled options where needed (check variety sensitivity to metribuzin).
- **Insects.** Colorado potato beetle is the certain pest in Kansas (K-State MF3541): scout from
  emergence, treat at 1 adult or 4 larvae per plant before 20% defoliation, rotate modes of action
  (spinosad, neonicotinoid at planting, Group 28, Group 30; never the same class twice in a
  season), rotate fields ≥¼ mile from last year's potatoes, straw mulch and hand removal on small
  blocks. Also potato leafhopper (hopperburn), aphids (virus vectors; less critical for a non-seed
  crop), flea beetles, wireworms on ground out of sod. Budget $200–260/ac insect plus fungicide.
- **Diseases.** Early blight (Alternaria) in warm humid June weather, chlorothalonil/mancozeb
  program at 7–10 days once rows close; late blight is rare in Kansas but possible in a wet spring;
  common scab (keep pH <6.5, even moisture at tuber set); Rhizoctonia black scurf (plant into warm
  soil, avoid deep planting); blackleg/soft rot (certified seed, do not harvest wet); Verticillium
  wilt (rotation). Rotate 3 years away from potatoes, tomatoes and peppers.
- **Physiological.** Heat (soil >80°F) causes heat sprouts, knobs and second growth and is the
  main Kansas yield limiter; greening from poor hilling; hollow heart in big tubers under uneven
  water.
- **Harvest.** New potatoes from flowering onward (mid-June) by hand or digger for chef June
  sales. Main crop: kill vines (mow or flail, or let senesce) 2–3 weeks before digging so skins
  set; dig when soil is 50–65°F and dry, early morning in July; one-row chain digger at
  ~0.3–0.5 ac/hr with 2–4 people picking into field crates behind it. Avoid bruising; do not
  dig when pulp temperature >75°F.
- **Post-harvest.** Do not wash before storage. Cure 10–14 days at 50–60°F and 85–95% RH
  (Iowa State; wound healing), then store at 40–45°F, 90–95% RH, dark, with air movement; the
  shared CoolBot room can hold this. Reds and yellows keep 4–7 months to the April end of the
  winter window; fingerlings 2–4 months; new potatoes 1–3 weeks. Wash, dry and grade the week of
  delivery. Chef packs: 5 lb or 10 lb paper bags or 50 lb carton by size (creamer A/B/C);
  grocery: 50 lb cartons or 5 lb consumer bags with PLU/PTI if a chain.

## 9. Profitability verdict
- **Best channel: chef-direct**, fingerlings and colored specialty types first, new potatoes in
  June as the door-opener. Grocery at terminal prices loses money at this scale and is only a
  seconds and overflow outlet; the farmers market pays the best price but Wichita's winter
  markets cannot move an acre of potatoes, so it is a side channel for 3–5 lb bags.
- **Stage-1 acres: 0.75 ac** (≈ 11,000 lb marketable; 7,000 lb to chefs, rest to market and
  seconds), planted only after ≥6 chef LOIs for weekly winter potatoes at ≥$1.25/lb
  (competitor-analysis rule) and a seed order placed by January.
- **Family income at FAMILY rate: ~$6,000/ac, ~$4,500 for 0.75 ac** ($1,750 wages on ~160 hr
  plus ~$2,800 profit), falling to ~$2,200/ac net if the labor is hired.
- What changes the verdict: chef price realized below $1.25/lb (net ≈ 0 at $1.12) → drop
  fingerlings and keep only new potatoes in June; marketable yield below ~11,000 lb/ac for two
  seasons → stop; a second person dedicated to produce and 3+ acres → buy the two-row conveyor
  digger and open the grocery independents at $0.80+/lb.

## 10. Risks and disqualifiers
| Risk | Likelihood | Effect | Control / disqualifier |
|---|---|---|---|
| Harvest collides with wheat harvest (late June–July) | high | yield and quality loss if dig slips into August heat | dig new potatoes mid-June before wheat; schedule main dig for the first week after wheat; kill vines early; if neither works, this crop is disqualified on this farm |
| Soil heat >80°F in June–July (heat sprouts, second growth, soft rot) | high in a hot year | adverse yield 9,000 lb | early varieties, straw mulch, irrigation to cool soil, early planting |
| Colorado potato beetle resistance and defoliation | high | 30–50% yield loss untreated | MF3541 program; rotate classes; field rotation ≥¼ mile |
| Scab on high-pH Sedgwick soils | medium | grade-out to seconds, chefs reject | pick lowest-pH sandy field; tolerant varieties; even moisture; if field pH >7.0 with no alternative field, disqualifier for washed specialty grades |
| Chef accounts fewer than 6 or price <$1.25 | medium | chef net falls below zero near $1.12 | LOIs first; floor rule |
| Storage failure (room warm, sprouting, rot) | medium | lose winter window | CoolBot with alarm; cull before storage; sell fingerlings by January |
| Seed sold out or arrives late | medium | miss March window | order December |
| FSMA / GAP paperwork if Whole Foods or Dillons DC | low at stage 1 | $1,000–2,500/yr | stay with chef and independents until 3+ ac |
