# Peppers (bell, specialty sweet, hot and specialty for chefs)

## 1. Fit for this farm
- **Soil and water**: same ground as tomatoes: deep, well-drained loam, pH 6.2–6.8, drip
  under black plastic, 1–1.5 in/week in July. Peppers tolerate heat better than tomatoes
  once set but are more sensitive to wet feet and to cold soil at transplanting.
- **Season**: transplant May 10–25 in Sedgwick County (K-State calendar May 9 statewide,
  earlier in southern Kansas; soil 60°F at 3 in.); first green bells early–mid July, red
  and colored bells from early August, picking through frost. Tunnel: transplant
  mid-April, pick from late June into November; colored bells and chef specialty types
  ripen reliably only under cover in Kansas.
- **Equipment**: shares every implement and the tunnel with tomatoes; nothing in the
  grain fleet harvests peppers.
- **Mechanizable**: no (hand pick, 3–6 pickings; conveyor saves carrying only).
- **Storable**: short. Bells hold 2–3 weeks at 45–50°F and 90–95% RH; hot peppers
  similar; neither holds across a market week without cold storage. Dried or smoked
  chiles (chipotle, paprika, dried flakes) are a storable chef product with a small
  dehydrator: worth a trial, not in the base model.
- **Grain calendar conflict**: medium–high; lighter than tomatoes because peppers are
  picked weekly rather than three times a week and staking is a single pass, but the
  July–September picking still overlaps spraying, silage and fall seedbed work.
- **Trust**: as tomatoes; the only structure is the shared tunnel.

## 3. Agronomy
- **Transplants**: seed 8 weeks before set-out (mid-March field, late February tunnel) at
  80–85°F germination; peppers are slower than tomatoes; 50- or 72-cell plugs, no bloom
  at set-out.
- **Planting**: rows 5–6 ft on center under black plastic, in-row 12–18 in. (K-State
  trials 18 in.; many growers double-row 12–15 in. on a 30 in. bed): 9,000–14,500
  plants/ac; the model uses 12,000. A 30×96 tunnel holds ~700 plants in four double
  rows.
- **Support**: short stakes and one or two strings (UC: 62 hr/ac install) prevent lodging
  of heavy bell plants after August storms; specialty types often need none.
- **Irrigation**: drip, 1 in/week May–June, 1.5 in/week July–August; steady water
  prevents blossom end rot and fruit drop. Tunnel 100% drip.
- **Fertility**: 100–130 lb N/ac, 30–40% pre-plant, remainder fertigated weekly from
  first fruit set; excess early N delays set; calcium adequate via pH and soil.
- **Weeds**: plastic in-row; cultivation or fabric between rows; labeled pre-emergents
  (s-metolachlor, napropamide) with the dedicated clean sprayer.
- **Insects (Kansas)**: European corn borer and corn earworm larvae entering fruit under
  the cap (worst adjacent to corn and sorghum; scout and treat at moth flights),
  aphids (virus vectors, honeydew), thrips (TSWV), flea beetles at set-out, two-spotted
  spider mite in tunnels, stink bugs, hornworm occasionally; pepper weevil is a southern
  pest, rare in Kansas but check transplants from southern greenhouses. Pepper maggot
  possible on bells.
- **Diseases (Kansas)**: bacterial spot is the main field disease (treated seed, X10R
  varieties, copper plus mancozeb, no overhead water, 3-year rotation away from
  nightshades); Phytophthora blight and root rot in wet, poorly drained soil (raised
  beds, no ponding zones, resistant 'Paladin'/'Aristotle'); anthracnose on ripe fruit in
  wet Augusts; TSWV and cucumber mosaic via thrips and aphids (K-State lists TSWV on
  pepper); southern blight in hot soils; powdery mildew in tunnels late season.
- **Physiological**: blossom end rot (K-State lists it for pepper; same calcium and water
  cause as tomato); **sunscald** on exposed fruit when foliage is sparse or plants lodge
  (K-State lists sunscalded fruit; dense canopy, shade cloth in tunnel); **heat blossom
  drop**: flowers abort above about 90°F days and 75°F nights, so field peppers set in
  June and again in late August with a mid-summer lull; the tunnel with 30% shade cloth
  carries set through July. Colored bells in the field often rot before coloring in
  Kansas heat; the premium colored and specialty program belongs in the tunnel.
- **Harvest**: green bells at full size and firm walls (3–4 lb/hr slower than slicers:
  rate about 100 lb/hr); colored bells at 80%+ color; shishito/padrón at 2–3 in. every
  3–4 days; jalapeño green at full size or red for chefs; poblano dark green and 4–6 in.
  Cut or snap with the cap intact; field heat out within 2 hours.
- **Post-harvest**: dry brush or no wash (bells bruise and rot after washing unless dried
  and cooled fast); grade by size and color; 45–50°F, 90–95% RH, 2–3 weeks for bells,
  1–2 weeks for thin-walled types; never below 45°F (chilling injury). Culls to chefs for
  roasting at $0.60–1.00/lb, or dried.

## 9. Profitability verdict
- **Best channel**: chef-direct specialty and hot peppers (shishito, padrón, poblano,
  'Carmen', colored bells) bundled with the tomato deliveries: $16,700/ac net at FAMILY,
  $45.46 per labor hour, and the smallest selling-time burden. Market/CSA is slightly
  higher per acre ($18,200) but the market ceiling for peppers is only 60–150 lb/day.
  Grocery bells are a seconds outlet only.
- **Recommended stage-1 scale**: **0.15–0.25 acre field block (1,800–3,000 plants:
  green bells for market and CSA, jalapeño and poblano, shishito) plus two rows (~150
  plants) of colored bells and specialty sweets in the shared tomato tunnel.** Expected
  season ~3,700–4,500 lb, 120–160 labor hours, FAMILY-rate net ≈ $4,000–5,500, family
  income ≈ $5,500–7,000. Peppers are added to the tomato program for table variety and
  chef order size, not as a standalone enterprise; a full acre is justified only after
  two seasons of chef demand above 500 lb/week.
- **Net income to the family at FAMILY rate**: $22,050/ac field chef-direct (wages
  $5,335 + net $16,715); $9,010 per tunnel chef-direct.
- **What changes the verdict**: (1) chef interest in a dried/smoked chile line → a
  storable product that fits the winter storables channel in competitor-analysis.md and
  lifts peppers above tomatoes in strategic fit; (2) grocery price for bells confirmed
  under $1.25/lb → drop grocery entirely; (3) a second tunnel funded by EQIP → colored
  bell program expands to half a tunnel; (4) no dedicated person → do not plant.

## 10. Risks and disqualifiers
| Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bacterial spot from infected transplants | medium | high | treated seed, own plugs, X10R varieties, copper program |
| Phytophthora in a wet June on heavy ground | medium | high | raised plastic beds on loam, no ponding zones, resistant bells |
| Heat blossom drop and sunscald July–August | high | medium | tunnel shade cloth, dense canopy, staking |
| Colored bells rot before coloring in field | high | medium | colored program in tunnel only |
| Corn borer/earworm fruit entry near grain fields | medium | medium | scout moth flights, isolate plot from corn and sorghum edges, treat at threshold |
| Bell price undercut by imports at grocery | high | medium | no wholesale bells; chef and market only |
| Labor conflict with tomatoes and grain in August | high | medium | peppers are weekly picks; schedule them on non-tomato days |
| Hail and wind (field) | medium | high | NAP insurance; tunnel for premium types |
| Food-safety incident | low | very high | GAP practices, dry handling, lot records, liability cover |
**Disqualifiers**: no committed person for the shared produce enterprise; no legal water;
chef blended price below **$1.75/lb** or grocery below **$1.25/lb** (hired-labor
breakeven); peppers planned without tomatoes (the pepper line alone does not justify the
chef route or the tunnel); any pepper acre beyond 0.25 ac before the first season's
sales are recorded.
