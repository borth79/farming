# Green beans (fresh-market bush snap beans)

## 1. Fit for this farm
- **Role.** A farmers-market and CSA staple that fills the table June–October, not an
  enterprise in its own right. Low price per pound ($1–4/lb), heavy hand picking (15–30 lb
  per person-hour), no storage (7–10 days at 40–45 F). Beans earn their place only if they
  bring customers to a table that also sells flowers, sweet corn, tomatoes and melons, or if
  3+ acres are machine-picked for a buyer who has signed first.
- **Soil, water, season.** Warm-season legume; any well-drained loam, pH 6–7; planted after
  frost (soil 60 F) through early August for a fall crop (K-State Shawnee). Needs ~1 in/week,
  critical at bloom and pod fill; blossoms drop and pods curl when flowering in 95–100 F
  heat with hot wind, so Kansas plantings aim to flower before July 1 and after August 20.
  Drip or overhead both work; drip keeps foliage dry (bacterial blight).
- **Equipment.** Plants with the existing row-crop planter at 30-in rows (bean plates) or a
  push seeder on beds; cultivates with existing tools; sprayer existing. Harvest is the
  whole question: hand picking, or a used one-row tow-behind picker (Oxbo/Pixall BH100) that
  the farm's 45+ hp tractors can pull. The combine has no role (fresh market).
- **Labor.** 300–500 hr/acre hand-picked, 90–130 hr/acre machine-picked. Hand picking is
  3 pickings per planting spaced 4–7 days, each a 2–4 hr job per 100-ft bed pair; picking
  falls in June–July and September, between wheat harvest and fall drilling.
- **Trust.** No structure, lease, water or business-line change at stage 1; a picker purchase
  over $25,000 is a capital decision for the trust checklist.
- **Food safety.** Beans count toward FSMA produce sales thresholds (business-plan.md); hand
  contact crop, so wash-hand stations and clean totes.

## 3. Agronomy
**Planting.** Wichita frost-free ~Apr 15–20 (verify K-State freeze map); first planting
Apr 25–May 5 when soil at 2 in is 60 F (Provider tolerates 55 F); succession every 10–14 days
to June 10, pause through the heat, resume Jul 20–Aug 10 for fall pickings in September–early
October (K-State Shawnee: fall beans early August; Illinois/Maryland: every 2–3 weeks to
late July). Six plantings a season; each occupies the ground 8–9 weeks. Seed 1–1.5 in deep,
2–3 in in-row, 30-in rows (row-crop planter) or two rows per 4-ft bed at 18 in; 80–100 lb
seed/acre. Do not pre-irrigate heavily; beans rot in cold wet soil. Rhizobium inoculant
optional on new ground.

**Fertility.** Soil test; beans fix some N but respond to 30–50 lb N/acre pre-plant on sandy
ground; P and K to soil test (medium); avoid excess N (vine, delayed set). Zn deficiency
shows on high-pH soils; molybdenum rarely.

**Water.** 1 in/week; 1.5 in/week during bloom and pod fill in June; drip tape (one per
2 rows) keeps leaves dry; if overhead, water at dawn so plants dry by picking time.

**Weeds.** Pre-plant tillage, stale seedbed, cultivate twice before canopy (row-crop
cultivator at 30-in rows); hand hoe in-row once. Labeled herbicides exist for snap beans
(trifluralin, S-metolachlor, bentazon, clethodim; check Kansas labels) but a small plot is
usually cultivated. Palmer amaranth escapes must be pulled before seed.

**Insects.**
- Mexican bean beetle: the defining pest of Kansas beans; adults and spiny yellow larvae
  skeletonize leaves from the underside; damage peaks July–August (Clemson, JIPM 2016), so
  early May and August plantings escape the worst; scout undersides twice a week; hand crush
  egg masses; spinosad, pyrethrin, neem or Pediobius foveolatus parasitoid releases (organic
  growers); resistant lines (Regal, Idaho Refugee) are old and not fresh-market standard.
- Bean leaf beetle: holes in leaves, pod scarring in fall plantings.
- Potato leafhopper: hopperburn on fall beans in dry years.
- Aphids and thrips: minor; spider mites in August heat.
- Corn earworm and stink bugs: pod feeding on fall plantings next to corn or sorghum.
- Seedcorn maggot in cool wet soil (treated seed).

**Diseases.**
- Bacterial blight (common and halo): water-soaked spots with yellow halos, pod lesions;
  seed-borne and splash-spread; western-grown certified seed, no picking or cultivating when
  foliage is wet (K-State Shawnee), drip, 2-year rotation, copper has limited value.
- Rust: in humid late summer; resistant varieties, sulfur or chlorothalonil.
- White mold (Sclerotinia) and Pythium root rot in wet soil; anthracnose on seed.
- Bean common mosaic and curly top (leafhopper) viruses: resistant varieties (most modern).
- Heat blossom drop (physiological) above 95 F; pod curl and "pollywog" short pods.

**Harvest.** Pods 4–6 in, pencil thickness, seeds not bulging, snap cleanly; first picking
50–60 days after sowing, then 2 more pickings at 4–7 day intervals (3 pickings per
planting; 2 in heat, 4 in a cool September). Pick after dew dries. Picking rate 15–30 lb per
person-hour (SARE, UW); the first and second pickings carry 70–80% of yield. Machine: a
one-row tow-behind picker makes one pass when 50–60% of pods are market size, picks
375–1,000 lb/hr with a 2-person crew, destroys the plant, and leaves 20–40% of pods (too
small, too large, or unpicked); grading out leaves and broken pods afterward at 500–750 lb/hr.

**Post-harvest.** Shade immediately; hydrocool or cold-room to 40–45 F within 2 hours; 95%
humidity; never below 40 F for long (chilling, russet spots); vented bushel cartons (30 lb)
or 1-lb and 2-lb bags for market; 7–10 days of shelf life; not storable (schema
storable_months = 0).

**Machine-picked scenario (3–5 acres, used BH100 at $35,000 over 10 years and 4 acres =
$875/acre depreciation):** yield 3,800 lb once-over, ~120 hr/acre, variable 1,600, fixed
1,925. At grocer $1.10: revenue 3,762, net **−$1,830/acre** even at HIRED labor of only

acres, which no Wichita grocer pays for standard beans and which exceeds the chef channel's
4,000–10,000 lb/yr ceiling (1–2 acres). The picker therefore pays only with a processor or
distributor contract at $1.70+ (none identified) or with a pea crop sharing the machine.
Payback on $1,500 of hand-pick capital: first season. Payback on a $35,000 picker: none at
grocer prices; 6+ years at $2.25 on 4 acres.

## 9. Profitability verdict
- **Best channel:** farmers market and CSA at $3/lb in 1-lb and 2-lb bags, with chef-direct
  for filet and colored beans as a second line; grocers never at this scale (below the
  competitor-analysis floor logic: a crop that nets negative per hour is declined).
- **Stage-1 scale: 0.1–0.25 acre** (4–10 bed pairs, 6 succession plantings, Provider then
  Bronco/Jade, one August planting), hand-picked by family, sold only at the two Saturday
  markets and in CSA shares as a table-filler. Expected 500–1,250 lb sold, gross $1,500–3,750,
  **family income (wages plus net at FAMILY) $1,100–2,300**, net to the enterprise $400–900.
  Net per labor hour $6–7 at base: below the HIRED wage, so beans never justify hiring a
  picker.
- **Do not scale.** Beans fail the business plan's labor-hour rule unless (a) a buyer signs an
  LOI at ≥$1.70/lb for 15,000+ lb (4 acres machine-picked), or (b) filet beans for chefs at
  $4+/lb are proven in year 1, or (c) a shared or custom picker is available (none known in
  the Wichita area; verify with Amish growers near Yoder/Hutchinson and the SEK Produce
  Auction).
- **What changes the verdict:** market price above $3.50/lb with 90% sell-through (net per hour
  reaches $10); a pick-your-own bean patch at $1.50/lb (removes 280 hr/acre, needs trust
  checklist for public access); machine purchase shared with a pea crop or a neighbor.
