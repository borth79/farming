# Pumpkins and winter squash

> Research note: K-State, USDA AMS and UK CCD hosts failed to resolve in this session; figures
> are from search summaries and are marked **verify** where the number was not shown directly.
> The 2024 K-State pumpkin trial was run at Haysville in Sedgwick County, so its yield range is
> the best local anchor the project has for any produce crop. Wichita retail per-pumpkin and
> u-pick prices were not found online and must be collected on site in October 2027.

## 1. Fit for this farm
- **Lowest labor per dollar of any produce crop here** (~85–110 hr/acre, one harvest), the
  only produce crop that is **storable** (pumpkins 2–3 months, butternut 3–6), and the one with
  an **agritourism option**. The business plan ranks it second after sweet corn; the
  mechanization file lists it as the "easiest vine crop."
- **Equipment fit — good.** Direct-seeded with the row planter (large-seed plate or vacuum,
  60–72 in rows, or every other 30-in row), cultivated and sprayed with existing equipment.
  Harvest is cut-and-windrow by hand, then loaded by hand or with a boom conveyor into the
  farm's existing wagons or truck. Stage 1 needs no new implement.
- **Soil and water.** Well-drained loam or sandy loam; avoid the water-management agent's
  ponding zones (fruit rot). Vine crops use ~1 in/week; pumpkins can be grown dryland in
  Kansas but fruit size and set in July–August heat improve with drip; the K-State trial was
  drip-irrigated. Plan drip for the market/agritourism block; wholesale jacks can be dryland
  with a yield haircut (adverse case).
- **Season fits the grain calendar.** Planted **June 1–20** after wheat harvest starts (or
  just before), needing little attention in June; harvest **September 10–October 5** before
  fall wheat drilling peaks and after corn/sorghum harvest begins — the only real overlap is
  with sorghum harvest in late September. October selling is the quiet month for grain work.
- **Rotation.** Cucurbits rotate on a 3–4 year cycle within the plot (business-plan.md);
  pumpkins follow sweet corn or grain well. Check plant-back for sulfonylurea and clopyralid
  residues; atrazine carryover injures cucurbits — do not follow atrazine-treated corn or
  sorghum within the label interval (**verify** label: typically the next season only with
  caution).
- **Trust fit.** Field sales to markets, chefs and grocers raise no trust issue. **U-pick or
  an on-farm pumpkin event is a new business line with public access to trust land**: it
  passes `knowledge/operations/family-trust-constraints.md` (liability, insurance, signage,
  trustee consent) before any advertising. Section 7 and 10 carry the caveat.

## 3. Agronomy
| Item | Recommendation | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Planting date | Safe after frost in early–mid May with soil ≥60 °F, but **most Kansas growers plant early to mid-June** so fruit matures for October; 100–120 day types planted June 5–15 mature mid–late September | K-State Butler Co.; MF1115 |
| Spacing / population | Large jacks: 6–8 ft rows × 3–4 ft ⇒ 1,600–2,400 plants/acre; pie and butternut (vining) 5–6 ft × 2–3 ft ⇒ 2,900–4,400; semi-bush squash denser | general extension; verify MF2030 |
| Fertility | 60–100 lb N/acre (half at planting, half at vine run), P/K to soil test; excess N delays maturity and grows vine not fruit | general; verify MF2030 |
| Irrigation | ~1 in/week, most critical at flowering and fruit set (July); stop 2 weeks before harvest to harden rinds | general |
| Weeds | Pre: ethalfluralin + clomazone (Strategy) or halosulfuron (Sandea); cultivate until vines run; then vines shade. Palmer amaranth escapes by hand before seed set | labels; general |
| **Cucumber beetles (striped, spotted)** | Attack seedlings and transmit **bacterial wilt** (vines collapse in days; no cure). Scout at emergence; treat at ~1 beetle/plant on seedlings (threshold **verify**); neonicotinoid seed treatment or in-furrow at planting is the standard protection; foliar pyrethroid later only in the evening to spare bees | K-State pest list; Midwest guides |
| **Squash bugs** | Kansas's signature pumpkin pest; adults overwinter in debris, lay bronze egg masses on leaf undersides June–July. Treat **when egg masses hatch (nymphs)** — adults are hard to kill; 2–3 sprays; clean up debris in fall | K-State MF3308 (verify thresholds) |
| **Squash vine borer** | Moth flies June–July in Kansas; larvae bore into stems; no control once inside. Sanitation, fall/spring tillage to kill pupae, early summer-squash trap plants, insecticide at the stem base during moth flight; **C. moschata (butternut) is nearly immune** — a reason to weight the mix toward butternut | K-State MF3309 |
| Aphids, mites | late season; usually tolerated | K-State |
| **Powdery mildew** | Serious every year in Kansas; defoliates vines in August and exposes fruit to sunscald and poor handles. PMR varieties plus 2–4 fungicide sprays from first sign (chlorothalonil + rotating systemic) | K-State Butler Co. |
| Downy mildew, anthracnose, Plectosporium, Phytophthora fruit rot | wet years and low ground | Midwest guides |
| Viruses (WMV, ZYMV, CMV) | aphid-borne; late plantings worse; mottled fruit unsaleable | general |
| Physiological | Poor fruit set in heat above ~95 °F (July); hollow or soft handles from N excess or PM; sunscald | general |
| Pollination | Native bees often adequate; one hive per 1–2 acres recommended for consistent set — a bee-keeping neighbor or $60–100/hive rental | general |
| Harvest timing | Full color, hard rind (thumbnail test), dry stems; cut with pruners leaving a 3–4 in handle; **harvest before frost** and before the first hard freeze; Haysville trial harvested Sep 4–Oct 1 | K-State 2024 trial |
| Curing and storage | No warm cure needed for butternut (WFLO/Alabama); let jacks dry a week in the field or on pallets; **store at 50–55 °F, 50–70% RH**, off the ground, not in a damp shed; pumpkins 2–3 months, butternut 3–6, acorn/delicata 1–2 | OSU, UVM, UMass, UC Davis |
| Post-harvest | Wipe soil, cull soft spots, no washing of jacks; chlorinated wash for squash going to grocery; store jacks in shade — sun and heat shorten life | general |

## 9. Profitability verdict
- **Best channel per acre:** chef-direct winter squash ($10,215 net/acre at FAMILY, ~$131/hour),
  because the farm can deliver cured butternut, kabocha and pie pumpkins from November to March
  when no Wichita grower has local squash — but it is the same squash line already in the
  chef-direct storables plan, limited by ~20–25 accounts (~1–2 acres). **Best channel for
  jack-o-lanterns:** farmers market by the piece ($9,250/acre, ~$95/hour) up to ~1 acre;
  wholesale ($4,094/acre, $59/hour) is the safe volume outlet and the only channel that can take
  3+ acres.
- **Stage-1 recommendation: 2 acres** — 1 acre of mixed jacks, pie and specialty for the two
  markets with overflow to independents in bins, and 1 acre of butternut, kabocha, delicata and
  pie pumpkins for chef accounts and winter market dates. Expected family income at FAMILY rate
  ≈ $15,000–20,000 for ~200 hours, mostly in September–October. Drip on both acres; direct-seed
  June 5–15.
- **What changes the verdict:** (up) a confirmed Dillons store-level or independent-grocer
  program at ≥$0.30/lb justifies a third wholesale acre and a used conveyor; a trust-approved
  agritourism event adds a channel for culls and small fruit. (down) wholesale offers below
  $0.30/lb for jacks or $0.60/lb for butternut (decline per competitor rule 3); squash bug or
  vine borer losses above ~30% on C. pepo types shift the mix to butternut; no drip means
  adverse-case yields in a hot July.

## 10. Risks and disqualifiers
| Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation / disqualifier |
|---|---|---|---|
| Unsold jack-o-lanterns on November 1 (zero value) | medium | high for the market acre | pre-sell bins to independents by September 1; cap market acreage at 1; cull to feed |
| Squash bugs, cucumber beetles/bacterial wilt, vine borer | high (Kansas) | medium–high | seed treatment, nymph-timed sprays, sanitation, moschata share; **disqualify C. pepo types** if losses exceed 30% two years running |
| Powdery mildew defoliation, sunscald, weak handles | high | medium | PMR varieties, fungicide program |
| July heat >95 °F at fruit set | medium | medium | drip; June planting sets fruit in late July–August |
| Early frost before harvest (rare before Oct 10 here) | low | high | harvest by Oct 5 |
| Atrazine or clopyralid carryover from the grain program | medium | high | plot history check; no cucurbits after atrazine within label interval |
| Herbicide drift (dicamba/2,4-D) from grain fields | medium | high | buffers, notify applicators (business-plan.md) |
| Storage losses (damp shed, <50 °F) | medium | medium | heated/ventilated shed space at 50–55 °F |
| **Agritourism liability and trust exposure** (public on trust land, children, vehicles, animals) | — | high | **Disqualifier for stage 1**; only after family-trust checklist, Agritourism Act registration, signage, insurance endorsement and trustee consent |
| Overlap with sorghum harvest in late September | medium | low–medium | harvest pumpkins in two passes, Sep 10–20 and Oct 1–5 |
