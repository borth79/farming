# Tomatoes (field and high tunnel; slicers, heirlooms, cherry/grape, paste)

## 1. Fit for this farm
- **Soil and water**: tomatoes want deep, well-drained loam, pH 6.2–6.8, and 1–2 in/week
  of water in July through drip under plastic mulch. The farm's loam receiving zones with a
  well that can legally water a garden-scale plot qualify; ponding zones and recent
  atrazine, sulfonylurea or clopyralid ground do not (plant-back intervals per label).
- **Season**: south-central Kansas transplant window about May 1–15 (K-State calendar
  May 2 statewide, 1–2 weeks earlier in southern Kansas); first pick early July in the
  field, ripe fruit through frost (mid-October). A high tunnel moves transplanting to
  early April and the first pick to early–mid June and extends picking into November,
  about 4–6 weeks at each end (business-plan.md; Kentucky CCD "4–5 weeks ahead").
- **Equipment**: shares the tractor, a bed shaper and used plastic-mulch layer, the drip
  pump and the wash and cold-storage station with peppers, melons and cucurbits. Nothing
  in the grain fleet harvests tomatoes; everything is hand work.
- **Mechanizable**: no. Fresh-market harvest, pruning, tying and grading are hand tasks;
  a picking conveyor only removes carrying (mechanization file). Rule 2 of that file
  caps hand-picked crops at what one person can pick in 20 hr/week until a second
  person is committed.
- **Storable**: no. Ripe fruit holds 5–10 days at 55–60°F; never refrigerate below 50°F.
  Every pound must be sold within the week it is picked.
- **Grain calendar conflict**: high. Transplanting and trellising (May) overlap pre-plant
  work and spraying; peak picking (July–August, three pickings a week) overlaps wheat
  harvest clean-up, sorghum and soybean spraying and silage. Tunnel production shifts
  the early peak into June, directly onto wheat harvest. A dedicated person is a
  precondition, not an option.
- **Trust**: a produce plot on owned land is a land-use change but not a new entity or
  lease; a high tunnel is a structure, so the family-trust checklist
  (`knowledge/operations/family-trust-constraints.md`) runs before the EQIP contract is
  signed, because the NRCS contract binds the land for the practice life.

## 3. Agronomy
- **Transplants**: seed 6–7 weeks before set-out (mid-March for field, late February for
  tunnel); harden off; graft heirlooms for the tunnel. A 50-cell plug is the standard.
- **Planting**: field, after soil at 4 in. is 60°F, normally May 1–15 in Sedgwick County;
  risk of late frost through about April 20. Rows 5–6 ft on center under black plastic
  (white or reflective after June 1 in the tunnel to cut heat), in-row 18 in. for
  determinates (K-State Olathe trials used 18 in.), 24 in. for indeterminate heirlooms
  and cherries: 2,900–3,600 plants/ac field; a 30×96 tunnel holds four to five rows of
  ~96 ft, roughly 450–520 plants.
- **Trellis**: Florida weave with 5–6 ft wooden or metal stakes every 2–3 plants for
  determinates; string trellis to overhead wire or lower-and-lean in the tunnel for
  indeterminates. Prune indeterminates to one or two leaders; sucker determinates to the
  first flower cluster only.
- **Irrigation**: drip under mulch, 1 in/week in May–June rising to 1.5–2 in/week in July
  (about 0.3 in/day peak); even moisture is the main defense against blossom end rot and
  cracking. Tunnel crops need 100% of water from drip.
- **Fertility**: soil test; typical 100–120 lb N/ac split (40% pre-plant, remainder
  fertigated weekly from first fruit set), P and K to test, calcium adequate with pH
  6.2–6.8; avoid excess N before fruit set. Tunnel soils accumulate salts; leach or flood
  between crops and test EC yearly.
- **Weeds**: plastic mulch in-row; between rows cultivation, a cover-crop or landscape
  fabric strip, or a labeled pre-emergent (metribuzin, s-metolachlor; check label and the
  separate clean sprayer rule in business-plan.md).
- **Insects (Kansas)**: tomato and tobacco hornworm (hand-pick or Bt), stink bugs
  (cloudy-spot fruit damage, worst near sorghum and soybean field edges in August), two-
  spotted spider mites (hot dry July, worse in tunnels; miticide or predatory mites),
  thrips (TSWV vector; reflective mulch, weed control), aphids and whiteflies in tunnels,
  flea beetles and cutworms at transplanting, tomato fruitworm (= corn earworm) moving
  from sweet corn and sorghum. Scout twice weekly.
- **Diseases (Kansas)**: septoria leaf spot is more common than early blight in Kansas
  (K-State); both start low and worsen after fruit set, so a protectant program
  (chlorothalonil or mancozeb plus copper, rotated with a strobilurin or SDHI) runs from
  first fruit set every 7–14 days in the field; stake, mulch, drip and 3-year rotation
  away from nightshades cut inoculum. Bacterial spot and speck: treated seed, copper plus
  mancozeb, no overhead water, no working wet plants. Tomato spotted wilt virus (K-State
  L723): no cure; resistant hybrids, thrips control, rogue infected plants. Fusarium and
  Verticillium: resistant varieties (VFN) and grafting. Tunnels trade leaf diseases for
  leaf mold, powdery mildew, botrytis and whitefly; roll up sides for air movement.
- **Physiological**: blossom end rot (calcium uptake failure under uneven water and heat;
  mulch, drip, no root pruning); cracking and radial splits after rain on heirlooms (the
  tunnel is the fix: rain never hits the fruit); catfacing after cool nights at set;
  sunscald where foliage is lost; and **heat set failure**: K-State notes poor set when
  nights stay above 75°F and days above 95°F with dry wind, and blossom drop above 90°F
  with low humidity. In Sedgwick County that is most of mid-July to mid-August, so field
  tomatoes typically show a 2–3 week gap in ripe fruit in late August. Shade cloth (30%)
  on the tunnel and heat-set varieties ('Heatmaster', 'Solar Fire', 'Florida 91') narrow
  the gap. Plan sales around it: the chef needs continuity, so the tunnel plus a late
  (June 15–25) field planting covers September.
- **Harvest**: breaker to pink for grocery and chef (ripen at 65–70°F), vine-ripe for
  market; every 2–3 days in peak season; morning pick into padded totes, never stacked
  deeper than two layers. Hand pick rate 60–100 lb/hr for slicers (WSU budget 80 lb/hr),
  25–40 lb/hr for cherries (PFI: ~5 min per pint picked and packed).
- **Post-harvest**: no field wash for slicers (wipe); grade on a padded table; cherries
  into pints; hold at 55–60°F and 85–90% RH; 7–10 days for firm hybrids, 3–5 for ripe
  heirlooms. Culls and seconds (cracked, catfaced) go to chefs for sauce at $0.75–1.25/lb
  or into CSA boxes.

## 9. Profitability verdict
- **Best channel**: chef-direct heirloom, cherry and slicer program from the tunnel,
  with the farmers market taking the surplus and seconds. Market/CSA nets slightly more
  per acre on paper ($26,300 vs $24,900 at FAMILY) but needs 90 more hours, depends on
  150–400 lb/day of table sales, and competes with 100+ vendors in peak tomato season;
  chef-direct earns the most per hour ($45.76) and sells in 10 lb flats. Grocery/wholesale
  is marginal ($7,700/ac hired, negative in the adverse case) and is a seconds outlet only.
- **Recommended stage-1 scale**: **one 30×96 high tunnel (0.066 ac) plus a 0.25 ac field
  block** (about 800 plants, half heat-set hybrids, 20 heirloom, 150 cherry, a late June
  planting for September). Expected season: ~11,700 lb marketable, 480–520 labor hours,
  FAMILY-rate net ≈ $15,500 (tunnel $9,400 + field $6,200), family income (wages + net) ≈
  $21,000. That is within one person's 20 hr/week in June–September only if peppers are
  held to a quarter acre or less in the same year.
- **Net income to the family at FAMILY rate**: $32,720/ac field chef-direct (wages
  $7,865 + net $24,855); $12,720 per tunnel chef-direct.
- **What changes the verdict**: (1) fewer than 6 chef LOIs for weekly summer tomatoes
  at ≥$2.50/lb blended → drop to one tunnel sold at market only; (2) Firefly/RISE holding
  the heirloom accounts → lead with cherry/grape and heat-set slicers and September
  supply, where Firefly's rooftop is thin; (3) EQIP denied → still build the tunnel if two
  seasons of market sales are proven, otherwise field only; (4) no dedicated person →
  **do not plant**; (5) water right not confirmed for the plot → tunnel only on the
  domestic well if under the DWR garden threshold (verify).
