# Sweet potatoes

## 1. Fit for this farm
- **Climate and soil.** A heat-loving, 100–120 day crop that likes Kansas summers; K-State's
  historic bulletin names the Arkansas River valley sandy soils as the state's natural sweet
  potato ground, which is Sedgwick County's west side. Wants well-drained sandy loam, pH 5.8–6.5
  tolerated to 7.0, low N. Heavy clay gives rough, cracked roots.
- **Water.** Drought tolerant once established but marketable grade needs ~1 in/week in July–Aug
  and even moisture during root sizing; drip on raised beds is the standard. Dryland is possible
  on sandy ground with yield and grade risk (adverse case).
- **Season.** Transplant slips late May to mid-June (soil ≥65°F, 2–3 weeks after last frost),
  dig mid-September to mid-October before frost. **Planting lands between wheat spraying and
  wheat harvest; digging lands on soybean and sorghum harvest and wheat planting.** Fits better
  than potatoes because the dig window is 4–5 weeks wide and frost, not heat, is the deadline.
- **Equipment.** Existing tractors shape beds, pull a waterwheel or hand-set slips, mow vines
  and pull the shared one-row digger set deeper; pickup is by hand (roots bruise, so hand pickup
  into padded crates is the norm even on large farms). Curing needs a heated, humid bay; storage
  is warm (55–60°F), so it cannot share the potato cooler's air.
- **Storable** 6–10 months after curing; the only crop here that sells from October to the
  following summer, and the one Wichita grocers and chefs can get locally from no one in winter.
- **Labor** ~200–340 hr/ac by channel (section 5); transplanting and pickup are the peaks.
- **Trust.** No structure or water change at stage 1 on existing irrigated ground.

|---|---|---|---|
| Orange, standard | all | Covington (NC standard, uniform, stores well), Beauregard (early, Fusarium and Rhizopus resistant, Missouri/Oklahoma standard), Orleans | certified slips: Sprout Mountain conventional ~$140–165/1,000 plus freight; NC farm-supply cases ~$95/1,000 pickup only; LSU station $450/1,000 (small lots) |
| Specialty for chefs | chef, market | Murasaki (purple skin, white flesh; lower yield), Bonita (white), Japanese types, O'Henry | $165–250/1,000; limited supply, order by February |
| Petite / "fingerling" sweets | chef | any variety at 8–10 in in-row spacing and early dig | same slips, closer spacing |
- Slip count: 12 in in-row on 36–40 in rows ≈ 13,000–14,500 slips/ac; 10 in spacing ≈ 16,000.
  Base cost **$2,100/ac** (13,000 × $0.16 delivered); adverse $2,600; favorable $1,300 if the
  farm grows its own slips from 20 bu of saved seed roots in a hotbed or high tunnel (Missouri:
  ~20 bu seed roots per acre; K-State Olathe is studying slip production economics). Own slips
  save money but carry disease (scurf, Fusarium) if seed roots are not clean; buy certified
  slips in year 1.

## 3. Agronomy
- **Planting.** Raised beds 8–10 in high (drainage, easier digging), 36–40 in centers, black
  plastic or bare bed with drip tape. Set slips 3–4 in deep with at least 2 nodes buried, 10–12
  in apart, late May to mid-June when soil is ≥65°F; water in immediately. Waterwheel
  transplanter (rentable or used $2,000–4,000) sets 2 rows with 3 people; hand setting is ~40
  hr/ac. Cool, wet weeks after setting cause stand loss (Oklahoma note).
- **Fertility.** Low N: 40–60 lb N/ac total (excess N gives vines and no roots), P to test,
  K 100–150 lb K2O/ac on sandy soils; side-dress a little N at 3–4 weeks. Budget $200/ac.
- **Irrigation.** ~1 in/week in July–August, less in September to firm skins; stop 2 weeks before
  digging. Drip tape $400–500/ac.
- **Weeds.** Cultivate and hoe for the first 4–6 weeks; vines then shade the row. Plastic mulch
  removes most in-row weeding. Few herbicides are labelled; check the current Midwest Vegetable
  Production Guide.
- **Insects.** **Sweet potato weevil is not established in Kansas** (quarantine states are
  the Gulf and Southeast from Texas to the Carolinas; Kansas appears on no list found) — verify
  with the Kansas Department of Agriculture, and buy slips only from certified weevil-free
  sources (NC, Mississippi, Louisiana weevil-free programs). Kansas pests are wireworm and white
  grubs on ground out of sod (rotate 2 years from sod and from corn), flea beetles (skin
  scarring), cucumber beetle larvae, grasshoppers, and deer, which eat vines. Budget $80/ac.
- **Diseases.** Fusarium wilt and surface rot (resistant varieties, certified slips), scurf
  (black skin blotching; clean seed roots, rotation), Rhizopus soft rot in storage (gentle
  handling, prompt curing), black rot. Rotate 3 years.
- **Physiological.** Cracking from uneven water; jumbos and rough roots from high N or wide
  spacing; chilling injury (hard core) if roots sit below 50°F in the field or storage.
- **Harvest.** Mow or flail vines (or cut with a rolling coulter) the day before digging; dig
  when soil is dry and above 55°F, before frost or within a day after a light frost (frost on
  vines does not hurt roots unless the chill reaches them). One-row digger set 8–10 in deep at
  ~0.3 ac/hr, 3–4 people picking into padded 20 lb crates or bins; grade in the field into No.
  1 (2–3.5 in dia, 3–9 in long), jumbo (chefs and market) and canner/petite (chefs). Never
  drop more than 6 in; skins are tender until cured.
- **Curing.** 80–90°F and 85–90% RH for 5–7 days (WSU; K-State 85–90°F 1–2 weeks) heals wounds
  and converts starch to sugar; a small insulated room with a space heater and humidifier
  works at this scale. Then ventilate down to storage conditions.
- **Storage.** 55–60°F, 85–90% RH, dark, with air exchange: 6–10 months (Missouri, Iowa State).
  Below 50–55°F causes chilling injury and off-flavor, so sweet potatoes cannot sit in the
  potato cooler; see section 6. Do not wash until the week of sale; wash, dry, grade, pack in
  40 lb cartons (grocery) or 10–20 lb cartons (chef).

## 9. Profitability verdict
- **Best channel: chef-direct**, with the Thanksgiving grocery window as the volume outlet for
  No. 1s and the market for bags. Grocery alone loses money below ~5 acres; market alone is
  capped by winter market days.
- **Stage-1 acres: 0.5 ac** (≈ 7,500 lb marketable, ~5,000 lb to chefs), after LOIs and a slip
  order placed by February; grow Covington or Beauregard plus one specialty (Murasaki) for chefs.
- **Family income at FAMILY rate: ~$6,500/ac, ~$3,250 for 0.5 ac** (~$1,240 wages on ~113 hr
  plus ~$2,000 profit); ~$2,500/ac net if labor is hired.
- What changes the verdict: a confirmed curing/warm-storage zone (without it the crop is not
  storable and must sell in October at commodity price → disqualified); chef price below ~$1.10
  → grocery-only is negative, stop; proven 400 bu yields on the sandy field → go to 1–2 acres and
  buy the waterwheel transplanter; sweet potato weevil found in Kansas → stop and destroy stock.

## 10. Risks and disqualifiers
| Risk | Likelihood | Effect | Control / disqualifier |
|---|---|---|---|
| No warm (55–60°F) humid curing and storage space | medium | roots chill and rot; forced October dump at $0.50/lb | build the two-zone room before planting; **disqualifier** if not built |
| Cool, wet June after transplanting | medium | stand loss, replant cost $1,000+ | wait for 65°F soil; order 10% extra slips |
| Dig window collides with fall grain harvest and wheat planting | high | roots left past frost | dig in the last two weeks of September before sorghum; vines mowed ahead; 0.5 ac is two half-days |
| Wireworm/grub scarring on ground out of sod or corn | medium | grade-out 20–40% | rotate from sod 2 yr; avoid following corn; bait stations |
| Deer grazing vines | medium–high near the river | yield loss | fence or repellent; plant near the farmstead |
| Sweet potato weevil introduced on slips | low | quarantine, crop loss | certified weevil-free slips only; confirm Kansas status with KDA |
| Commodity price collapse in November (retail ads at $0.77/lb) | high | grocery channel negative | hold cured stock for Jan–Apr chef sales; never sell to a DC |
| Slip freight and minimums for a half acre | medium | +$300–500 | combine order with another grower or pick up in Missouri/Arkansas |
