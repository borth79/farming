# Melons (watermelon and cantaloupe/muskmelon)

> Research note: K-State, USDA AMS, UK CCD and Missouri hosts failed to resolve in this
> session; figures are from search summaries and flagged **verify** where the number was not
> shown directly. Kansas has a real commercial melon industry on the sandy Arkansas River
> corridor (DeVore Farms, Stafford County, ~200 acres, selling to Dillons' Hutchinson DC), but
> no Kansas enterprise budget was found, and no Wichita retail or market melon price was found.
> Confidence is the lowest of the three produce files written on this date.

## 1. Fit for this farm
- **Soil — the deciding question.** Watermelon "does best in deep, sandy soils" (K-State);
  cantaloupe likewise prefers warm, well-drained sandy loam. The farm's fields are
  unrecorded (farm-profile.md). If a field lies on the sandy Arkansas River corridor terraces
  south-west of Wichita, melons are a natural fit, as DeVore's Stafford County operation shows;
  on heavy upland clay loam they size poorly, mature late and rot in wet Augusts (small icebox
  types tolerate upland ground). **Rule: no melon acre until a soil series is confirmed as
  sandy loam or loamy sand with good drainage.**
- **Water — required.** Consistent fruit size needs 1–2 in/week in July; plastic mulch and
  drip are the standard system and raise yield 2–3× over bare ground (NC State: 2,000–5,000
  cantaloupes/acre bare vs 6,000–12,000 on plastic). This is the most irrigation-dependent of
  the three crops, so the water-right open item is a hard gate.
- **Equipment fit — fair.** Tractors pull a used plastic mulch layer and a water-wheel
  transplanter (both new purchases, shared with tomatoes and peppers later); the sprayer and
  cultivator work the row middles. **Harvest is entirely by hand** (mechanization file); a boom
  conveyor only helps above ~2 acres.
- **Season.** Transplant **May 10–25** after soil reaches 65–70 °F; vines run through June
  (little labor — compatible with wheat harvest); harvest **July 10–August 25** (cantaloupe
  earlier and over 3–4 weeks of every-other-day picking; watermelon in 2–3 pickings). Harvest
  overlaps sweet corn picking and the hottest weeks; it does not touch grain work.
- **Perishable.** Cantaloupe 5–14 days at 36–41 °F; watermelon 2–3 weeks at 50–60 °F. The
  shared cold room is needed for cantaloupe; watermelon can wait in shade a week.
- **Labor — the binding constraint.** 200–230 hours/acre, 60% of it in a 6-week harvest and
  grading window; the mechanization file's rule 4 applies: melons enter a wholesale price sheet
  only when a person is dedicated to the enterprise.
- **Food safety — the reputational constraint.** Cantaloupe's netted rind carries pathogens
  that no wash removes; the 2011 Jensen Farms Listeria outbreak (33 deaths) and the 2023 and
  2026 Salmonella outbreaks keep cantaloupe under more FDA and buyer scrutiny than any other
  crop in this plan. At stage 1 the farm is below the FSMA covered-farm threshold (business-
  plan.md), but grocers will demand GAP for cantaloupe regardless, and the trust's liability
  exposure is real. Watermelon is lower risk (smooth rind, usually sold whole).
- **Trust fit.** No structure or lease; a water-use change would trip the trust checklist.

## 9. Profitability verdict
- **Best channel per acre:** chef-direct ($12,960 net/acre at FAMILY, $74/hour) on specialty
  cantaloupe, Charentais/Galia and mini seedless — but capped at ~0.2–0.5 acre by account count.
  **Best scalable channel:** farmers market ($6,400/acre, $39/hour, family income ~$8,900/acre)
  for about 1 acre. Wholesale fails the $25/hour rule and is not a stage-1 channel.
- **Stage-1 recommendation: 0.5 acre** (0.3 acre seedless and mini watermelon with pollenizers,
  0.2 acre cantaloupe and one specialty melon) on plastic and drip, **only if** (a) the plot is
  confirmed sandy loam with legal irrigation and (b) one person is dedicated to produce in
  July–August (sweet corn and melons harvest in the same weeks). Sell the top grade to 3–5 chef
  accounts and the rest by the piece at both markets. Expected family income at FAMILY rate
  ≈ $5,000–6,000 for ~115 hours. Treat year 1 as the Kansas yield trial the project lacks.
- **What changes the verdict:** (up) a sandy field near Wichita with a confirmed water right and
  a second committed person make 1–2 market acres plus a used boom conveyor reasonable; a
  GAP audit (cost-shared) opens independents and later Dillons store-level buys. (down) heavy
  soils, no drip, a cantaloupe food-safety incident anywhere in the region (demand collapses
  for a season), or market prices under $5 per watermelon / $3 per cantaloupe push the market
  channel under the $25/hour floor; then drop cantaloupe first and keep only mini seedless
  watermelon for market, or exit.

## 10. Risks and disqualifiers
| Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation / disqualifier |
|---|---|---|---|
| Heavy or poorly drained soil on the available plot | unknown | high | **Disqualifier** until a sandy loam field is identified |

| Poor pollination of seedless (hollow heart, low set) | medium | medium | 1:3 pollenizers, 2 hives/acre, no bloom-time sprays |
