# Phosphorus and potassium fertilizer

## Summary
P and K are less time-critical than nitrogen because soil reserves buffer the crop;
this makes them the most flexible cost line. Prices are driven by mining concentration
and trade policy rather than energy. The management lever is soil-test based build,
maintain, or drawdown strategy, timed to market lows.

## Products
| Product | Analysis | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| MAP | 11-52-0 | common P source on the Plains |
| DAP | 18-46-0 | alternative P source; some N |
| Potash (MOP) | 0-0-60 | K source; Kansas soils often adequate, test first |
| Starter blends | various | in-furrow or 2x2 for corn/sorghum |

## Price drivers
1. Phosphate: Morocco (OCP), China export restrictions, Russia, US (Mosaic);
   phosphate rock and sulfur/ammonia input costs; countervailing duties on imports.
2. Potash: Canada (Saskatchewan), Russia, Belarus; sanctions on Belarus/Russia;
   contract prices with China and India set the global tone.
3. Freight and Gulf import logistics.
4. Grain prices and farmer affordability (demand destruction at high prices).

## Management levers
- Soil test every 2–3 years per field; use K-State build/maintain/drawdown framework.
- When prices are high and soil tests are above critical levels, draw down reserves
  and defer purchases; when low, build.
- Variable-rate application by soil-test zone where field variability justifies it.
- Removal-based maintenance: lbs P2O5 and K2O removed per bushel per crop (table to be
  added from K-State values).

## Decision rules (draft)
- Defer P and K purchases when the price index is in the top third of its 5-year
  range and soil tests permit; buy ahead when in the bottom third.
- Never skip starter P on corn/sorghum on low-testing or cold soils regardless of price.
